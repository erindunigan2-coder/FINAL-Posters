# Poster #43 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Zinc (Acid) -- Pre-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 4 of the acid chloride zinc process. This rinse removes activation acid before parts enter the zinc bath. For acid zinc, the consequences of acid drag-in are specific and severe: excess HCl lowers bath pH below 4.8, disrupts brightener performance, and can cause boric acid to precipitate out of solution. Tighter conductivity target (< 200 uS/cm) than the pre-activation rinse.

Hero visual: a single flowing rinse tank with conductivity monitoring emphasis and a "what acid drag-in does to your bath" consequences callout.

### ZONE 5 -- Problems

**Section label:** `WHAT GOES WRONG AT THE PRE-PLATE RINSE` -- Y: 21.2".

| Problem | Symptom Downstream | Root Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acid carry-over | pH drop in zinc bath; rough deposit | Rinse too short or single stage | Add counterflow stage; extend time |
| Boric acid crash | White crystals in zinc bath; roughness | Acid drag-in dropped pH below 4.8 | Raise pH; filter; Hull cell |
| Parts dry between rinse and plate | Flash oxide; poor zinc adhesion | Transfer time too long | Reduce transfer time; keep parts wet |
| High conductivity | Accumulating acid in rinse | Water flow too low or exhausted | Increase flow; dump and refill |

Problem: `#E05C5C`. Symptom: `#E8A020`. Cause: `#F0EDE8`. Fix: `#27AE60`.

## Design Notes

The key differentiator from Poster #35 (Pre-Plate Rinse -- Alkaline) is the boric acid precipitation cascade. In alkaline zinc, acid drag-in changes the NaOH:Zn ratio. In acid zinc, acid drag-in crashes boric acid out of solution -- a much more dramatic and visually obvious failure mode (white crystals forming in the bath). The consequences callout should make this visceral.

The tighter conductivity target (< 200 uS/cm vs. < 500 uS/cm for the pre-activation rinse) reflects the acid zinc bath's sensitivity to pH disruption.
