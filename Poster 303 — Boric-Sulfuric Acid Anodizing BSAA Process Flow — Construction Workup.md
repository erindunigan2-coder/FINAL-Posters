# Poster #303 -- Construction Workup
## Boric-Sulfuric Acid Anodizing (BSAA) -- Process Flow

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

This is the cluster overview poster for BSAA -- the chromate-free replacement for Type I chromic acid anodizing. It shows the complete 8-stage process at a glance. BSAA uses a dilute sulfuric acid + boric acid electrolyte to produce a thin oxide functionally equivalent to Type I but without any hexavalent chromium. Developed by Boeing (BAC 5632), now NADCAP-approved and rapidly gaining aerospace adoption as Cr(VI) regulations tighten worldwide. The concept hook: "Same thin oxide. Same fatigue retention. Same paint adhesion. Zero hexavalent chromium."

## Design Notes

The BSAA process flow poster is structurally identical to the Type I process flow (#295) but with a fundamentally different message: this is the future, not the past. The Emerald green accent on the anodize stage (instead of Coral safety warnings on the Type I poster) signals "go" instead of "caution." The side-by-side comparison is the hero concept -- same performance, zero regulatory burden. The regulatory strip at the bottom of Zone 4 drives the business case home in four bullet points. The biggest content difference from Type I: no Cr(VI) safety warnings anywhere on the poster. That absence IS the message.
