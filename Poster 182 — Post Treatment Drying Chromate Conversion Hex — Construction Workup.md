# Poster #182 -- Construction Workup
## Post Treatment / Drying -- Chromate Conversion (Hex)

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 7 of 7 -- the final poster in the CC-04 cluster. This poster covers air drying (NO oven above 150 F), the 24-hour cure, the paint application window (72 hours per most aerospace specs), and -- critically -- the full regulatory context: Cr(VI) is a known human carcinogen subject to OSHA PEL, REACH authorization, and RoHS prohibition with aerospace exemptions. MIL-DTL-5541 Type II (trivalent) is the designated replacement, but Type I remains specified for legacy programs.

This poster carries the heaviest regulatory content in the entire poster library. It must be factual, not alarmist -- but the safety and regulatory information must be prominent and unmissable.

## Design Notes

This is the capstone poster of the CC-04 cluster and carries the heaviest regulatory content of any poster in the entire library. The regulatory panel in Zone 5 must be factual, comprehensive, and impossible to miss -- but not alarmist. The tone is "this is the reality, these are the rules, these are the reasons." The cure timeline visual in Zone 4 is operationally valuable -- every operator needs to know the 24-hour cure, 72-hour paint window, and aging consequences.

The air-dry-only rule mirrors the cold-rinse-only rule from Poster #181. Together, these two posters form a thermal protection bracket around the fresh chromate coating: no heat at rinse, no heat at dry. The temperature limit callout (140 F thermal stability limit) connects the two posters logically.

The regulatory panel addresses the RoHS/REACH flag that Watson identified for Poster 26 in the original series planning. The content is technically grounded: Type II is the replacement, but Type I persists because no trivalent coating can self-heal. That single scientific fact -- the absence of a mobile Cr6+ reservoir in trivalent coatings -- is the entire reason this regulatory tension exists.
