The Acid Copper Sulfate Plating Tank
Near 100% cathode efficiency — almost every amp-hour deposits copper. The most efficient plating bath in common use.

Bath Chemistry — The Four Essentials

All four components must be in balance — the brightener system and Cl⁻ interact directly
Copper Sulfate
180–250 g/L CuSO₄·5H₂O
  • Cu metal: 45–65 g/L
  • Source: blue CuSO₄·5H₂O crystals
  • Low Cu → burning at HCD
  • High Cu → crystallization risk
Sulfuric Acid
45–100 g/L H₂SO₄
  • Role: conductivity + Cu solubility
  • High acid → better throwing power
  • Low acid → higher brightness
  • Acid:Cu ratio is the key balance
Chloride (Cl⁻)
30–80 ppm (target: 40–60 ppm)
  • Source: HCl addition (small amounts)
  • Interacts with carrier for leveling
  • Low Cl⁻ → hazy, milky, no leveling
  • High Cl⁻ (>100 ppm) → dull, streaky
Brightener System
Per supplier TDS
  • Carrier (suppressor): PEG-type polymer
  • Brightener (accel): SPS-type sulfide
  • Leveler: N-containing organic
  • All three + Cl⁻ must balance

What Goes Wrong — 6 Common Defects

Identify symptom → trace cause → apply fix
Hazy / Milky
CauseLow Cl⁻, organic contamination, low brightener
FixCheck Cl⁻ by AgNO₃ titration; carbon treat bath
Dull Deposit
CauseExcess Cl⁻ (>100 ppm), organic byproduct buildup
FixDilute bath or H₂O₂ treatment; cut HCl additions
Roughness
CauseAnode sludge from wrong anodes or missing anode bags
FixInstall PP anode bags; increase filtration rate
Pitting
CauseOil contamination, low carrier, poor air agitation
FixCarbon treat; add carrier per TDS; increase air agitation
Burning (HCD)
CauseCD too high, low Cu metal, or high acid:Cu ratio
FixReduce CD; add CuSO₄; check acid:Cu balance
Skip Plating
CauseNo Cu strike on steel, immersion deposit, silicate film
FixApply alkaline Cu strike first; check cleaner for silicates

Anodes — Phosphorized Copper
Mandatory for acid copper sulfate — phosphorized only, no exceptions
TypePhosphorized copper, 0.04–0.06% P content
FormBalls or nuggets in titanium baskets
A:C Ratio1:1 to 2:1 (anode to cathode area)
Anode BagsPolypropylene (PP), 1–2 micron rating
Why Phosphorized?
Phosphorus forms a black film (copper phosphide) on the anode surface during operation. This film prevents Cu⁺ ion formation. Pure copper anodes produce Cu⁺ which disproportionates into metallic copper particles — causing roughness, filter clogging, and deposit contamination throughout the bath.
NEVER use pure copper anodes in acid copper sulfate.
Phosphorized only — 0.04–0.06% P.
