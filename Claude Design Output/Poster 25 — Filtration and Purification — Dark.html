FILTRATION AND PURIFICATION
Keeping Your Bath Clean — The Most Underrated Quality Tool in the Shop
If you can see particles in your solution, your filter stopped working two weeks ago.
Filter Types — Know Your Options
Cartridge Filter
1–50 μm / Depth or pleated
Low–moderate flow
The workhorse. Disposable or cleanable. Depth cartridges trap throughout media; pleated for higher flow. Most common for individual tank filtration.
Best for: Individual tank circulation loops
Bag Filter
1–200 μm / Surface capture
High volume
Lower cost per change. Higher flow capacity. Less efficient at fine filtration but excellent for heavy-solids baths. Easy to change.
Best for: High-flow, heavy-particle applications
Carbon Canister
Granular activated carbon
Organic removal / Continuous or batch
Removes organic contamination — brightener breakdown products, oil, surfactant residue. Continuous GAC bed or batch powdered carbon added directly.
Best for: Organic contamination control (nickel, copper)
Pump & Filter Unit
Centrifugal (mag-drive) or air-operated
Cartridge or bag / Sized to tank volume
Complete package — pump, housing, filter media integrated. Mag-drive standard for corrosive solutions. Air-operated for viscous or heated solutions.
Best for: Dedicated tank filtration systems
1 μm = fine Ni/Cr filtration
5 μm = standard plating filtration
10–25 μm = acid copper / barrel
>25 μm = coarse pre-filtration only
How Much Filtration Is Enough?
Process Min Turnovers/hr Target Filter Rating Notes
Watts nickel (bright) 3 5 1–5 μm Continuous; carbon canister recommended
Hard chrome 2 3 5–10 μm Trivalent chrome higher — consult supplier

Pump flow rate (GPM) = Tank volume (gal) × Turnovers/hr ÷ 60
Carbon Treatment — The Organic Detox
1
Analyze
Test Hull cell for organic symptoms: hazy deposits, skip plate, reduced ductility.
2
Adjust pH
Lower pH to 3.0–3.5 with dilute sulfuric acid (nickel) — promotes iron co-precipitation and maximizes carbon adsorption efficiency.
3
Add Carbon
Add 2–5 g/L powdered activated carbon. Stir 2–4 hours minimum for complete adsorption.
4
Filter
Filter through 1–5 μm media to remove ALL carbon particles. No exceptions.
5
Re-Adjust
Raise pH to operating range with nickel carbonate. Replenish ALL organic additives — brighteners, carrier, and wetting agent.
6
Verify
Run Hull cell to confirm organic removal. Compare before/after panels side by side.
DO
  • Test Hull cell BEFORE and AFTER treatment
  • Replenish all organic additives post-treatment
  • Use food-grade or reagent-grade carbon
  • Filter until solution is completely clear
DON'T
  • Return bath to service without removing ALL carbon by filtration
  • Skip post-treatment pH adjustment
  • Use carbon treatment as a substitute for metallic contamination removal
Dummy Plating
Current density:
2–5 ASF
Cathode:
Corrugated mild steel
Duration:
4–24 hours
Removes:
Cu, Pb, Cd, contaminant metals
Removes metallic contaminants by depositing them on a sacrificial cathode at low current density. The corrugated cathode maximizes surface area for efficient removal.
Run at LCD — you want the contaminants, not good metal.
Advanced Purification Methods
Permanganate Oxidizes organics that carbon cannot remove. Requires careful dosing — excess must be reduced before plating.
Hydrogen peroxide Fe2+ to Fe3+ for precipitation. Caution — excess decomposes nickel brighteners rapidly.
Electrodialysis Capital-intensive. Reserved for high-value baths (gold, palladium) where solution replacement cost justifies the equipment.
Freezing Alkaline zinc carbonate removal. Cool to crystallize sodium carbonate, then filter out precipitate.
Maintenance Schedule
Task Frequency Notes
Check filter pressure differential Daily Replace when ΔP exceeds manufacturer spec
Inspect filter housing seals Weekly Bypass leaks defeat the filter entirely
Change cartridge / bag filters As needed Run on pressure differential, NOT calendar
Carbon canister media replacement Monthly / Hull cell Replace when Hull cell shows organic symptoms
Clean pump strainer / inlet screen Weekly Clogged strainer starves the pump
Full system inspection Quarterly Pump seals, hose connections, housing cracks
Your Filter Is Failing When…
  • Rough deposits appear that weren't there last week
  • Filter pressure gauge reads zero (bypass or clogged)
  • Solution clarity decreases visibly
  • Hull cell panel shows pitting or roughness
  • Flow rate from return line drops noticeably
  • You can't remember the last filter change
The cheapest filter change you'll ever do is the one you do on time.
