Standard Thickness Target — Verify with XRF
Standard busbar specification requires 8–15 µm (300–600 µin) silver thickness. High-reliability applications may require 15–25 µm (600–1000 µin). At typical plating rate of 0.8–1.2 µm/min at 1.5 A/dm², standard thickness requires approximately 7–19 minutes. Verify thickness with XRF measurement on every rack or lot.

Current Distribution on Busbar Geometry
Busbars with bolt holes, edges, and corners create high current density (HCD) areas that attract excess silver, while recessed areas plate thin. Use robber (thief) bars to draw current away from edges and even out distribution. Current shields may be needed for complex geometries. The silver-to-free-cyanide ratio (typical 1:1.5 to 1:3, metal to free KCN by weight) is the primary control for deposit quality — deviations cause dull, rough, or burned deposits.

Silver Anode Management

Unlike the strike bath (inert anodes), the plate bath uses pure silver (99.99%) soluble anodes in polypropylene bags. Anodes dissolve to replenish silver consumed by plating. Monitor anode bags for tears — silver particles in solution cause rough deposits. Maintain anode area at 1.5–2× cathode area for even dissolution.

99.99% Ag anodes in PP bags. Check bags daily for damage.

Thickness Verification

Silver thickness is the most critical specification on the finished busbar. Measure with XRF per ASTM B568 at multiple points on every rack or lot. Check high-current-density areas (edges, near bolt holes) and low-current-density areas (recesses, centers of flat runs). Both must be within specification to pass.

XRF per ASTM B568 — every rack or lot. Min 3 readings per part.

Troubleshooting Silver plate issues

Burnt / Rough at Edges

Current density too high at HCD areas. Reduce overall CD. Add robber/thief bars. Add edge shields. Verify anode-to-cathode distance is adequate for even distribution.

Thin in Bolt Holes / Recesses

Low current density in recessed areas. Increase metal concentration. Add auxiliary (conforming) anodes. Increase agitation in recessed areas.

White Haze / Powdery Deposit

Free KCN too high or too low, or silver metal out of range. Analyze for Ag metal and free KCN. Target metal:free KCN ratio 1:1.5 to 1:3. Carbon treat for organic contamination.

Rapid Tarnishing After Plate

Residual cyanide from poor rinse (Step 17) or sulfur in packaging/atmosphere. Improve final rinse quality. Use sulfur-free packaging. Apply anti-tarnish if customer spec allows.

⚠ Safety
CYANIDE BATH — KCN is lethal by ingestion, inhalation, and skin absorption. This is the fourth cyanide bath on this line. NEVER mix cyanide solutions with acid — this generates deadly hydrogen cyanide (HCN) gas. Maintain bath pH >10 at all times. Operate under slot ventilation at minimum 100 fpm face velocity. HCN monitors must be installed and calibrated. Cyanide antidote kit must be accessible within 30 seconds. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, face shield, and apron.
