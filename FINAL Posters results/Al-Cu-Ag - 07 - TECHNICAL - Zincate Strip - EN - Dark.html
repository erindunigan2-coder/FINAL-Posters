Why Strip and Re-Apply?
The first zincate (Step 5) conditions the aluminum surface but produces a relatively coarse zinc crystal structure. Stripping this first layer and re-applying (Step 9) produces a dramatically thinner, finer-grained, more uniform zinc deposit. This double-zincate process is mandatory for reliable adhesion in critical applications. Single zincate adhesion is inconsistent — the second zincate is what gives the process its reliability. The first zincate’s real job was to prepare the aluminum surface for the second application.

Correct Endpoint

All zinc dissolved. Bare aluminum visible with a bright, clean appearance across all surfaces. No gray patches, no dark residue, no powdery remnants. The aluminum should look similar to how it appeared after the acid etch (Step 3) — uniformly bright.

Transfer immediately to DI rinse (Step 8). Freshly stripped aluminum is as reactive as freshly etched — oxide begins forming immediately.

Incomplete Strip — Do Not Proceed

Any remaining gray patches indicate residual zinc. These patches will cause uneven deposition in the second zincate (Step 9), resulting in localized thick and thin zones. Adhesion failures in the copper and silver layers above will trace back to this step. Re-immerse in the nitric acid until all zinc is fully dissolved.

If zinc persists after 30 seconds, check acid strength — may be exhausted or diluted by excess dragout.

Troubleshooting Zincate strip issues

Zinc Not Dissolving

Acid concentration too low (diluted by repeated use or excessive dragout). Check acid strength. Replace or fortify with fresh 50% HNO₃. Also verify that the coating is actually zinc — dark deposits may indicate contaminated zincate bath.

Gray Patches Remaining

Incomplete strip. Re-immerse for additional 5–10 seconds. If patches persist, the zincate bath may have deposited an abnormally thick or alloyed layer — investigate Step 5 parameters.

Aluminum Surface Pitting

Over-immersion in nitric acid or acid temperature too high. Limit immersion to 15 seconds maximum. Verify ambient temperature. Pitting creates nucleation defects that propagate through all subsequent layers.

Second Zincate Non-Uniform (Step 9)

Root cause often traces to incomplete strip here. Verify visual endpoint: no gray patches. Also check that DI rinse (Step 8) is adequate — acid carryover into the alkaline zincate bath degrades performance.

⚠ Safety
Nitric acid (50% v/v) is a strong oxidizer and is corrosive to skin, eyes, and respiratory tract. Produces toxic brown NOx fumes, especially when dissolving metals. Wear face shield, chemical-resistant gloves, apron, and rubber boots. Work under slot ventilation. Do not store or use near organic solvents, oils, or combustible materials — nitric acid is a powerful oxidizer that can cause fire or explosion on contact with organics. If acid contacts skin, flush immediately with water for 15 minutes.
