## Phase 6 -- Alkaline Vulnerability + Common Failures

Y: 20.5" to 26.5".

**Left -- Why This Rinse Is Critical (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
Title: `ALKALINE Zn-Ni VULNERABILITY` `#E8A020`.
- Bath operates at NaOH 100--150 g/L (pH > 13)
- Acid drag-in immediately neutralized -- consuming NaOH, dropping local pH
- Zn:Ni alloy ratio is pH-sensitive -- even 0.5 pH-unit drop shifts Ni%
- Acid Zn-Ni (pH 5.5--6.5) more tolerant but still affected

**Right -- Common Failures (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**
Title: `WHAT GOES WRONG`.

| Failure | Root Cause | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Single rinse instead of double | Cost-cutting or line layout | Acid drag-in exceeds buffering |
| Rinse water not flowing | Valve closed, flow meter broken | Rinse becomes contaminated sump |
| Parts sit in air | Slow transfer, rack backup | Flash oxide; adhesion loss |
| Drag-out not recovered | No drag-out tank | Chemistry lost to drain; cost increase |

## Phase 7 -- Drag-Out Economics + Safety

Y: 26.5" to 32.5".

**Left -- Drag-Out Recovery (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**
Title: `DRAG-OUT RECOVERY -- THE BUSINESS CASE` `#27AE60`.
- Zn-Ni baths are expensive -- proprietary complexants, NaOH, nickel salts
- Triple rinse recommended: drag-out recovery + double counterflow
- Recovery tank captures concentrated Zn-Ni solution
- Return to plating bath periodically
- ROI: pays for itself within months on production barrel line

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**
Fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 1pt `#E05C5C`. Title: `SAFETY`.
- Rinse water may contain residual HCl -- handle as acidic
- Zn-Ni drag-out contains nickel compounds (dermal sensitizer; IARC Group 2B)
- Gloves (neoprene), goggles, apron required
- Drain to waste treatment -- never sanitary sewer
