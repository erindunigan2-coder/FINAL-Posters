## Phase 5 -- Acid Drag-In Impact

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `WHAT ACID DRAG-IN DOES TO YOUR NiCo BATH`.

Three callout boxes:

**pH DROP** (accent `#E05C5C`): HCl drops bath pH | Target 3.5--4.5 | Below 3.5: poor throwing power, HCD burning | Above 4.5: hydroxide precipitation, roughness | Prevention > correction.

**CHLORIDE BUILDUP** (accent `#E8A020`): HCl introduces free Cl- | Small NiCl2 is normal (5--15 g/L) | Excess from drag-in increases anode corrosion, shifts alloy composition | Not easily removed -- prevention is only practical control.

**PITTING** (accent `#E05C5C`): Low pH + excess Cl- = gas evolution + pin-hole pits | NiCo aerospace parts cannot tolerate pitting | One bad rinse can contaminate a bath for days.

## Phase 6 -- Transfer Timing

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `TRANSFER TIMING -- HOW FAST IS FAST ENOUGH?`. Two-column layout.

**Left -- Steel / Standard Acid Dip** (accent `#2EC4B6`):
30--60 sec immersion | No strict time limit | Parts can sit briefly if still wet | Do not allow air-dry | Comfortable pace, focus on thoroughness.

**Right -- Superalloy / Wood's Strike** (accent `#E8A020`):
15--30 sec MAX rinse | Transfer to NiCo within 60 sec of exiting rinse | Thin Ni strike re-passivates in air | Some shops transfer LIVE (current on) | `Speed is everything -- have the plating bath ready before you start the strike` `#E05C5C`.

**Verdict banner:** `The Wood's strike exists because these alloys fight adhesion. Do not give them time to win.` `#E8A020`

## Phase 7 -- Common Rinse Failures

Y: 26.5" to 32.5". Section: `WHAT GOES WRONG`. Four cards:

| # | Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BATH pH DRIFT | Acid carry-over from insufficient rinsing | Increase flow; monitor pH every shift |
| 2 | PITTING IN NiCo DEPOSIT | Chloride contamination from drag-in | Improve rinse; consider double rinse |
| 3 | ADHESION LOSS (SUPERALLOYS) | Strike passivated during extended rinse hold | Reduce transfer time; consider live transfer |
| 4 | ROUGH DEPOSIT | Particulate from dirty rinse water | Filter rinse tank; use DI water |
