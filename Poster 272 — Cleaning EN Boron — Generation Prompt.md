## Phase 5 -- Cleaning Methods Comparison

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `THREE CLEANING METHODS`.

Three columns (Soak / Cathodic Electroclean / Anodic Electroclean). Same structure as Poster #264.

**EN-B specific note below:** `EN-B wear applications often involve high-strength steel substrates. Hydrogen absorption during cathodic cleaning is a real risk -- prefer anodic cleaning or minimize cathodic time.` `#E8A020`.

## Phase 6 -- Substrate-Specific Notes

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `SUBSTRATE CONSIDERATIONS`.

2x2 grid:

| Position | Substrate | Accent | Key Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1C1 | Steel / Iron | `#2EC4B6` | Standard clean; steel is catalytic for EN-B; HCl/H2SO4 activation follows; less sensitive to marginal activation |
| R1C2 | Aluminum | `#E8A020` | Non-etch alkaline (pH <10.5); double zincate follows; rinse thoroughly before zincate |
| R2C1 | Stainless Steel | `#27AE60` | Standard clean; Wood's Ni strike follows; passive layer must be removed |
| R2C2 | Plastics (ABS, PC) | `#E05C5C` | Proprietary conditioner; Sn/Pd colloidal follows; EN-B on plastics uncommon but used for wear housings |
