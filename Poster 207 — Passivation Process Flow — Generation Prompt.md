## Phase 4 -- Process Flow Hero

Y: 2.9" to 15.5". Section: `THE COMPLETE PROCESS -- STAGE BY STAGE`.

### Step 5 -- Eight-stage U-flow diagram

**Top Row:** 1. Alkaline Clean (`#2EC4B6`) / 2. Rinse Post-Clean (`#2EC4B6`) / 3. Free Iron Sources (`#E8A020` -- educational) / 4. Water Quality (`#2EC4B6`)

**Bottom Row:** 5. Passivation Bath (`#27AE60`) / 6. Rinse Post-Acid (`#2EC4B6`) / 7. Verification (`#E8A020`) / 8. Drying (`#E8A020`)

**Inside each box:**

**Box 1 -- Alkaline Clean:** `4--8 oz/gal, 130--180 F, 5--15 min`. Purpose: Remove machining oils, cutting fluids. Check: `NO CHLORIDE in cleaner -- chloride pits stainless` `#E05C5C`

**Box 2 -- Rinse (Post-Clean):** `Ambient, overflow/spray`. Purpose: Remove cleaner residues. Check: `DI water for aerospace/medical parts`

**Box 3 -- Free Iron Sources:** Educational stage. Purpose: `Free iron on surface = the reason we passivate`. Check: `Sources: machining tools, grinding, carbon steel contact, shop air`

**Box 4 -- Water Quality:** `DI or RO preferred, < 500 uS/cm`. Purpose: `Clean, chloride-free water before acid bath`. Check: `Chloride in rinse water can pit stainless during passivation`

**Box 5 -- Passivation Bath:** `Nitric: 20--45% HNO3 / Citric: 4--20% C6H8O7` / `70--150 F, 4--30 min`. Purpose: `Remove free iron; restore Cr2O3 passive film`. Check: `Alloy family determines acid selection`

**Box 6 -- Rinse (Post-Acid):** `Ambient to warm, multiple stages for critical`. Purpose: `Remove all acid residues`. Check: `Trapped acid in crevices = #1 cause of post-passivation staining`

**Box 7 -- Verification:** `CuSO4 test (ASTM A380) / salt spray / humidity`. Purpose: `Confirm free iron removed`. Check: `Cu deposits = FAIL (free iron remains)`

**Box 8 -- Drying:** `Air dry or warm forced air, 120--150 F max`. Purpose: `Remove moisture; prevent crevice corrosion`. Check: `NO high-temp oven -- thermal oxidation defeats purpose` `#E05C5C`

## Phase 6 -- Nitric vs. Citric

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section: `NITRIC VS. CITRIC -- THE TWO PATHS`.

**Left -- NITRIC ACID (HNO3)** (accent `#E8A020`): `The Traditional Standard`. 20--45% vol / 70--150 F / 20--30 min. Oxidizing acid -- dissolves iron AND promotes Cr2O3. Excellent for 300-series, good for martensitic with dichromate. Long bath life. Safety: fuming, toxic NOx. ASTM A967 Types 1--4. Bottom: `Legacy standard. Still dominant in aerospace. Handles difficult alloys better.`

**Right -- CITRIC ACID (C6H8O7)** (accent `#27AE60`): `The Modern Alternative`. 4--20% wt / 70--160 F / 4--20 min. Chelates iron; relies on dissolved O2 for Cr2O3. Excellent for 300-series, may struggle with free-machining. Safe to handle, non-hazardous waste, biodegradable. ASTM A967 Types 5--8. Bottom: `Gaining share rapidly. Preferred for new installations. Safer and cheaper to operate.`

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting

Y: 28.5" to 32.5". Section: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON PROBLEMS`.

| # | X | Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5" | CuSO4 TEST FAILURE | Free iron remaining; insufficient time/conc | Increase time/conc/temp; replace bath |
| 2 | 6.33" | STAINING AFTER PASSIVATION | Trapped acid in crevices; chloride in rinse | Improve rinsing; use DI water; dry promptly |
| 3 | 12.16" | PITTING DURING PASSIVATION | Chloride contamination; bath temp too high | Use chloride-free reagents; reduce temp |
| 4 | 18.0" | ETCHING / MATTE FINISH | Acid too strong; temp too high; time too long | Reduce conc/temp/time; try citric for sensitive grades |
