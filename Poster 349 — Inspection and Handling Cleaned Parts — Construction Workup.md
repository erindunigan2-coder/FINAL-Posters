# Poster #349 -- Construction Workup
## Inspection & Handling -- Cleaned Parts

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Poster 7 of 7 in the CT-01 cluster. The final poster covers what happens after cleaning -- how to verify the surface is actually clean and how to handle parts without re-contaminating them. The hero visual is a water break test diagram showing pass and fail conditions. The handling rules are the kind of common-sense-that-is-not-common knowledge that prevents re-contamination between cleaning and the next process step.

This poster closes the loop on the alkaline cleaning cluster and sets up the handoff to whatever comes next (electroclean, acid activate, or plate).

### ZONE 4 -- ASTM F22 Procedure + Wipe Test

**Two-column layout (Y: 14.7" to 19.8")**

**Left -- ASTM F22 Water Break Test Procedure (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Rounded rect H: 4.5", fill `#1E2435`, left accent 0.06" `#27AE60`.

Title: `WATER BREAK TEST (ASTM F22)` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#27AE60`

Numbered steps, Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`:
```
1. Rinse part with clean water
2. Hold vertically and observe water film
3. Look for breaks, beading, or dry spots
4. Must be performed within 30 seconds of rinse
   (air-dry allows recontamination from atmosphere)
5. PASS: uniform water sheet, no breaks
6. FAIL: any break = residual contamination
```

Note: `Per ASTM F22 -- Standard Test Method for Hydrophobic Surface Films by the Water-Break Test` JetBrains Mono 11 pt `#F0EDE8` at 50%

**Right -- Wipe Test (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**

Rounded rect H: 4.5", fill `#1E2435`, left accent 0.06" `#E8A020`.

Title: `WIPE TEST (SUPPLEMENTAL)` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`

Body:
```
When to use:
  - Buffed parts (water break hard to interpret)
  - Dark substrates (hard to see water film)
  - Confirmation of water break result

Method:
  1. Wipe cleaned surface with clean white cloth
  2. Examine cloth for discoloration
  3. Any color = residual soil or smut
  4. Especially useful for buffed parts

Limitation:
  - Less sensitive than water break test
  - Does not detect monolayer contamination
```

### ZONE 5 -- Handling Rules

**Section label:** `HANDLING AFTER CLEANING -- THE RULES` -- Y: 20.2".

**BLOCK F -- Handling Rules Grid (Y: 20.8" to 24.8")**

Two rows of three cards.

| Card | Position | Icon Concept | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| NO BARE HANDS | R1C1 | hand-x | NEVER touch cleaned surfaces with bare hands. Fingerprint oils = instant re-contamination. Use clean cotton gloves or handle by edges/racks only. |
| PROCESS QUICKLY | R1C2 | clock | Move to next step within 15-30 minutes. Steel flash-rusts in air. Zinc die cast oxidizes rapidly. Every minute of delay is a risk. |
| CLEAN GLOVES ONLY | R1C3 | gloves | Cotton gloves must be clean and oil-free. Dirty gloves are worse than bare hands -- they spread contamination you cannot see. |
| KEEP PARTS WET | R2C1 | droplet | If delay is unavoidable, keep parts submerged in clean rinse water. Air exposure is the enemy of a clean surface. |
| NO STACKING | R2C2 | layers-x | Do not stack cleaned parts. Surface-to-surface contact traps moisture and creates corrosion points. |
| RACK INSPECTION | R2C3 | rack | Racks must be clean. Contaminated rack tips transfer soil to parts at every contact point. Strip and recoat racks on schedule. |

Each card: Rounded rect W: 7.33", H: 1.8", fill `#1E2435`, radius 6.
- Rule name: Barlow SemiBold 14 pt `#E8A020`
- Rule text: Inter Regular 12 pt `#F0EDE8`
- Cards with "NEVER" or "NO": left accent `#E05C5C`. Others: left accent `#2EC4B6`.

## Design Notes

This poster closes the CT-01 cluster. The water break test hero is the right visual because this single test is the most universal quality check in all of metal finishing. The PASS/FAIL side-by-side must be instantly readable -- an operator glancing at the poster should be able to compare what they see on their parts to what the poster shows. The flash rust warning section has real urgency -- this is where cleaned parts go wrong most often. The handling rules are common sense that nobody teaches new operators until they have already ruined a rack of parts.
