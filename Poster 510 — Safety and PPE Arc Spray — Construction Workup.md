# Poster #510 -- Construction Workup
## Safety & PPE -- Arc Spray

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Safety poster for the arc spray cluster. Arc spray's #1 safety challenge is fume generation -- it produces more fume per unit time than any other thermal spray process because of its extreme deposition rate. Zinc fume fever is the dominant hazard when spraying zinc (the most common arc spray feedstock). The electric arc adds UV, electrical, and spatter hazards. This poster is designed to hang at the entrance to every arc spray booth or field station.

**Section label:** `ARC SPRAY HAZARD IDENTIFICATION` -- Y: 4.4".

**BLOCK B -- 7-Row Hazard Table**

Y: 5.0" to 13.0". Full width.

Column widths: Hazard (3.5") | Details (6.5") | Controls (6.5") | Severity (2.0") | Standard (4.5")

| Hazard | Details | Controls | Severity | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noise | 95--115 dB at operator position | Double hearing protection above 100 dB (plugs + muffs) | HIGH | OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95 |
| Electric arc | 18--40 VDC open circuit; 100--400 A operating; arc flash risk | Welding-grade PPE; insulated gloves; proper grounding; GFCI protection | HIGH | NFPA 70E |
| UV/IR radiation | Arc produces significant UV -- comparable to MIG welding | Shade 8--12 welding lens; no exposed skin near arc | HIGH | ANSI Z87.1 |
| Zinc fume fever | Very high fume rate from zinc wire; flu-like symptoms 4--12 hr after exposure | Mandatory supplied-air RPE (PAPR or airline) for zinc; LEV essential | CRITICAL | ACGIH TLV 2 mg/m3 |
| Metal fumes (other) | Al, stainless, Cu -- all produce hazardous fumes at high rates | Fume extraction; RPE rated for specific metals | HIGH | OSHA PEL per metal |
| Electrical | DC power supply; cable damage on jobsites | Lockout/tagout; cable inspection; GFCI | MODERATE | OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333 |
| Fire / spatter | Molten metal droplets travel 2--5 meters from gun | Fire blankets; clear combustibles; fire watch; extinguisher within reach | HIGH | NFPA 51B |

### ZONE 5 -- Zinc Fume Fever Deep-Dive

**Section label:** `ZINC FUME FEVER -- THE #1 ARC SPRAY HEALTH RISK` -- Y: 22.2".

**BLOCK E -- Zinc Fume Fever Panel**

Y: 22.8" to 28.0". Three columns.

**Column 1 -- Symptoms (X: 0.5", W: 7.3"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#E05C5C`
- Title: `SYMPTOMS` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`
- `Onset: 4--12 hours after exposure`
- `Fever, chills, sweating`
- `Muscle aches and headache`
- `Nausea, fatigue, metallic taste`
- `Resembles flu -- often misdiagnosed`
- `Usually resolves in 24--48 hours`
- `Repeated exposure does NOT build immunity`

**Column 2 -- Prevention (X: 8.15", W: 7.3"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#27AE60`
- Title: `PREVENTION` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#27AE60`
- `Supplied-air RPE (PAPR or airline respirator)`
- `Local exhaust ventilation (LEV) with adequate CFM`
- `Enclosed spray booth with downdraft ventilation`
- `Monitor airborne zinc fume: TLV = 2 mg/m3 (ACGIH)`
- `Rotate operators on long zinc-spray shifts`
- `Pre-shift hydration and nutrition`

**Column 3 -- Response (X: 15.85", W: 7.3"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `IF SYMPTOMS OCCUR` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`
- `Remove from exposure immediately`
- `Move to fresh air`
- `Seek medical attention`
- `Report incident -- investigate ventilation failure`
- `Do not return to zinc spraying until symptom-free`
- `Review and upgrade RPE and ventilation before resuming`
