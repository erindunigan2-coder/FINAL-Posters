# Poster #48 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- Zinc-Nickel

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of the zinc-nickel process. Watson's research is blunt: "Cleanliness is even more critical than plain zinc. Any organic residue causes alloy composition shifts and passivate adhesion failures. Double cleaning (soak + electroclean) is mandatory, not optional." This poster carries that message hard. The hero visual emphasizes the mandatory double-clean sequence.

Cleaning parameters are functionally identical to acid zinc (Poster #40) -- same alkaline soak cleaner, same electrocleaner. The difference is intensity: double cleaning is not a recommendation, it is a requirement. Non-silicated cleaners are still mandatory (alkaline Zn-Ni baths are sensitive to silicate contamination for the same reasons as acid zinc).

|---|---|

| Polarity | Cathodic then anodic (anodic last) |

**Right -- Why Double Clean? (X: 15.0", W: 8.5"):**

Rounded rect fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`.
Title: `WHY DOUBLE CLEAN FOR Zn-Ni?` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`

Body (Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`):
- `Zinc-nickel alloy composition (12--16% Ni) is controlled by current density, temperature, and surface condition.`
- `Organic contamination on the surface changes the cathode film chemistry -- the nickel content shifts unpredictably.`
- `Low Ni% (< 12%): corrosion performance drops to plain zinc levels.`
- `High Ni% (> 16%): cracking and adhesion failures.`
- `Passivate adhesion on Zn-Ni is also more sensitive to surface contamination than on plain zinc.`
- `The electroclean step removes embedded contaminants that soak cleaning alone cannot reach.`

Key stat: `A 1% shift in Ni% can mean a 200-hour shift in salt spray performance.` JetBrains Mono 12 pt `#E05C5C`

### ZONE 5 -- Common Problems & Fixes

**Section label:** `WHAT GOES WRONG AT THE CLEANING STAGE` -- Y: 22.2".

| Problem | Symptom Downstream | Root Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ni% out of spec | Alloy composition inconsistent across parts | Organic residue on surface shifting cathode chemistry | Verify double-clean sequence; check cleaner strength |
| Passivate flaking | Chromate peels on tape test | Organic film between zinc-nickel and passivate | Add anodic electroclean step; extend time |
| Skip plating | Bare areas on zinc-nickel deposit | Silicate residue from cleaner | Switch to non-silicated cleaner |
| Poor adhesion | Zn-Ni peels from substrate | Under-cleaning, residual oxide | Anodic electroclean; verify water-break-free |
| Pitting | Small pits in Zn-Ni deposit | Oil micro-droplets carried to plate bath | Carbon treat Zn-Ni bath; improve cleaning |

## Design Notes

The tagline and the coral-bordered mandatory callout are the heart of this poster. Watson's brief couldn't be clearer: double cleaning is not optional for Zn-Ni. This poster should feel like it's giving an order, not a suggestion. The parameter data is identical to Poster #40, but the framing is entirely different -- this is about discipline and precision, not just cleanliness.
