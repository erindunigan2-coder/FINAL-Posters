## Phase 5 -- Why No Rinse Is Needed

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `WHY REVERSE ETCH ELIMINATES THIS STAGE`.

Full-width panel, accent `#27AE60`:

| Advantage | Explanation |
|---|---|
| NO TRANSFER | Part never leaves the bath -- zero re-contamination |
| NO RINSE CONTAMINATION | No acid drag-in to worry about |
| NO RE-OXIDATION | Surface stays immersed -- no air exposure |
| THERMAL CONTINUITY | Part stays at bath temp -- no thermal shock |

Bottom: `Reverse etch is the reason hard chrome lines are physically shorter than most plating lines. Fewer tanks. Fewer transfers. Fewer failure points.` -- `#E8A020`

## Phase 6 -- Exception Path Details

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `EXCEPTION PATH -- RINSE AFTER EXTERNAL ACID ETCH`.

| Scenario | Acid | Time | Rinse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy oxide from heat treatment | 20--50% HCl | 1--5 min | Single, ambient, 15--30 sec |
| Hardened tool steel (> 60 HRC) | 10--30% H2SO4 | 1--3 min | Single, ambient, 15--30 sec |
| Case-hardened surfaces | 20--30% HCl | 2--5 min | Single, ambient, 15--30 sec |
| Heavy corrosion/scale | 20--50% HCl | 3--5 min | Single, ambient, 30--60 sec |

Note: `After external acid etch, the part still gets reverse etched in the chrome bath (brief, 15--30 sec) before plating. The acid etch supplements; it does not replace.` -- `#2EC4B6`

---

## Phase 7 -- Acid Drag-In Risks

Y: 26.5" to 32.5". Section: `ACID DRAG-IN RISKS (EXTERNAL ETCH ONLY)`.

| Contaminant | Effect | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| HCl drag-in | Chloride attacks lead anodes; disrupts anode film | HIGH (`#E05C5C`) |
| H2SO4 drag-in | Increases sulfate -- shifts CrO3:SO4 ratio | MODERATE (`#E8A020`) |
| Iron salts | Adds to iron contamination from reverse etch | LOW (`#2EC4B6`) |

Key: `Chloride is the killer. If you use HCl for external acid etch, your rinse quality is critical.` -- `#E05C5C`
Recommendation: `Prefer H2SO4 over HCl when possible. Sulfate drag-in is easier to manage than chloride.` -- `#E8A020`
