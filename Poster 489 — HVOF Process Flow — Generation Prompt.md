## Phase 6 -- HVOF vs. Hard Chrome + Troubleshooting

### HVOF vs. Hard Chrome (Y: 22.0"-28.5")

Section label: `THE TRANSITION -- HVOF VS. HARD CHROME` centered, 28 pt.

Two side-by-side cards:

**Left -- HVOF WC-12Co** (accent `#E8A020`): Hardness 1100-1400 HV300 | Porosity < 1% (< 0.5%) | Bond > 70 MPa | Wear rate 1-5 x 10^-7 mm3/Nm | Fatigue: neutral to beneficial | Oxide < 0.5% | Max temp 500 degC | Cr(VI): ZERO. Bottom highlight: `HVOF WC-Co matches or exceeds hard chrome in every measurable property`.

**Right -- Hard Chrome** (accent `#E05C5C`): Hardness 800-1000 HV | Porosity < 1% (micro-cracked) | Bond 40-80 MPa | Wear rate 5-15 x 10^-7 mm3/Nm | Fatigue: detrimental (tensile + H2 embrittlement) | Max temp 400 degC | Cr(VI): YES -- NESHAP + REACH. Bottom highlight: `EPA NESHAP and EU REACH are driving the transition away from hexavalent chromium`.

### Troubleshooting Quick Hits (Y: 28.5"-32.5")

Section label: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON PROBLEMS` centered, 24 pt.

Four cards in a row, left accent `#E05C5C`:

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| DELAMINATION | Poor surface prep or residual chrome from strip | Verify SP-5; confirm no residual chrome |
| LOW HARDNESS | Decarburization (WC decomposing) | Reduce flame temp; shorten dwell time |
| SUBSTRATE OVERHEATING | Insufficient cooling between passes | Add compressed air jets; increase traverse |
| POROSITY > 1% | Standoff too far or powder too coarse | Reduce standoff; verify powder 5-45 um |
