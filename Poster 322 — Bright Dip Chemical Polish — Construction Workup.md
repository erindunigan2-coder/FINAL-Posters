# Poster #322 -- Construction Workup
## Bright Dip (Chemical Polish) -- Bright Anodizing

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 3 of 8 -- the defining stage of bright anodizing. This is where the mirror finish is created. A near-boiling mixture of phosphoric acid (85%) and nitric acid (5%) chemically polishes the aluminum surface by preferentially dissolving microscopic peaks faster than valleys, producing specular reflectivity.

This is the MOST DANGEROUS operation in any anodizing shop. Toxic brown NOx fumes evolve vigorously. The acid mix is at near-boiling temperature. Safety must dominate this poster. The hero visual is the bright dip tank with a prominent safety overlay.

| Parameter | Control Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| H3PO4 | ~85% by volume (85% reagent grade) | Primary bath component |
| HNO3 | ~5% by volume (70% reagent grade) | Oxidizing agent -- drives polishing |
| Ratio | 95 parts H3PO4 : 5 parts HNO3 (typical) | Varies by formulation |
| Temperature | 190--210 F (88--99 C) | NEAR BOILING -- critical |
| Time | 30--120 sec | Very short -- time-critical |
| Specific gravity | Monitor -- increases as Al dissolves | Track for bath life |
| Dissolved Al | < 40--50 g/L | Above this: polishing degrades |
| Copper limit | Monitor on Cu alloys | Enrichment causes staining |

Data: JetBrains Mono 12 pt `#F0EDE8`.

**Right -- Bath Life + Maintenance (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#2EC4B6` 0.06".
Title: `BATH MANAGEMENT` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#2EC4B6`

Body (Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 155%):

> -- Monitor specific gravity regularly (increases with dissolved aluminum)
> -- Replace HNO3 additions as nitric acid is consumed in the reaction
> -- Decant or replace bath when dissolved aluminum exceeds 40--50 g/L
> -- Copper buildup from Cu-bearing alloys degrades polishing quality
> -- Some shops use continuous metering pumps for HNO3 replenishment
> -- Bath temperature must be maintained within +/- 5 F of setpoint

## Design Notes

This is the most safety-intensive poster in the entire anodizing series. The Coral border on the tank diagram, the Coral stage badge, and the full-width safety panel all reinforce that the bright dip is uniquely dangerous. The polishing mechanism diagram (boundary layer theory) is the key educational visual -- it explains WHY chemical polishing produces a mirror finish. Time criticality (30--120 sec) must be visually prominent because over-polishing attacks grain boundaries and ruins the finish.
