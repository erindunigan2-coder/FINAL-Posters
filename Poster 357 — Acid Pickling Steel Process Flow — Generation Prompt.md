## Phase 5 -- HCl vs H2SO4 Comparison

Y: 14.5" to 21.0". Section label: `WHICH ACID? -- HCL VS. H2SO4`.

Two glass cards.

**Left -- HCl** (amber-accented): `The Fast Ambient Pickle`
- Ambient (68-95 F) / 15-30% v/v / Faster at room temp / HIGH fume -- requires exhaust / All scale types / ~200 g/L FeCl2 capacity / Rinses easily
- `Most common choice for job shops -- fast, ambient, no heating`

**Right -- H2SO4** (teal-accented): `The Hot Heavy-Scale Pickle`
- 120-175 F (requires heat) / 10-25% v/v / Slower at room temp / Low fume / Heavy thick scale / ~120 g/L FeSO4 capacity / FeSO4 less soluble
- `Lower acid cost; less fume -- preferred for high-volume continuous lines`

## Phase 6 -- Hydrogen Embrittlement Risk

Y: 21.0" to 27.5". Section label: `HYDROGEN EMBRITTLEMENT -- KNOW YOUR RISK`. Sublabel: `Acid + Iron = Hydrogen. Higher hardness = higher risk. ASTM B849 is non-negotiable.`

Glass table with color-coded risk levels:

| Hardness | Risk | Inhibitor | Bake | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 31 HRC | LOW (`#27AE60`) | Recommended | Not usually required | Standard pickling |
| 31-39 HRC | MODERATE (`#E8A020`) | Required | 375 F, 4-8 hrs after plating | Within 4 hrs of exposure |
| 40-50 HRC | HIGH (`#E05C5C`) | MUST use | 375-410 F, 8-24 hrs | MANDATORY per ASTM B849 |
| > 50 HRC | EXTREME (`#E05C5C` bold) | Maximum dose | AVOID acid pickle if possible | Use mechanical cleaning (blast) |

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting

Y: 27.5" to 32.5". Section label: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON FAILURES`.

Four coral-accented glass cards:

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| OVER-ETCHING | Too long; no inhibitor; too hot | Reduce time; add inhibitor; check temp |
| UNDER-PICKLING | Acid exhausted; iron too high; temp low (H2SO4) | Replenish acid; dump if iron limit hit |
| FLASH RUST | Delay between pickle and next step | Rinse immediately; proceed within 1-2 min |
| BLACK SMUT | Iron redeposited from overloaded bath | Improve rinsing; check iron level |
