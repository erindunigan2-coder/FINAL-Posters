## Phase 5 -- Parameters + Sub-Zero Treatment

### Temper Parameters (Y: 13.0"--19.0")

**Left -- STANDARD TEMPER (`#E8A020`):** 300--400 F; 1--2 hr min; air or N2; no restriction on heating rate; air cool; per AMS 2759/7: 300--375 F, 2 hr min.

**Right -- HARDNESS AFTER TEMPER (`#27AE60`):** As-quenched 60--65 HRC; after 300 F 59--63 (minimal loss); after 400 F 57--61; after 500 F 52--56 (excessive). `Stay below 400 F to preserve case hardness.`

### Sub-Zero Treatment (Y: 19.0"--26.0")

Section label: `SUB-ZERO TREATMENT -- RETAINED AUSTENITE CONVERSION`.

**Left -- THE PROBLEM (`#E05C5C`):** CN has HIGHER RA risk than carburizing; N stabilizes austenite; RA is soft; RA transforms in service = dimensional instability; spec limit typically max 15--20% RA.

**Right -- PROCEDURE (`#2EC4B6`):** -100 to -120 F; dry ice + alcohol or mechanical cryo; 1--2 hr; perform BEFORE tempering (quench -> sub-zero -> temper); converts RA to martensite; re-temper after.

Bottom: `Sequence: Quench -> Sub-Zero (if needed) -> Temper -> Final inspection. Never temper before sub-zero -- tempering stabilizes austenite.`

## Phase 6 -- Temper Rules

Y: 26.0"--32.5". Section label: `NON-NEGOTIABLE TEMPER RULES` in `#E05C5C`. 4 cards:

| Rule | Explanation |
|---|---|
| TEMPER WITHIN 1 HOUR OF QUENCH | Delayed temper increases cracking; residual stress is maximum after quench |
| NEVER SKIP THE TEMPER | Untempered martensite is extremely brittle; parts crack in service |
| NEVER EXCEED 400 F WITHOUT CAUSE | Over-tempering softens case; spec limits exist for a reason |
| SUB-ZERO BEFORE TEMPER | Tempering stabilizes RA; sub-zero after temper is less effective |
