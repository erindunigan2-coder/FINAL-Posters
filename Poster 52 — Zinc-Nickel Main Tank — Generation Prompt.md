## Phase 6 -- Defects + Analytical Methods

Y: 20.5" to 27.0". Section: `COMMON DEFECTS AND CORRECTIVE ACTIONS`.

### Step 9 -- Defect table (7 rows)

| Defect | Cause | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low Ni (<10%) | Low bath Ni, high CD, low temperature | Add Ni replenisher; reduce CD; raise temp |
| High Ni (>18%) | High bath Ni, low CD, high temperature | Reduce Ni; plate at higher CD |
| Burning at HCD | Low Zn metal, high CD | Add Zn; reduce CD |
| Dark deposits | Organic contamination, metallic (Cu, Fe) | Carbon treat; dummy plate; check cleaning |
| Blistering | Poor cleaning, insufficient activation | Review pre-treatment; extend electrocleaning |
| Uneven alloy distribution | Poor agitation, improper racking | Improve agitation; re-rack for uniform CD |
| Poor passivation acceptance | Over-bright deposit, Ni% out of range | Adjust brightener; correct alloy ratio |

### Step 10 -- Analytical Methods callout (Y: 25.8" to 26.8")

Accent `#2EC4B6`. `Zinc: EDTA @ pH 10 | Nickel: EDTA + murexide or DMG | NaOH: HCl titration | Alloy (deposit): XRF every shift | Hull cell: 267 mL, 2A, 10 min`

## Phase 7 -- Anode Management + Safety

Y: 27.0" to 32.5".

**Left -- Anode Management (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**
Title: `ANODE MANAGEMENT` `#E8A020`.
- Alkaline Zn-Ni: Pure zinc anodes (99.99% SHG). No nickel in the anode.
- Anode bags: PP, 1--5 micron. Mandatory. Prevents zinc fines.
- A:C ratio: 1:1 to 2:1. Maintain consistent anode area.
- Anode passivation: uncommon in alkaline (NaOH dissolves zinc readily)
- Monitor: Zinc metal builds (anode efficiency > cathode efficiency). Adjust by reducing anode area.

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**
Fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 1pt `#E05C5C`. Title: `SAFETY`.
- NaOH (100--150 g/L): severe chemical burn. pH > 13.
- Nickel compounds: dermal sensitizer; IARC Group 2B carcinogen (inhalation)
- PPE: NaOH-resistant gloves (neoprene), face shield, rubber apron
- Ventilation: fume suppression recommended; nickel mist controlled
- Wastewater: Zn at pH 8.5--9.5; Ni at pH 9.0--10.0. Two-stage precipitation may be needed.
