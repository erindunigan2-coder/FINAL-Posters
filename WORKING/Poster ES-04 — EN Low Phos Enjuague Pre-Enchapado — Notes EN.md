# English Translation Notes
## Poster ES-04 — EN Low Phos: Critical Rinse Pre-Plating (Stage 4 of 7)

---

### HEADER

**Eyebrow tag (amber monospace):**
Electroless Nickel — Stage 4 of 7

**Main title:**
CRITICAL RINSE

**Subtitle (coral/red):**
The most important rinse on the line — protects the nickel bath

**Body text:**
Chlorides cause pitting in the deposit. Chromate kills the entire bath. There is no way to reverse it once it gets in.

---

### KEY NUMBER ZONE

**Label:** THE KEY NUMBER

**Number:** < 20 µS/cm

**Note:**
Target conductivity in the final tank. Use deionized (DI) water — tap water is not adequate for this stage.

---

### WHAT YOU MUST DO

1. Use deionized (DI) water in the final tank — do not use tap water.
2. Measure conductivity at the start of the shift — it must be below 20 µS/cm.
3. Submerge parts 30–60 seconds per stage with gentle agitation.
4. For aluminum parts: transfer to the EN bath in under 30 seconds — the zinc layer oxidizes quickly.
5. If chloride levels exceed 5 ppm, change the water in the final tank immediately.

---

### COMMON PROBLEMS

| Problem | What to Look For | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Pitting in deposit | Small craters all over the surface | Switch to DI water; increase number of rinse stages |
| **EN bath inactive** | Bath not depositing nickel | **Check for chromate (Cr⁶⁺) contamination; notify supervisor immediately** |
| No initiation on aluminum | Part comes out with no plating | Shorten transfer time; verify fresh zincate |

*Translator note: Cr⁶⁺ = hexavalent chromium — a serious bath killer and hazardous substance.*

---

### SAFETY

- Rinse water contains acid drag-out — use gloves and goggles during maintenance.
- If the previous process uses chromate: the water is hazardous Cr⁶⁺ waste — goes to a dedicated sump, not general drain.
- Never discharge rinse water directly to the drain — goes to the treatment system.

---

### FOOTER

Consult the supervisor if conductivity will not drop below 20 µS/cm or if you suspect EN bath contamination.
