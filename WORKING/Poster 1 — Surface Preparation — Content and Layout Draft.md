## Section 1 — Watson Flag Status and Design Decisions

**Status: NONE — all data production-ready.**

All values in this document trace directly to Watson's Research Brief v1. Watson sourced data from: Products Finishing, NMFRC, PAVCO, ASTM B322, the 1993 Metal Finishing Guidebook, Drew's Quick Reference Notes, and domain expertise.

Watson's brief noted that the "80% of plating defects" statistic is an industry consensus approximation, not a formally measured value. I am using "80%+" on the poster — this is safe and appropriately qualified. The "+" signals it is an approximation without undermining its impact.

**Design decisions made during layout development:**

- **Layout orientation — Vertical flowchart, NOT horizontal:** Watson's brief suggests a horizontal left-to-right flow diagram. However, the poster is portrait orientation (24x36") — a horizontal flow would compress each step into a narrow column and leave massive dead space top and bottom. A vertical top-to-bottom flow uses the full height of the poster naturally, gives each step generous breathing room, and reads intuitively (top = start, bottom = plate). This is the right call for a poster format.

- **Substrate-specific variations (Watson Element: Substrate Decision Tree):** Included as a compact reference table in Zone 4 rather than a branching decision tree diagram. A decision tree would require a lot of real estate for what is essentially a 5-row lookup. The table format delivers the same information in a denser, more reference-friendly package.

- **Electrocleaner polarity diagram (Watson Visual Concept 4):** Consolidated into the Electrocleaning step's callout within the flowchart, rather than a separate side-by-side circuit diagram. The key insight — "Anodic = activates, Cathodic = scrubs, always finish anodic" — is communicated as a callout rule within the step, which is how a shop floor operator actually uses this information.

- **Water Break Test (Watson Visual Concept 2):** Given a dedicated two-panel illustration as Block C — this is the poster's second visual anchor after the flowchart. The "If the water beads, don't plate" line is the single most memorable takeaway and deserves prime real estate.

- **Failure Mode Gallery (Watson Visual Concept 3):** Included as Block E — a 3x2 grid of defect icons with one-line labels. Coral `#E05C5C` accent throughout. Positioned in Zone 4 alongside the substrate table.

- **The 80% callout:** Integrated into the header band as a large-format statistic — the poster's thesis statement. It sits at the intersection of the headline and subheading, impossible to miss from 8 feet.

**Flowchart construction:**

The flowchart is a series of 7 step blocks connected by downward arrows. Each step block has a color-coded left accent bar, a step number, a step name, and a compact parameter card.

**Step block styling:**
- Outer container: Rounded rectangle, full content width (minus margins), height approximately 1.8-2.0" per step
- Fill: `#1A1F2E` (base row) or `#252B3D` (alternate row) — alternating
- Left accent bar: 6 pt wide rectangle, accent color per step type (see below), full height of block, flush left
- Corner radius: 6 pt

**Step type color coding:**
- Alkaline steps (Soak Clean, Electro Clean): Teal `#2EC4B6` left accent
- Rinse steps: `#3A4055` Mid Slate left accent (neutral/structural)
- Acid step (Acid Activation): Amber `#E8A020` left accent
- Plating tank (final): Emerald `#27AE60` left accent

**Step number circle:**
- Circle: 36 pt diameter, fill = step accent color, positioned at the left edge of the content area (inside the accent bar)
- Number: Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 22 pt, `#1A1F2E` centered in circle

**Step name (Barlow SemiBold, 26-28 pt, step accent color):**

**Step role (Inter Regular, 18-20 pt, `#F0EDE8`):**

**Parameter card (JetBrains Mono Regular, 16-17 pt, `#F0EDE8`):** — compact data inside a `#1E2435` Dark Callout mini-box at right side of step block

---

**STEP 1 — SOAK CLEAN**

- Accent: Teal `#2EC4B6`
- Step name: `SOAK CLEAN`
- Role: `Removes bulk oils, greases, cutting fluids, and shop dirt by chemical action.`
- Parameter card:
  ```
  140-180 deg F | 4-8 oz/gal | 3-10 min
  ```
- Key insight (Inter Medium, 16 pt, `#2EC4B6`, below role):
  > The heavy lifter — if the soak cleaner fails, every step after it is compromised.

**STEP 2 — RINSE**

- Accent: Mid Slate `#3A4055`
- Step name: `RINSE`
- Role: `Removes alkaline cleaner residue. Prevents cross-contamination into acid tanks.`
- Parameter card:
  ```
  Counterflow cascade | Min. 2 tanks | 70-120 deg F
  ```

**STEP 3 — ELECTROLYTIC CLEAN**

- Accent: Teal `#2EC4B6`
- Step name: `ELECTROLYTIC CLEAN`
- Role: `Alkaline chemistry + gas evolution scrubs final soil traces from the surface.`
- Parameter card:
  ```
  15-50 ASF | 30-120 sec | 140-180 deg F
  ```
- Key insight (Inter Medium, 16 pt, `#2EC4B6`):
  > Cathodic = 2x gas volume (maximum scrubbing). Anodic = surface activation. Always finish anodic.

**STEP 4 — RINSE**

- Accent: Mid Slate `#3A4055`
- Step name: `RINSE`
- Role: `Removes alkaline electrocleaner before acid step. Prevents neutralization of the acid bath.`

**STEP 5 — ACID ACTIVATION**

- Accent: Amber `#E8A020`
- Step name: `ACID ACTIVATION`
- Role: `Brief acid immersion dissolves surface oxides and leaves bare, active metal ready for plating.`
- Parameter card:
  ```
  Room temp | 10-50% v/v | 15-60 sec
  ```
- Key insight (Inter Medium, 16 pt, `#E8A020`):
  > Seconds count. Too long = etching. Too short = oxide remains.

**STEP 6 — RINSE**

- Accent: Mid Slate `#3A4055`
- Step name: `FINAL RINSE`
- Role: `Last rinse before plating. Must be extremely clean — acid drag-in alters bath pH and chemistry.`

**STEP 7 — PLATE**

- Accent: Emerald `#27AE60`
- Step name: `PLATE`
- Role: `Clean, active surface receives the plating deposit with full adhesion.`
- Key insight (Inter Medium, 16 pt, `#27AE60`):
  > A properly prepared surface is the foundation of every quality deposit.

**Arrow connectors between steps:**
- Canva line elements with downward arrowheads, `#3A4055` Mid Slate, 2 pt stroke
- Positioned centered between step blocks
- Arrow length: approximately 0.3" between blocks

**Design notes:**
- The flowchart is the HERO visual — it dominates the poster and is the first thing a viewer reads after the headline
- Steps 2, 4, and 6 (rinses) can be slightly shorter in height than the process steps to create visual hierarchy — rinses are important but are supporting steps, not the main acts
- Parameter cards sit right-aligned within each step block to create a consistent data column down the right side of the flowchart

| Substrate | Sequence Variation | Key Consideration | Left Border |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Steel / Iron** | Standard sequence | H₂ embrittlement risk above 39 HRC — minimize cathodic exposure; bake within 4 hrs | Teal |
