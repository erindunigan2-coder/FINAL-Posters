# English Translation Notes
## Poster ES-05 — EN Low Phos: EN Bath (Stage 5 of 7)

---

### HEADER

**Eyebrow tag (amber monospace):**
Electroless Nickel — Stage 5 of 7

**Main title:**
EN BATH

**Subtitle (emerald/green):**
Autocatalytic nickel-phosphorus deposit — no rectifier, no anode

**Body text:**
The chemistry deposits the nickel on its own. Stable temperature, pH in range, and stabilizer present — if any one of these is missing, the bath decomposes violently and exothermically.

*Translator note: "Autocatalítico" = autocatalytic — the chemical reaction is self-sustaining once started.*

---

### KEY NUMBER ZONE

**Label:** THE KEY NUMBER

**Number:** 80–92 °C

**Note:**
Operating temperature. Below 80 °C deposition slows. Above 95 °C the bath can decompose. Monitor continuously.

---

### WHAT YOU MUST DO

1. Verify temperature (80–92 °C), pH (8.5–9.5), and Ni²⁺ level (4.5–6.0 g/L) at the start of the shift and every 2 hours.
2. Maintain continuous filtration — minimum 1 to 2 tank volumes per hour.
3. Never leave the hot bath idle without parts — use a dummy (auxiliary) part if needed.
4. **(Red alert)** If you notice sudden turbidity, unusual bubbling, or temperature rising on its own — STOP and call the supervisor immediately.
5. Record the MTO number — after 6–8 MTOs the bath must be discarded.

*Translator note: MTO = Metal Turnover — a measure of how many times the nickel content of the bath has been consumed and replenished.*

---

### COMMON PROBLEMS

| Problem | What to Look For | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Low deposit rate | Thickness per shift too low (< 10 µm/hr) | Check temperature, pH, and Ni²⁺ concentration |
| Pitting | Craters in the plated surface | Check chloride drag-out from rinse; increase agitation |
| **Bath decomposition** | Violent turbidity, temperature rising on its own | **EMERGENCY: dilute with cold DI water; do not add chemicals; call supervisor** |

---

### SAFETY

- Bath at 80–92 °C causes serious burns — use thermal gloves when handling loads.
- The reaction continuously releases H₂ — active ventilation required; do not allow accumulation.
- Dissolved nickel is a sensitizer — avoid repeated skin contact; use nitrile gloves.
- **(Red)** Bath decomposition: evacuate the area and call the supervisor — do not try to stop it alone.

---

### FOOTER

Consult the supervisor before adding any chemical to the bath or if pH or temperature is out of range.
