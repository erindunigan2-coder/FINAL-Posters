Electroless Nickel — Stage 5 of 7
EN BATH
Autocatalytic nickel-phosphorus deposit — no rectifier, no anode
The chemistry deposits the nickel on its own. If stabilizer is depleted, if temperature spikes above 95 °C, or if excess catalytic surface is introduced, the bath can decompose — a rapid, exothermic, irreversible reaction.

THE KEY NUMBER
80–92 °C
Operating temperature. Below 80 °C deposition slows. Above 95 °C the bath can decompose. Monitor continuously.
WHAT YOU MUST DO
1
Verify temperature (80–92 °C), pH (per supplier TDS), and Ni²⁺ level (4.5–6.0 g/L) at shift start and every 2 hours.
2
Maintain continuous filtration — minimum 1 to 2 tank volumes per hour.
3
Never leave the hot bath idle without parts — use a dummy part if needed.
4
If you notice sudden turbidity, unusual bubbling, or temperature rising on its own — STOP and call the supervisor immediately.
5
Track the MTO count — typical bath life is 6–8 metal turnovers, but follow your supplier's specification. Performance degrades as MTO increases.

⚠ SAFETY
●Bath at 80–92 °C causes serious burns — use thermal gloves when handling loads.
●Reaction continuously releases H₂ — active ventilation required; do not allow accumulation.
●Dissolved nickel is a sensitizer — avoid repeated skin contact; use nitrile gloves.
●Bath decomposition: evacuate the area and call the supervisor — do not try to stop it alone.
Consult the supervisor before adding any chemical to the bath or if pH or temperature is out of range.
