# English Translation Notes
## Poster ES-07 — EN Low Phos: Final Treatment (Stage 7 of 7)

---

### HEADER

**Eyebrow tag (amber monospace):**
Electroless Nickel — Stage 7 of 7

**Main title:**
FINAL TREATMENT

**Subtitle:**
Heat treatment — hardens the deposit and relieves hydrogen embrittlement

**Body text:**
650 HV coming out of the bath. 1100 HV coming out of the oven. For high-strength steel, baking is not optional — it is the difference between a good part and one that can fracture without warning.

*Translator note: HV = Vickers Hardness. "Fragilización por hidrógeno" = hydrogen embrittlement (HE) — hydrogen absorbed during plating that can cause delayed cracking.*

---

### KEY NUMBER ZONE

**Label:** THE KEY NUMBER

**Number:** 190–210 °C / 4 hours min.

**Note:**
HE relief baking for high-strength steel (> 1000 MPa). Perform within the first 4 hours after plating. For maximum hardness: 350–400 °C, 1 hour minimum.

---

### WHAT YOU MUST DO

1. Identify the substrate type and required treatment before loading the oven.
2. **(Red alert)** For high-strength steel (> 1000 MPa or > 40 HRC): bake at 190–210 °C within the first 4 hours after plating — do not wait.
3. For maximum hardness on standard steel: bake at 350–400 °C for a minimum of 1 hour.
4. Load parts individually in the oven — do not stack them; contact causes marks.
5. Record in the logbook: part number, temperature, time, oven ID, and operator name.

*Translator note: HRC = Rockwell Hardness C scale. HE baking (horneado de alivio) is required by ASTM B849.*

---

### COMMON PROBLEMS

| Problem | What to Look For | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient hardness | Deposit does not reach specified hardness | Check oven calibration (±5 °C); confirm temperature reached the part |
| **Delayed fracture in high-strength steel** | Part fractures hours or days after plating | **Verify HE baking was done within 4 hours and ran a minimum of 4 hours** |
| Adhesion loss on aluminum | Deposit lifts or blisters | Never exceed 290 °C on aluminum |

---

### SAFETY

- Oven operates at 190–400 °C — severe burn risk. Use certified thermal gloves.
- Baking releases hydrogen — active ventilation required in the oven area.
- If oven uses inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar): asphyxiation risk — verify oxygen level before entering the area.
- Allow parts to cool completely before handling without protection.

---

### FOOTER

HE relief baking for high-strength steel is mandatory per ASTM B849 — never skip this step without written supervisor authorization.
