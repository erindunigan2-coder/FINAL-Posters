FINAL
TREATMENT
Heat treatment — hardens the deposit and relieves hydrogen embrittlement
As-plated hardness is typically 500–700 HV depending on phosphorus content. After heat treatment at 350–400 °C, hardness can reach 900–1100 HV — highest for low-phosphorus deposits. For high-strength steel, baking is not optional — it is the difference between a good part and one that can fracture without warning.

THE KEY NUMBER
190–210 °C / 4 hours min.
HE relief baking for high-strength steel (> 1000 MPa). The 4-hour clock starts when the part leaves the EN bath — not when it finishes rinsing or drying. For maximum hardness: 350–400 °C, 1 hour min.
WHAT YOU MUST DO
1
Identify the substrate type and required treatment before loading the oven.
2
For high-strength steel (> 1000 MPa or approx. > 34 HRC): bake at 190–210 °C within the first 4 hours after plating — the clock starts when the part leaves the EN bath. Do not wait.
3
For maximum hardness on steel substrates only: bake at 350–400 °C for a minimum of 1 hour. Do not apply this cycle to aluminum — see the 290 °C limit below.
4
Load parts individually in the oven — do not stack them; contact causes marks.
5
Record in the logbook: part number, temperature, time, oven ID, and operator name.

⚠ SAFETY
●Oven operates at 190–400 °C — severe burn risk. Use certified thermal gloves.
●Baking releases hydrogen — active ventilation required in the oven area.
●If oven uses inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar): asphyxiation risk — verify oxygen level before entering the area.
●Allow parts to cool completely before handling without protection.
HE relief baking for high-strength steel is mandatory per ASTM B849 — never skip this step without written supervisor authorization.
