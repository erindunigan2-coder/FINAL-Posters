# Poster #203 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Black Oxide -- Pre-Oxide

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 4 of 8. This rinse sits between the acid pickle and the 285 F black oxide bath. It has one job: remove every trace of acid. But it also carries a safety-critical function -- water dragged into a 285 F concentrated caustic bath causes violent spattering. Parts must be well-rinsed AND well-drained. This is the most safety-sensitive rinse in the entire black oxide line.

### ZONE 4 -- Safety Warning -- Hot Caustic Spattering

**Section label:** `SAFETY -- WATER INTO HOT CAUSTIC` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Large Safety Panel**

Rounded rect, H: 4.8", fill `#1E2435`, border 2 pt `#E05C5C`, left accent 0.12" `#E05C5C`.

**Header inside panel:**
- Triangle warning icon (SVG monoline, `#E05C5C`)
- `DANGER: VIOLENT SPATTERING HAZARD` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 28 pt `#E05C5C`

**Body (two columns inside panel):**

Left column (X: 1.0", W: 10.5"):
- `THE PHYSICS` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#E8A020`
- Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 160%:
```
The black oxide bath operates at 280--295 F
(138--146 C) -- above the boiling point of
water at standard pressure.

Water carried into this bath on parts
INSTANTLY boils and expands ~1,700x in
volume (liquid to steam).

The result: explosive spattering of
concentrated hot caustic solution
(600--900 g/L NaOH at 285 F).
```

Right column (X: 12.5", W: 10.0"):
- `PREVENTION` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#27AE60`
- Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 160%:
```
1. Rinse thoroughly -- multiple dips if needed
2. DRAIN until dripping stops completely
3. Tilt and rotate baskets to empty blind holes
4. Shake parts free of trapped water
5. Pre-heat parts in hot water rinse before
   oxide bath (optional but recommended)

PPE: full face shield, chemical apron,
long gloves, heat-resistant over-gloves.
```

### ZONE 6 -- Drain Time + Best Practices

**Section label:** `DRAIN TIME AND BEST PRACTICES` -- Y: 26.7".

**Two-column layout (Y: 27.3" to 32.3"):**

**Left -- Drain Time Guide (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

| Part Type | Minimum Drain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flat parts / plates | 15--30 sec | Tilt to shed water |
| Baskets of small parts | 30--60 sec | Shake basket; rotate |
| Complex geometries | 60--120 sec | Tilt multiple angles; blind holes trap water |
| Threaded parts | 60--120 sec | Spin or blow out threads |
| Tubes / hollow parts | 60--120 sec | Orient vertically to drain |

Data: JetBrains Mono Regular 12 pt `#F0EDE8`. Headers: Barlow SemiBold 13 pt.

**Right -- Best Practices (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**

Callout box, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#2EC4B6`:

```
1. Two-stage rinse: immersion + spray for thoroughness
2. Check pH on part surface -- must be neutral (6--8)
3. Drain until NO dripping -- count to 10 after last drop
4. Watch for trapped water in cups, channels, or threads
5. If in doubt, add a hot water pre-heat dip before oxide bath
6. Never rush this step -- 30 extra seconds of drain time
   prevents a safety incident
```

## Design Notes

This is one of the few rinse posters in the series where safety is the lead story, not chemistry. The hot caustic spattering panel must be the largest, most visually dominant element on the poster -- coral border, oversized warning text, physically unmissable at wall distance. The 1,700x expansion fact (liquid water to steam) is the kind of visceral detail that makes people remember the poster. Every black oxide shop should have this on the wall next to the oxide tank.
