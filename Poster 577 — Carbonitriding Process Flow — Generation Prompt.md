## Phase 6 -- Comparison + Troubleshooting

### Carbonitriding vs. Carburizing (Y: 22.0"--28.5")

Section label: `WHY CARBONITRIDING? -- CARBONITRIDING VS. CARBURIZING`.

**Left -- CARBONITRIDING (`#27AE60`):** 1400--1600 F | ECD 0.003--0.030 in | 1--6 hr | Endo + NH3 | Oil quench | Best for low-alloy, free-machining steels | N lowers critical cooling rate | Higher RA risk | Less distortion. Highlight: `Nitrogen makes martensite possible in steels too lean to carburize alone`.

**Right -- CARBURIZING (`#E8A020`):** 1650--1750 F | ECD 0.020--0.100+ in | 2--28 hr | Endo only | Oil quench | Best for 8620, 9310 | No hardenability boost | Moderate RA | More distortion. Highlight: `Deep cases on alloy steels -- the workhorse for gears, shafts, and bearings`.

### Troubleshooting (Y: 28.5"--32.5")

Section label: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON PROBLEMS`. 4 cards:

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| EXCESSIVE RETAINED AUSTENITE | Too much NH3; N stabilizes austenite | Reduce NH3 %; sub-zero treatment (-120 F) |
| SURFACE POROSITY | Excess NH3; N2 gas entrapment | Reduce NH3; avoid spikes |
| SHALLOW CASE | Temp too low; time too short; excess NH3 | Optimize temp/time; reduce NH3 |
| SOFT SPOTS | Part nesting; surface contamination | Proper fixturing; thorough cleaning |
