# Poster #664 -- Construction Workup
## Flash / Leveling -- Liquid Spray Painting

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 8. Flash and leveling is where solvent does its invisible work -- evaporating at just the right rate to let the film flow smooth before it skins over. The hero contrasts three flash environments: ambient, force, and waterborne. The intercoat adhesion recoat window table is the poster's second anchor -- miss that 4-24 hour epoxy window and you are scuff-sanding the entire job. Tail solvents are the formulator's secret weapon for leveling, and they deserve their own callout.

### ZONE 3 -- Three Flash Environments Hero

**Section label:** `THREE FLASH ENVIRONMENTS -- MATCHED TO YOUR COATING` -- Y: 4.4".

**BLOCK B -- Three Columns (Y: 5.0" to 14.5")**

*Ambient Flash (X: 0.5", W: 7.33"):*
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#27AE60`
- Title: `AMBIENT FLASH` -- Barlow SemiBold, 20 pt, `#27AE60`
- Subtitle: `Standard for Solvent-Borne Coatings`
- Parameters (JetBrains Mono 12 pt):
```
Temperature: 65--85 F (18--29 C)
Time: 5--15 min between coats
Humidity: < 85% RH preferred
Air movement: Gentle, not forced
```
- How it works (Inter Medium 14 pt):
- `Solvent evaporates naturally`
- `Film surface skins first, bulk follows`
- `Leveling occurs while film is still fluid`
- Best for: `Standard alkyds, lacquers, single-stage enamels`
- Note: `Longer flash in cold/humid conditions; shorter in hot/dry`

*Force Flash (X: 8.17", W: 7.33"):*
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `FORCE FLASH` -- Barlow SemiBold, 20 pt, `#E8A020`
- Subtitle: `Accelerated Solvent Release`
- Parameters:
```
Temperature: 140--180 F (60--82 C)
Time: 5--10 min
Purpose: Drive off solvent before bake
Prevents solvent pop in bake oven
```
- How it works:
- `Elevated temp accelerates evaporation`
- `Drives solvent from film interior`
- `Prevents trapped solvent causing defects in bake`
- Best for: `Before bake cure; multi-coat baked systems`
- Warning (Coral): `Skipping force flash before bake = solvent pop (bubbles in cured film)`

*Waterborne Flash (X: 15.83", W: 7.67"):*
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, top accent `#2EC4B6`
- Title: `WATERBORNE FLASH` -- Barlow SemiBold, 20 pt, `#2EC4B6`
- Subtitle: `Controlled Environment Required`
- Parameters:
```
Temperature: 70--80 F (21--27 C)
Humidity: 50--70% RH (CRITICAL)
Airflow: Directional, controlled velocity
Time: 3--10 min
```
- How it works:
- `Water evaporation is slower than solvent`
- `Requires controlled humidity and airflow`
- `Too fast = skin-over = solvent pop`
- `Too slow = sag and run`
- Best for: `Waterborne basecoats (automotive, industrial)`
- Note: `Waterborne demands environmental control that solvent-borne does not`

### ZONE 4 -- Intercoat Recoat Window

**Section label:** `THE RECOAT WINDOW -- MISS IT AND YOU ARE SANDING` -- Y: 15.2".

**BLOCK C -- Recoat Window Table + Callout (Y: 15.8" to 20.8")**

**Left -- Recoat Window Table (X: 0.5", W: 13.0"):**

Title: `MAXIMUM RECOAT WINDOWS BY COATING TYPE` -- Barlow SemiBold, 18 pt, `#F0EDE8`

| Coating Type | Recoat Window | What Happens If Exceeded | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkyd enamel | 24--72 hr | Surface too hard for chemical bite | Scuff sand 320--400 grit |
| 2K Epoxy | 4--24 hr (CRITICAL) | Epoxy hardens; no topcoat adhesion | Scuff sand 320--400 grit |
| 2K Urethane | 1--24 hr | Surface cures beyond recoat compatibility | Scuff sand 320--400 grit |
| Lacquer | Unlimited | Solvent in next coat re-dissolves surface | No sanding needed |
| Waterborne latex | 24--48 hr | Film coalesces fully | Scuff sand |

Header row: fill `#3A4055`. Data: JetBrains Mono 12 pt. Alternating rows: `#1E2435` / `#252B3D`.

**Right -- Recoat Warning (X: 14.0", W: 9.5"):**

Callout box, fill `#1E2435`, border 2 pt `#E05C5C`.
Title: `THE EPOXY TRAP` -- Barlow SemiBold, 20 pt, `#E05C5C`

`2K epoxy is the most common recoat window failure. The 4--24 hour window is short and temperature-dependent (faster cure in warm weather shrinks the window). If you miss it, the topcoat will NOT adhere without mechanical scuffing. No exceptions.`

### ZONE 5 -- Leveling Mechanics + Tail Solvents

**Section label:** `HOW SOLVENT BLEND CONTROLS LEVELING` -- Y: 21.2".

**Two-column layout (Y: 21.8" to 26.3"):**

**Left -- Leveling Mechanics (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Callout box, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`.
Title: `LEVELING -- WHAT CAUSES ORANGE PEEL` -- Barlow SemiBold, 18 pt, `#E8A020`

Four causes (Inter Medium 14 pt):
1. `Too-fast solvent evaporation (film skins before flowing smooth)`
2. `Too-low atomization pressure (coarse droplets do not merge)`
3. `Gun too far from part (droplets partially dry in flight)`
4. `Spray applied too dry (insufficient wet film for flow)`

Fix: `Slower solvent blend, higher atomization, closer gun distance, heavier wet film`

**Right -- Tail Solvents (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**

Callout box, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#27AE60`.
Title: `TAIL SOLVENTS -- THE LEVELING SECRET` -- Barlow SemiBold, 18 pt, `#27AE60`

Body:
- `Tail solvents are slow-evaporating solvents that keep the film fluid long enough to level`
- `Examples: butyl cellosolve, PM acetate, butyl carbitol`
- `They evaporate LAST -- holding the film open for flow`
- `Too much tail solvent = sag (film stays fluid too long)`
- `Too little = orange peel (film skins too fast)`
- `The solvent blend is formulated as a cascade: fast evaporators lift first, tail solvents hold the film open for leveling`

**Section label:** `WHEN FLASH / LEVELING FAILS -- 6 DEFECTS` -- Y: 26.7".

| Position | Defect | Color | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Design Notes

The recoat window table is this poster's critical contribution to the shop floor. The 2K epoxy trap is real -- painters who work with alkyds (72-hour window) get caught when they switch to epoxy (4-24 hours). The tail solvents callout gives the more advanced reader insight into formulation -- understanding WHY a slow thinner works is more powerful than just knowing to add one. The waterborne flash panel is increasingly important as shops transition from solvent-borne for VOC compliance.
