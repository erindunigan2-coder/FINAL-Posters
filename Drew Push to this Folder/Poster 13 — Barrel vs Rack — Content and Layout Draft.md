## Section 1 — Watson Flag Status and Design Decisions

**Status: ONE FLAG OPEN — non-blocking.**

**Open flag:** Watson should confirm the generally accepted upper weight and size limits for barrel plating. Industry consensus is approximately 6-8 inches in longest dimension and 1-2 lbs per part, but these thresholds vary by barrel design and process. The poster currently uses "approximately 6-8 inches / 1-2 lbs" with a qualifier. Watson can refine these values; the poster can proceed with the qualified ranges.

**Content sources:**
- Drew's Quick Reference: barrel rotation speed (6-8 RPM), higher zinc required for barrel, zinc rack = 15-20 ASF / barrel = 5-10 ASF
- Watson's Current Density Brief: rack vs. barrel CD comparison table, electrical contact differences, effective plating time
- General industry knowledge: barrel capacity, throwing power requirements, deposit uniformity characteristics

**Design decisions:**

- **Layout: split-poster comparison.** Left half = Barrel, right half = Rack. Central comparison table spans full width. Decision flowchart at bottom. This is the most natural way to present a binary comparison — the eye scans left-right and compares directly.

- **Illustration: cross-section views.** Left = barrel cross-section showing parts tumbling, cathode buttons, rotation direction. Right = rack showing parts hanging, direct contact points, current distribution. These are schematic-level, not photorealistic — built from Canva shapes.

- **This poster is a decision tool, not a process deep-dive.** Its primary audience is quoting engineers, sales teams, and supervisors deciding which method to use for a given job. The design emphasizes the decision matrix, not operating parameters.

### BLOCK C — Comparison Matrix (HERO Data Table — Zone 3)

**Section label (Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 32 pt, `#F0EDE8`, centered):**

> HEAD-TO-HEAD COMPARISON

**Table structure — 3 columns:**
1. **Factor** (~30% width) — Inter Medium, 18-20 pt, `#F0EDE8`
2. **Barrel** (~35% width) — Inter Regular, 18-20 pt, `#F0EDE8`
3. **Rack** (~35% width) — Inter Regular, 18-20 pt, `#F0EDE8`

**Column header row:** `#3A4055` Mid Slate fill
- `FACTOR` | `BARREL` (with Teal `#2EC4B6` accent) | `RACK` (with Amber `#E8A020` accent)

**Data rows (alternating `#1A1F2E` / `#252B3D`):**

| Factor | Barrel | Rack |
|--------|--------|------|
| **Part size** | Small — typically < 6-8" longest dimension; < 1-2 lbs per part | No practical upper limit — limited by tank size and rack capacity |
| **Part geometry** | Must tumble freely; no nesting, tangling, or fragile features | Any geometry — complex, delicate, or asymmetric parts accommodated |
| **Volume** | High volume — hundreds to thousands of parts per load | Low to medium volume — parts individually fixtured |
| **Electrical contact** | Intermittent — parts tumble against cathode buttons (~30-50% of cycle) | Continuous — direct fixture-to-part contact (100% of cycle) |
| **Current density** | Lower CD required (e.g., zinc: 5-10 ASF barrel vs. 15-20 ASF rack) | Higher CD achievable — uniform current distribution |
| **Deposit uniformity** | Less uniform — contact points get heavier deposit; recesses may plate thin | More uniform — part position controls thickness distribution |
| **Throwing power** | Higher throwing power required — current must reach buried parts | Moderate — anode-to-cathode geometry can be optimized |
| **Throughput (cost/part)** | Lower cost per part at high volume — labor is loading/unloading only | Higher cost per part — individual fixturing is labor-intensive |
| **Surface finish risk** | Part-to-part contact causes dings, scratches — not for cosmetic surfaces | No part-to-part contact — suitable for cosmetic and decorative finishes |
| **Plating time** | Longer — intermittent contact means effective plating time is 30-50% of total | Shorter — full current exposure for the entire cycle |

**Table footnote (Inter Regular, 13-14 pt, `#F0EDE8` at 60% opacity):**

> *CD ranges shown are representative for acid zinc plating (Drew's Quick Reference). Actual ranges vary by process — see Poster #11 Current Density Quick Reference for full process-specific CD data.*

### BLOCK D — Decision Guide (Zone 4 — Two-Column)

**Section label (Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 28 pt, `#F0EDE8`, centered):**

**Left column — "Barrel Wins When..." (Teal accent)**

> BARREL WINS WHEN...

Bullet list (Inter Regular, 18 pt, `#F0EDE8`):

> - Volume is high and cost per part must be low
> - Cosmetic finish is not critical
> - Parts do not nest or tangle
> - Throwing power of the bath chemistry is adequate

**Right column — "Rack Wins When..." (Amber accent)**

Callout box: fill `#1E2435`, left border 4 pt `#E8A020` Amber, corner radius 6 pt

Title (Barlow SemiBold, 22 pt, `#E8A020`):
> RACK WINS WHEN...

Bullet list (Inter Regular, 18 pt, `#F0EDE8`):
> - Parts are large, fragile, or have complex geometry
> - Deposit uniformity or thickness control is critical
> - Surface finish is cosmetic or decorative
> - Volume is low to medium (custom or job-shop work)
> - Specifications require measurable thickness at specific locations
