# Poster #180 -- Construction Workup
## Chromate Conversion (Hex) -- Main Stage

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

The main event of the CC-04 cluster. This is the densest poster in the series, covering: the complex redox coating mechanism (Al dissolution, Cr6+ reduction to Cr3+, gel layer formation), bath chemistry (CrO3, ferricyanide, fluoride), the self-healing property in full detail, operating parameters for immersion/spray/pen application, film characteristics, color interpretation, and the critical pH variable.

The self-healing mechanism is the reason hex chromate persists despite being a carcinogen. Cr6+ trapped in the gel migrates to scratches and redeposits Cr3+ oxide -- no other conversion coating does this. This is the flagship technical content for the entire cluster.

- Title: `REDOX COATING MECHANISM` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#27AE60`

Three numbered steps:
```
1. ALUMINUM DISSOLVES (Anodic): Al --> Al3+ + 3e-
2. CHROMATE REDUCES (Cathodic): Cr2O7(2-) + 14H+ + 6e- --> 2Cr3+ + 7H2O
3. GEL FORMS: Cr3+ + Al3+ + oxide + RESIDUAL Cr6+ --> mixed oxide gel layer
```

`The gel contains BOTH Cr3+ (barrier) and Cr6+ (self-healing reservoir)` Inter Medium 14 pt `#E8A020`

Step numbers: `#27AE60`. Equations: JetBrains Mono 12 pt `#F0EDE8`.

**Gel composition callout (Y: 13.5"):**
- `Cr2O3/Cr(OH)3 backbone + Al2O3 + CrO4(2-) reservoir (10--30% of total Cr is Cr6+) + CrF3/AlF3 bridges` JetBrains Mono 11 pt `#F0EDE8`

- `When scratched: Cr6+ ions migrate to exposed Al` Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`
- `Cr6+ reduces to Cr3+ oxide -- resealing the damage` Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`
- `NO OTHER CONVERSION COATING HAS THIS PROPERTY` Barlow SemiBold 14 pt `#27AE60`
- `Trivalent chrome (Type II) CANNOT self-heal` Inter Medium 13 pt `#E05C5C`

**Right -- Bath Chemistry Table (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
- Rounded rect, H: 4.8", fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020` 0.06"
- Title: `BATH CHEMISTRY` Barlow SemiBold 20 pt `#E8A020`

| Component | Concentration | Function |
|---|---|---|
| CrO3 or Na2Cr2O7 | 3--6 g/L (as Cr6+) | Oxidizing agent, film-forming |
| K3Fe(CN)6 | 0.5--1.5 g/L | Accelerator (depolarizer) |
| NaF or HF | 0.5--1.5 g/L as F- | Activator (dissolves Al2O3 barrier) |
| H3PO4 (optional) | 1--3 g/L | Phosphate-bearing films |
| pH (NaOH or HNO3) | 1.2--2.0 | MASTER VARIABLE |

`pH is the single most important control parameter. Below 1.2: over-attack. Above 2.0: no coating.` Inter Medium 12 pt `#E8A020`

## Design Notes

This is the flagship poster of the CC-04 cluster -- the one that justifies the entire series. The self-healing mechanism is the crown jewel: the visual showing Cr6+ migrating from the gel layer to a scratch and redepositing as Cr3+ oxide is the single most important graphic in the conversion coating poster library. It explains in one image why hex chromate persists despite being a carcinogen, why trivalent cannot replace it in all applications, and why aerospace still specifies Type I.

The color interpretation guide answers the question every operator asks: "Is this gold color right?" The answer is nuanced -- color indicates thickness but is not a spec criterion. Coating weight and salt spray are.

pH as the "master variable" must be visually emphasized. Every other parameter can be off slightly and the bath recovers; pH outside 1.3--1.8 and the process stops working.
