## Phase 4 -- Temperature-to-Properties Hero Table

Y: 4.2" to 14.5". Section label: `SALT BATH TEMPERATURE vs. TRANSFORMATION PRODUCT`. 4-row master table with color-coded left accents.

| Salt Temp | Bainite Type | Hardness | Elong. | Toughness | Best Applications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400--500 F | Lower bainite (Coral accent) | 50--55 HRC | 1--4% | Moderate | High-strength springs, clips, wear |
| 500--600 F | Mixed bainite (Amber) | 42--50 HRC | 4--7% | Good | Structural, fasteners |
| 600--700 F | Upper bainite (Emerald) | 35--42 HRC | 7--12% | Very good | Gears (ADI), impact-loaded |
| 700--750 F | Coarse/ausferrite (Teal) | 30--38 HRC | 10--18% | Excellent | High-ductility ADI, shock |

Key: `Lower temp = harder but less ductile. Higher temp = tougher but softer.`

---

## Phase 5 -- Lower vs. Upper Bainite

Y: 14.5" to 21.5". Section label: `LOWER BAINITE vs. UPPER BAINITE`.

**Left -- Lower Bainite** (Amber, 400--500 F): fine acicular ferrite + carbide, 50--55 HRC, 250--300 ksi tensile, 1--4% elongation, excellent fatigue. Choose when hardness and strength are primary.

**Right -- Upper Bainite** (Teal, 600--750 F): coarser ferrite laths + carbide between, 30--42 HRC, 150--200 ksi, 7--18% elongation, excellent impact. Choose when ductility and impact are primary.

## Phase 6 -- ADI Grade Mapping + Best Practices

**ASTM A897 ADI Grade Table** (Y: 21.5" to 27.5"): 5 grades with tensile, yield, elongation, hardness (HB), salt temp range, and bainite type. Note: ADI properties depend on base iron nodularity (min 80%).

**Temperature Control Best Practices** (Y: 27.5" to 32.5"): DO: continuous agitation, monthly TC calibration, multiple zones > 500 gal, pre-heat loads, monitor recovery, min 5:1 salt-to-part ratio. DON'T: overload bath, skip agitation, start timer before recovery, contaminate salt, single TC on large bath.
