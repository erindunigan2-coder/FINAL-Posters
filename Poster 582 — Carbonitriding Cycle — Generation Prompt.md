### Case Depth (Y: 14.5"--20.5")

| ECD (at 50 HRC) | Time at 1550 F | Application | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.005 in (0.13 mm) | 1--1.5 hr | Thin wear case; light-duty | Minimum practical |
| 0.010 in (0.25 mm) | 2--3 hr | Standard automotive; fasteners | Most common target |
| 0.020 in (0.50 mm) | 3--4 hr | Heavy-duty wear | Approaching maximum |
| 0.030 in (0.75 mm) | 5--6 hr | Maximum practical for CN | Above this: switch to carburizing |

Bottom: `Above 0.030 in ECD, switch to carburizing. Carbonitriding at depth = excessive retained austenite.`

### Suitable Steels (Y: 20.5"--26.5")

Section label: `IDEAL STEELS FOR CARBONITRIDING`. 4-row table:

| Category | Grades | Why Carbonitriding? |
|---|---|---|
| Plain carbon | 1018, 1020, 1022 | Low hardenability -- NEED nitrogen boost for martensite |
| Free machining | 12L14, 1117, 1215, 1144 | Classic CN candidates; sulfur inclusions tolerable |
| Sintered (PM) | Iron-based PM | Porosity limits case; CN's thin case is ideal |
| Low alloy (optional) | 8620 | Works but often overkill -- carburizing usually better |

Bottom: `The whole point of carbonitriding: nitrogen lowers the critical cooling rate so even 1018 forms hard martensite under oil quench.`

## Phase 6 -- Metallurgical Mechanism

Y: 26.5"--32.5". Section label: `WHAT HAPPENS IN THE FURNACE`.

3-step mechanism: (1) `CARBON TRANSFER: 2CO -> C(dissolved) + CO2` (`#E8A020`). (2) `NITROGEN TRANSFER: NH3 -> N(atomic) + 3/2 H2` (`#2EC4B6`). (3) `DIFFUSION: C and N migrate inward per Fick's law; combined C+N = 0.8--1.0% equivalent` (`#27AE60`).

Key insight: `Nitrogen lowers Ms temperature and retards pearlite/bainite. Steels that would form soft pearlite if merely carburized can now be quenched to hard martensite. That is the entire value proposition.`
