# Poster #128 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- Silver

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of 8. Cleaning for silver plating follows standard alkaline soak clean + electroclean protocol but with heightened emphasis on substrate-specific sequences. Copper/brass substrates follow one path; steel substrates require a copper strike undercoat before silver. This poster covers cleaning only -- the substrate routing decision is the key visual.

### ZONE 5 -- Substrate Compatibility Table

**Section label:** `SUBSTRATE COMPATIBILITY -- SILVER ADHESION` -- Y: 20.7".

**BLOCK F -- Table (Y: 21.3" to 26.3")**

| Substrate | Direct Silver? | Required Undercoat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | YES (with strike) | None | Most common silver substrate |
| Brass | YES (with strike) | None | Same as copper |
| Nickel-plated | YES (with strike) | None | H2SO4 5% activation, 10--15 sec |
| Steel / Iron | NO | Copper strike first | Silver will not adhere to steel |
| Stainless steel | NO | Nickel + copper strike | Very poor adhesion without undercoat |
| Aluminum | NO | Zincate + copper strike | Rarely silver-plated |
| Kovar / Alloy 42 | YES (with strike) | None | Common for electronics |

Table: standard alternating row style. "NO" cells in `#E05C5C`. "YES" cells in `#27AE60`.

## Design Notes

The substrate routing diagram is the hero -- copper/brass goes straight to silver strike, steel must detour through a copper strike first. This decision point is the single most important concept for cleaning in silver plating. The water-break-free callout is universal but especially critical here because silver is more sensitive to surface contamination than almost any other plating process.
