## Phase 5 -- What the Next Process Needs

Y: 15.0" to 21.0". Section: `DOWNSTREAM REQUIREMENTS -- WHAT COMES AFTER NICKEL`.

Four callout boxes in a 2x2 grid:

**DECORATIVE CHROME** (accent `#2EC4B6`):
Nickel must be active (not passivated). Transfer quickly -- ideally <30 sec. Do not let parts air-dry. Brightener drag-in degrades chrome bath. Thorough rinsing critical.

**GOLD PLATE** (accent `#E8A020`):
Nickel contamination causes dull deposits and co-deposition. Rinse very thoroughly. DI water for final rinse recommended. Some processes require mild activation dip after Ni rinse.

**LACQUER / ANTI-TARNISH** (accent `#27AE60`):
Surface must be completely free of salts and water spots. Final DI rinse essential. Hot-air dry before lacquer. Any residue under lacquer causes blistering.

**HE BAKE (HIGH-STRENGTH STEEL)** (accent `#E05C5C`):
Parts requiring HE bake (>31 HRC) must reach oven within 4 hours of plating (ASTM B850). Rinse, dry, transfer immediately. 375 +/- 25 F for 3--24 hours.

## Phase 6 -- Nickel Passivation + Timing

Y: 21.0" to 27.0". Two-column layout.

**Left -- Nickel Passivation Risk (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
Fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 1pt `#E05C5C`.
- Title: `NICKEL PASSIVATES IN AIR` `#E05C5C`
- Freshly plated nickel begins to passivate within minutes of air exposure. Passive film prevents adhesion of chrome, gold, or other coatings.
- For chrome: move in under 30 seconds. Do not let parts sit in air.
- If delay unavoidable: mild acid re-activation dip (1--5% H2SO4, 10--30 sec) can restore surface.

**Right -- Transfer Timing (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**
Fill `#1E2435`, accent `#E8A020`. Title: `TRANSFER TIMING`.

| Destination | Max Delay | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chrome | < 30 sec | Nickel passivates fast |
| Gold | < 2 min | Less critical but time-sensitive |
| Lacquer | DI rinse + dry first | Surface must be completely dry |
| HE bake oven | < 4 hours total | Per ASTM B850 |
| Storage (bright Ni final) | Apply anti-tarnish first | Nickel tarnishes in humid air |

## Phase 7 -- Failure Modes + Safety

Y: 27.0" to 32.5". Two-column layout.

**Left -- 4 Failure Modes (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**

| Problem | Cause | Downstream Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Brightener carry-over to chrome | Insufficient rinse volume | Chrome bath degradation, dull chrome |
| Nickel salt staining | Parts air-dried with nickel solution | White/green salt deposits under topcoat |
| Passivation before chrome | Delay between rinse and chrome | Chrome peeling, poor adhesion |
| HE bake missed or delayed | Parts queued too long after plating | Hydrogen embrittlement failure in service |

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**
Coral-tinted glass callout.
- Nickel drag-out rinse is acidic (pH 3--5). Avoid skin contact.
- Nickel salts cause dermatitis -- gloves required when handling parts.
- IARC Group 1 carcinogen (inhalable nickel). Mist extraction at rinse tanks near heated baths.
- Waste rinse water contains dissolved nickel -- route to treatment, not drain.
