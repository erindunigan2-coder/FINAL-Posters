# Poster #121 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Gold -- Pre-Activation

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 2 of 8. The rinse between cleaning and activation. Same fundamental purpose as Poster #113 (Tin) -- remove alkaline cleaner residue before the acid dip. The gold-specific twist: this rinse must be exceptionally thorough because any drag-in that eventually reaches the gold bath is amplified by the bath's extreme contamination sensitivity.

### ZONE 5 -- Drag-In Chain

**Section label:** `THE CONTAMINATION CHAIN -- RINSE TO GOLD BATH` -- Y: 20.7".

**BLOCK E -- Four-Link Chain Diagram**

Y: 21.3" to 26.0". Four connected boxes in a left-to-right chain with arrows between them.

| Link | Label | Color | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CLEANER | `#2EC4B6` | Alkaline cleaner on parts: NaOH, surfactants, chelators |
| 2 | BAD RINSE | `#E8A020` | Cleaner not fully removed. Residue carried to acid dip. |
| 3 | ACID DIP | `#E8A020` | Neutralized acid, dissolved metals from cleaner, pH buffered |

Arrows between links: 3 pt `#3A4055`, right-pointing.

## Design Notes

The drag-in chain diagram is the unique value of this poster. It connects the dots between a "harmless" rinse failure at Stage 2 and a gold bath contamination event at Stage 5. The cost callout in the final chain link ($100s--$1000s) makes the economic argument visceral. Gold plating operators need to understand that their rinse discipline directly affects the most expensive bath in the shop.
