## Section 1 — Watson Flag Status

**Status: NONE — all data production-ready.**

All values in this document trace directly to Watson's Research Brief v2. Watson sourced data from: the vault EN troubleshooting guide, domain expertise grounded in MacDermid Enthone Niklad/Enplate, Atotech Nichem, NASF/AESF Metal Finishing Guidebook, Products Finishing, ASTM B733, IPC-4552B, AMS 2404/2405, and MIL-C-26074.

Watson's brief noted that Gemini quota was exhausted during the research session, with data verified against vault records and domain expertise. All values used on this poster are internal-source confirmed. No external-source verification flags are raised because the data is consistent with well-established industry standards and the vault troubleshooting guide.

**Design decisions made during layout development:**

- **Heat Treatment Hardness Jump (Element 6 in the brief):** Included as a compact two-row data callout rather than a full bar chart. Space analysis confirms the bar chart approach would require approximately 15% of poster height to render at readable scale — that height is better allocated to the P% Spectrum table, which is the poster's primary data payload. The hardness jump is still communicated via a dedicated callout within the P% table's column headers.

- **Autocatalytic Reaction (Element 9 in the brief):** Included as a compact chemistry callout box (Block B). The reaction equation is central to understanding what makes EN unique and earns its space at minimal cost in real estate.

- **Corrosion Shield Icons (Watson Visual Concept 6):** Consolidated into the P% Spectrum table as a dedicated "Salt Spray" row with shield icons as secondary indicators. Three standalone shield icons would consume significant layout space for information already conveyed in the table. Efficiency wins.

- **Column color assignments for P% Spectrum table:**
  - Low-P: **Teal** `#2EC4B6` — rationale: Teal is the cool/precision/structural color in the palette; Low-P's primary use in electronics and precision contacts (low, controlled, refined) maps well to cool precision. Secondary rationale: Low-P baths operate at alkaline pH — teal reads chemically "cleaner."
  - Mid-P: **Amber** `#E8A020` — rationale: Amber is the workhorse warning-adjacent accent, but here it signals energy and activity. Mid-P is the workhorse of the industry, the most widely deployed class. Amber = heat, activity, production. Mid-P also runs at the highest temperature (85–91°C) — the hottest bath gets the hot color.
  - High-P: **Coral** `#E05C5C` — rationale: Coral marks the extreme end of the spectrum. High-P's corrosion resistance, chemical resistance, and non-magnetic behavior are all premium/extreme properties. Coral's intensity communicates this. Also consistent with its use elsewhere in the series for "alert/critical" — High-P requires the most precise bath control.

**What is an MTO? (below gauge — Inter Regular, 17–18 pt, `#F0EDE8` Dark / `#1A1F2E` Light):**

> 1 MTO (metal turnover) = the bath has deposited an amount of nickel equal to its original Ni²⁺ charge. Fresh bath: 0 MTO. As MTOs accumulate, orthophosphite builds up, deposit quality declines, and corrosion resistance drops.

**Closing note (Inter Medium, 16–17 pt, `#2EC4B6` Dark / `#1A8C82` Light, with 2 pt Teal left accent rule):**

> Unlike electrolytic baths, EN baths have a finite life — track your MTOs or accept the consequences in deposit quality.

**Discard threshold reminder (JetBrains Mono Regular, 16 pt, `#E05C5C`, below closing note):**

> Discard at: > 120 g/L orthophosphite OR 8–10 MTO — whichever comes first.

The secondary indicators (zone labels in text, tick labels, footnotes) ensure that information conveyed by the green/amber/coral colors is also conveyed in writing.

### Illustration 4.1 — Uniform Coating Cross-Section (Block C)

**Purpose:** The single most impactful visual differentiator for EN. Immediately answers the question "why bother with EN?"

**Build approach:** Canva-native layered rectangles. No external tool required.

**Canvas dimensions (within Zone 2 at 24×36"):**
- Total illustration zone height: ~6.1" (minus 0.5" section label above)
- Available illustration height: ~5.0"
- Left panel width: ~45% of artboard safe-zone width
- Right panel width: ~45% of artboard safe-zone width
- Center divider: 2 pt rule, `#3A4055`, 80% of illustration height

**Part profile construction (identical in both panels):**
- Substrate shape: an inverted U-channel (like a rectangular bracket). This shape has an external top surface, two external vertical walls, and an internal recess at the bottom. This geometry maximally demonstrates the HCD/LCD differential.
- Substrate fill: `#3A4055` Mid Slate (reads as steel)
- Substrate outline: `#F0EDE8` at 20% opacity, 1 pt stroke

**Left panel — Electrolytic Nickel deposit rectangles:**
- Top external surface deposit: `#C8D0D8` Bright Silver rectangle, height = 5 units (exaggerated thick)
- Outer corner edges: `#C8D0D8` rectangles at each outer corner, height = 5 units, width = 5 units (builds up at corners)
- Internal recess floor deposit: `#C8D0D8` rectangle, height = 1 unit (thin, barely visible)
- Internal recess walls (lower portions): `#C8D0D8` rectangle, height = 1 unit (thin)
- Ratio: 5:1 thick-to-thin — visually obvious, unmistakable

**Right panel — Electroless Nickel deposit rectangles:**
- All surfaces: `#C8D0D8` Bright Silver rectangles of identical height = 2 units
- Top, sides, internal floor, internal walls — all equal
- The sameness is the point

**Label arrow styling:**
- Arrows: Canva line elements with arrowhead, stroke `#E05C5C` Coral (left panel) / `#2EC4B6` Teal (right panel), 1.5 pt
- Arrow tips point to the deposit rectangle edge
- Labels: JetBrains Mono Regular, 14–16 pt

**Central callout banner:**
- Position: horizontally centered across both panels and the divider
- Vertically: centered at approximately 60% of the illustration height
- Box: `#1E2435` fill, `#2EC4B6` border 1 pt, corner radius 6 pt
- Padding: 8 pt vertical / 14 pt horizontal
- Box height approximately 28–30 pt text height + padding

**Canva layer order (bottom to top within illustration group):**
1. Left panel substrate shape (`#3A4055`)
2. Left panel deposit rectangles (`#C8D0D8`) — thick/thin
3. Left panel labels and arrows (Coral)
4. Left panel title text

7. Right panel labels and arrows (Teal)

### Illustration 4.2 — MTO Gauge (Block F)

**Purpose:** Communicates the finite life of an EN bath — a concept completely foreign to electrolytic plating operators.

**Build approach:** Canva-native. Three adjacent rectangles masked to a single rounded rectangle. Tick marks are thin Canva line elements.

**Gauge dimensions (within Zone 4B right column at 24×36"):**
- Gauge width: full column width (approximately 10.1" minus 0.5" margins = ~9.1")
- Gauge height: ~1.5"
- Corner radius on outer mask: 8 pt

**Construction:**
1. Draw outer rounded rectangle (`#3A4055` Mid Slate border, 2 pt, no fill, corner radius 8 pt)
2. Inside, draw three adjacent filled rectangles:
   - Green zone (60% width): `#27AE60` Emerald
   - Amber zone (20% width): `#E8A020` Amber
   - Coral zone (20% width): `#E05C5C` Coral
3. Apply the outer rounded rectangle as a mask (Canva: group + clip mask) so corners are rounded uniformly
4. Add vertical boundary lines at the Green/Amber and Amber/Coral junctions: `#1A1F2E`, 2 pt stroke, full gauge height
5. Add tick mark labels below the gauge (not inside): JetBrains Mono Regular, 16 pt, `#F0EDE8`
6. Add zone labels inside the bar: Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 16–18 pt; use `#1A1F2E` text for Green and Amber zones (light background), `#F0EDE8` text for Coral zone (dark background)

**Light edition:** Global Color remap will shift the three fill colors to their Light edition equivalents. The contrast check must be re-run for text inside the Amber zone on Light edition — Amber `#C8860A` may be close to `#1A1F2E` text; verify at workup stage.

## Section 5 — Light Edition Remap Notes

The Light edition is produced by remapping Global Colors per the locked Series Design Standards. No layout changes. No content changes. The following specific checks are required at the Canva workup stage:

| Element | Dark | Light | Check Needed |
|---------|------|-------|-------------|
| P% table — Low-P header | `#1A1F2E` text on `#2EC4B6` Teal | `#1A1F2E` text on `#1A8C82` Teal Dark | Verify contrast at Teal Dark: #1A1F2E on #1A8C82 — should pass AAA |
| P% table — Mid-P header | `#1A1F2E` text on `#E8A020` Amber | `#1A1F2E` text on `#C8860A` Amber Dark | Verify contrast: #1A1F2E on #C8860A — check ratio; Amber Dark may be marginal |
| P% table — High-P header | `#F0EDE8` text on `#E05C5C` Coral | `#F0EDE8` text on `#B83E3E` Coral Dark | `#F0EDE8` on dark coral should improve in Light edition |
| MTO gauge — Amber zone label | `#1A1F2E` text on `#E8A020` | `#1A1F2E` text on `#C8860A` | Check as above |
| Central callout banner (Block C) | `#F0EDE8` on `#1E2435` | `#1A1F2E` on `#ECEEF4` | Standard inversion — no concern |
