### ZONE 4 -- Conductivity + Surfactant Risk

**Section label:** `MONITORING YOUR RINSE -- WITH A SULFAMATE TWIST` -- Y: 15.2".

**BLOCK D -- Conductivity Monitoring**

Same gauge as Poster #57:
- Green: `< 500 microS/cm` -- `GOOD`
- Yellow: `500--1000 microS/cm` -- `MARGINAL`
- Red: `> 1000 microS/cm` -- `FAIL`

**BLOCK E -- Surfactant Carry-Over Risk**

Y: 18.5" to 20.8". Full-width callout.
- Rounded rect, fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 1 pt `#E05C5C`, radius 8
- Title: `SURFACTANT CARRY-OVER -- THE HIDDEN ORGANIC SOURCE` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`
- Body (Inter Medium 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 155%):

> Alkaline cleaners contain surfactants (detergents). If rinsing is inadequate, these surfactants travel through the activation step and into the sulfamate bath as organic contaminants.
>
> In a Watts bath, this causes pitting. In a sulfamate bath, this spikes internal stress -- the exact thing you chose sulfamate to avoid.
>
> Conductivity meters catch ionic residues but NOT surfactants. For sulfamate lines, supplement conductivity checks with periodic total organic carbon (TOC) testing of rinse water, or simply rinse more aggressively than you think necessary.

### ZONE 5 -- Rinse Types + Failure Modes

**Two-column layout (Y: 21.2" to 26.8"):**

**Left -- Rinse Type Comparison (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Same four-type table as Poster #57 but with sulfamate recommendation:

| Type | Efficiency | Water Use | Sulfamate Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single overflow | Low | High | Minimum acceptable |
| Double overflow | Moderate | Moderate | Good for moderate volume |
| Counterflow (2-stage) | High | Low | RECOMMENDED for sulfamate lines |
| Spray rinse | Very high | Very low | Excellent if equipment available |

**Right -- 4 Failure Modes (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**

| Problem | Cause | Downstream Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Alkaline drag-in to acid | Insufficient rinse | Acid bath weakened; staining |
| Surfactant carry-through | Cleaner not rinsed completely | Organic contamination --> stress in sulfamate |
| Contamination buildup | No overflow; stagnant rinse | Progressive organic loading |
| Cross-contamination | Shared rinse between processes | Chemical reactions on surface |

### ZONE 6 -- Practical Tips + Safety

**Left -- Tips (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**

> - For sulfamate lines, double the rinse time you would use for Watts. The cost of over-rinsing is water. The cost of under-rinsing is a stressed deposit.
> - Drain parts over the process tank for 5--10 seconds.
> - Dip racks 3--4 times minimum.
> - If you can smell cleaner in the rinse tank, you are not overflowing fast enough.
> - Consider a dedicated rinse system for your sulfamate line -- do not share with a Watts line if possible.

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**

Same as Poster #57:
> - Rinse water after alkaline cleaning is mildly caustic.
> - Overflow water routed to waste treatment.
> - Wet floors: slip hazard.

## Design Notes

This poster is 80% identical to Poster #57 (Watts Pre-Activation Rinse). The 20% difference -- the surfactant carry-over risk section -- is what justifies the separate poster. Sulfamate users need to see this message in context, not buried in a Watts poster with a footnote. The surfactant callout should be visually prominent and slightly alarming. The recommendation to double rinse time vs. Watts is a practical, actionable takeaway.
