## Phase 6 -- Case Depth Reference + Metallurgical Essentials

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section label: `CASE DEPTH VS. TIME -- THE CARBURIZER'S REFERENCE`.

**Left -- ECD Table (W: 11.0"):** `ECD AT 1700 F (925 C)` in Emerald. Left accent `#27AE60`.

| ECD (inch) | ECD (mm) | Total Cycle | TCD (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.020 | 0.5 | 2--3 hr | 0.030--0.035" |
| 0.030 | 0.75 | 3--5 hr | 0.045--0.055" |
| 0.040 | 1.0 | 5--7 hr | 0.060--0.070" |
| 0.060 | 1.5 | 8--12 hr | 0.085--0.100" |
| 0.080 | 2.0 | 14--20 hr | 0.110--0.130" |
| 0.100 | 2.5 | 20--28 hr | 0.140--0.160" |

Bottom note in Amber: `Rule of thumb: case depth is proportional to sqrt(time). Double the depth = 4x the time.`

**Right -- Metallurgical Essentials (W: 11.5"):** Left accent `#E8A020`.

- Austenite dissolves up to ~2.1% C (at 2098 F) vs. ferrite max ~0.02% C -- this is WHY we heat
- Surface carbon target: 0.75--0.95% C. Too high (>1.0%) = carbide network = REJECT
- Cp control: O2 probe + CO2 IR analyzer. Cross-check with shim stock method
- Best steels: 8620, 9310, 4320, 5120 (0.15--0.25% base carbon, good hardenability)
- ECD defined as depth to 50 HRC (~0.40% C for most carburizing steels)
- TCD = 1.3--1.6x ECD (total to core carbon)

**Callout bar (Y: 27.8"):** Pill shape, Emerald tint. `Carbon diffusion follows Fick's second law -- temperature is the throttle, time is the dial.`

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting Quick Hits + Footer

**Troubleshooting (Y: 28.5"--32.5"):** Section label: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON FAILURES`. Four cards, left accent `#E05C5C`.

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| SOFT SPOTS | Part nesting; oil/grease residue blocking carbon absorption | Improve spacing; thorough cleaning |
| CARBIDE NETWORK | Cp too high (>1.1%) during boost; inadequate diffuse cycle | Reduce boost Cp; extend diffuse time |
| EXCESSIVE DISTORTION | Non-uniform quench; parts not fixtured properly | Stress relieve pre-carb; press quench for gears |
| HIGH RETAINED AUSTENITE | Surface C too high (>1.0%); quench not severe enough | Control Cp; sub-zero treat (-100 to -120 F) |
