## Phase 4 -- Fixturing Methods (HERO)

Y: 4.2" to 14.5". Section label: `THREE FIXTURING APPROACHES`.

Three panels (W 7.33", H 6.5") with diagram placeholders:

| Panel | Method | Accent | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BETWEEN CENTERS | `#2EC4B6` | Part between tailstock and headstock (like a lathe). Part rotates. Non-magnetic centers. | Shafts, axles, pins. Most common for cylindrical. |
| 2 | CHUCK / COLLET | `#E8A020` | Part gripped in chuck/collet. Brass or non-magnetic SS jaws. Can rotate or stationary for single-shot. | Short parts, gears, flanges. |
| 3 | CNC SCANNING | `#27AE60` | Part held stationary (or rotating); coil travels on CNC carriage. Trailing spray quench follows. Scan speed: 0.1--2.0 in/sec. | Long shafts, rolls, bars. |

Below -- Scanning vs. Single-Shot strip:

**SINGLE-SHOT (Amber):** Entire area heated simultaneously. No coil movement. Fastest cycle. High power required. Best for localized areas (gear teeth, bearing seats).

**PROGRESSIVE SCAN (Teal):** Coil moves along part with trailing quench. Lower instantaneous power. Uniform case along length. Best for long surfaces (shafts).

## Phase 5 -- Coupling Distance

Y: 14.5" to 22.0". Section label: `COIL-TO-PART GAP -- THE CRITICAL DIMENSION`.

**Left -- Visual Gap Gauge:**

| Range | Label | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.030 in | TOO CLOSE (`#E05C5C`) | Arcing risk; coil damage; uneven heating |
| 0.040--0.060 in | OPTIMAL small parts (`#27AE60`) | Maximum efficiency; uniform heating |
| 0.060--0.125 in | OPTIMAL large parts (`#27AE60`) | Standard production range |
| 0.125--0.250 in | ACCEPTABLE (`#E8A020`) | Reduced efficiency; may need higher power |
| > 0.250 in | TOO FAR (`#E05C5C`) | Severe efficiency loss; non-uniform |

**Right -- Efficiency Relationship (Amber):**

0.040" gap = ~90% | 0.080" = ~75% | 0.125" = ~60% | 0.250" = ~35%. Efficiency drops as SQUARE of distance. Doubling gap = roughly 1/4 efficiency. A 0.010" change causes measurable heating variation. VERIFY GAP WITH FEELER GAUGES ON SETUP.

## Phase 6 -- Rotation + Material Selection

Y: 22.0" to 32.5".

**Left -- Part Rotation:**

| Application | Speed (RPM) | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Shafts (general) | 60--120 | Circumferential uniformity |
| Shafts (precision) | 120--200 | Tighter pattern control |
| Gears (encircling) | 0 (stationary) | Coil surrounds entire profile |
| Gears (tooth-by-tooth) | Indexed | One tooth at a time |
| Large cylinders | 30--60 | Slower for mass |

Note: Non-rotating parts have "hot side" and "cold side" -- non-uniform case. In `#E8A020`.

**Right -- Fixture Materials (Coral accent):**

`NON-MAGNETIC MATERIALS ONLY`.

| Material | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 300-series SS (304, 316) | Centers, chucks, fixtures | Austenitic = non-magnetic; standard |
| Brass / bronze | Centers, bushings | Excellent; non-magnetic; good wear |
| Ceramic | Insulating pads | Electrical isolation |
| UHMW / nylon | Guides, supports | Low-temp zones only |

Warning: `NEVER use carbon steel, tool steel, or 400-series SS. They are ferromagnetic and WILL heat up -- damaging fixture and stealing power from workpiece.`

Wear note: `Fixture wear changes coupling gap. Inspect regularly. Worn center = inconsistent case depth.` In `#2EC4B6`.
