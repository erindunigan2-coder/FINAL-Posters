## Phase 6 -- Nickel Passivation Science

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `WHY SPEED MATTERS -- NICKEL PASSIVATION`.

Full-width panel, accent `#E05C5C`. Content:

**What happens when nickel sits in air:**
- `0--10 sec: minimal oxide, chrome will adhere` -- `#27AE60`
- `10--30 sec: thin oxide forming, chrome adhesion still acceptable` -- `#E8A020`
- `30--60 sec: significant passive film, adhesion risk increases` -- `#E05C5C`
- `> 60 sec: heavy passivation, chrome WILL peel` -- `#E05C5C` bold

**Contributing factors:** Warm parts passivate faster | High humidity accelerates oxide | Parts that dry are almost guaranteed to fail | Rack density matters -- move full racks quickly.

**The fix if nickel passivates:** Mild acid dip (1--5% H2SO4 or 1--3% HCl, ambient, 10--30 sec) reactivates the surface. Or: reverse current activation in the chrome bath itself.

## Phase 7 -- Failure Modes

Y: 26.5" to 32.5". Section: `WHAT GOES WRONG -- 4 ADHESION FAILURES`.

2x2 grid, each card W: 11.0", H: 2.3", fill `#1E2435`, accent in defect color:

| Defect | Color | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| CHROME PEELING | `#E05C5C` | Nickel passivated before chrome entry | Reduce transfer time; acid dip activation |
| BLISTERING | `#E05C5C` | Moisture trapped under chrome | Ensure parts do not dry; check rack contact |
| HAZY CHROME | `#E8A020` | Partial passivation -- uneven adhesion | Tighten transfer window; standardize timing |
| FLAKING AT EDGES | `#E8A020` | High-CD areas most sensitive to adhesion loss | Improve activation; check racking geometry |
