# Poster #103 -- Construction Workup
## Copper Plating (Alkaline) -- Process Flow

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

This is the cluster overview poster for EP-10: Copper Plating (Alkaline Non-Cyanide). Alkaline non-cyanide copper is the modern, cyanide-free alternative to the traditional cyanide copper strike. It uses complexants (HEDP, citrate, or tartrate-based proprietary systems) to hold copper in solution at alkaline pH (8--11), preventing the immersion deposition that makes acid copper unusable on active metals. It is used primarily as a strike layer before acid copper plating on steel, zinc die castings, and ABS plastic -- the same substrates that previously required cyanide copper.

The deposit quality is lower than acid copper (matte to semi-bright) and cathode efficiency is lower (40--70%), but the bath serves a critical purpose: getting an adherent, non-immersion copper layer onto difficult substrates WITHOUT cyanide.

## Design Notes

This poster answers the question every modern plating shop asks: "Can we eliminate cyanide from our copper strike line?" The answer is yes -- alkaline non-cyanide copper does the same job as cyanide copper strike on most substrates. The comparison callout makes the trade-offs explicit: non-cyanide is safer and simpler to waste-treat, but cyanide still gives better adhesion on the most difficult substrates (zinc die castings in particular).

Critical technical notes:
- This is NOT cyanide copper. Watson's EP-10 research covers cyanide copper. Drew's instructions specify this poster covers the HEDP/citrate/tartrate non-cyanide alternative. All chemistry and parameters reflect non-cyanide systems.
- The comparison zone includes cyanide copper for reference but the poster content is 100% non-cyanide focused.
- Zinc die cast activation is the most failure-prone step -- over-etching the zinc substrate is the #1 cause of adhesion failure.
