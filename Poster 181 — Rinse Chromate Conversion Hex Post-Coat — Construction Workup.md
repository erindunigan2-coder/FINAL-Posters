# Poster #181 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Chromate Conversion (Hex) -- Post-Coat

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 7. This rinse is unlike any other in the conversion coating poster library. Three absolute rules: (1) COLD WATER ONLY -- never hot water, never warm water, ambient to 77 F maximum. (2) BRIEF -- 15--30 seconds, do not over-rinse. (3) GENTLE -- the fresh chromate gel is soft and easily damaged.

Hot water destroys the fresh gel coating. It drives off the Cr6+ self-healing component, degrades corrosion resistance, and can cause the coating to lose its characteristic gold color. This is the most temperature-sensitive rinse in any conversion coating process.

|---|---|---|

## Design Notes

This is the most distinctive rinse poster in the entire poster library. Every other rinse poster says "add clean water at the right temperature." This one says "if you use hot water, you have destroyed an invisible property and the part will fail in testing despite looking perfect." The temperature danger scale in the hero zone makes this visually unmistakable. The cold vs. hot rinse comparison table -- where appearance is "may appear similar (deceptive)" but salt spray drops from 336+ to <48 hours -- is the data that sticks.

The 24-hour cure period is operationally significant and often violated in production environments. Having it on the wall, in big type, backed by a damage source table, gives quality managers a visual tool to enforce the rule.
