## Phase 4 -- Free Iron Concept Hero

Y: 2.9" to 15.5". Section: `FREE IRON -- THE PROBLEM PASSIVATION SOLVES`.

### Step 5 -- Three concept panels (Y: 3.8" to 8.5")

Full-width rounded rect with three sub-panels:

**Panel 1 -- IDEAL: CLEAN SURFACE** (`#27AE60`): Cr-rich alloy (11--30% Cr) in air forms Cr2O3 passive film (1--5 nm). Invisible. Self-healing: scratched, Cr reacts with O2 and reforms in seconds.

**Panel 2 -- REALITY: FREE IRON ON SURFACE** (`#E05C5C`): Manufacturing deposits metallic iron ON surface: machining (carbon steel tools), grinding (iron-laden abrasives), handling (steel fixtures). These particles BLOCK passive film. When wet, free iron rusts -- "rust on stainless."

**Panel 3 -- AFTER PASSIVATION** (`#27AE60`): Passivation acid dissolves only free iron (Cr and Ni resist). With iron removed, Cr-enriched surface reacts with O2 to form fresh Cr2O3 passive film. Corrosion resistance: RESTORED.

## Phase 5 -- The Passive Film Explained

Y: 15.5" to 22.0". Section: `THE PASSIVE FILM -- Cr2O3`.

**Left -- WHAT IS IT?** (`#27AE60`): Cr2O3-rich amorphous oxide, 1--5 nm thick (thinner than a virus), invisible, forms spontaneously in air. Provides ALL corrosion resistance of stainless steel. Minimum Cr ~10.5% for "stainless" designation.

**Right -- HOW DOES IT PROTECT?** (`#E8A020`): 1. BARRIER: chemically inert. 2. SELF-HEALING: scratched Cr reforms in seconds (if surface free of contamination). 3. SELECTIVE: Cr oxidizes preferentially over Fe. PASSIVATION ENRICHES surface in Cr by removing diluting iron.

---

## Phase 6 -- Free Iron vs. Alloyed Iron

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section: `FREE IRON vs. ALLOYED IRON -- THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION`.

**Left -- ALLOYED IRON** (`#27AE60`): Base metal of stainless (60--80% by weight). Atomically bonded in crystal lattice with Cr, Ni, Mo. Participates in passive film. Passivation does NOT remove it -- acid preferentially attacks free (unalloyed) iron.

**Right -- FREE IRON** (`#E05C5C`): Foreign contamination deposited ON surface. NOT part of alloy. Has no Cr to protect it. Rusts when wet. Creates galvanic cells. BLOCKS passive film beneath it. Passivation REMOVES free iron -- its entire purpose.

## Phase 7 -- Detection Methods

Y: 28.5" to 32.5". Section: `FREE IRON DETECTION -- 4 METHODS`.

| # | X | Method | How It Works | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5" | COPPER SULFATE TEST | CuSO4/H2SO4 immersion 6 min; Cu deposits on free iron | Pink/red = FAIL; no color = PASS |
| 2 | 6.33" | FERROXYL TEST | K3Fe(CN)6 + HNO3 solution; Turnbull's blue on iron | Blue spots = free iron present |
| 3 | 12.16" | WATER IMMERSION | Clean water 24 hr | Rust spots = free iron |
| 4 | 18.0" | HIGH HUMIDITY | 95% RH, 95 F, 24 hr | Rust spots = free iron (most sensitive) |
