## Phase 5 -- Drag-In to Tin Bath

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `WHAT ACID DRAG-IN DOES TO YOUR TIN BATH`.

Full-width panel, three columns:

| Column | Header | Color | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | `pH DROP` | `#E8A020` | Excess acid lowers pH below operating range. Low pH = rough, powdery tin. Accelerates Sn2+ oxidation to Sn4+. |
| Center | `METAL CONTAMINATION` | `#E05C5C` | Dissolved copper, iron, or zinc from activation acid. Iron >20 ppm darkens deposit. Copper causes immersion deposits. |
| Right | `CHEMISTRY WASTE` | `#E05C5C` | Every ounce of acid carried in must be compensated. Drag-in is money down the drain. |

Bottom rule (Emerald): `Good rinsing extends bath life. Poor rinsing shortens it.`

## Phase 6 -- Chloride Warning

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `CHLORIDE CONTAMINATION -- THE HIDDEN THREAT`.

Full-width Coral-tinted panel (border 2pt `#E05C5C`). Title: `HCl ACTIVATION + TIN BATH = CHLORIDE RISK`.

**Left column:** If HCl was used for steel activation: Cl- ions drag into rinse then tin bath / Attacks tin anodes / Causes pitting and hazy deposits / Threshold: < 10 ppm Cl- in acid sulfate bath.

**Right column -- Prevention:** Use H2SO4 instead of HCl when possible / Extra rinse stage if HCl required / Double-rinse with counterflow cascade / Monitor conductivity before tin tank / Test tin bath for chloride.

Bottom note: `If you run HCl activation and acid sulfate tin on the same line, this rinse is the firewall. Treat it that way.`
