## Phase 6 -- Why Austempering? Comparison

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section label: `WHY AUSTEMPERING? -- BAINITE VS. MARTENSITE`.

**Left -- Austempering (Bainite)** (accent `#27AE60`): Final structure: bainite | Hardness: 30--55 HRC | Toughness: EXCELLENT | Distortion: 60--90% less | Temper: NO | Section limit: ~0.5" plain carbon, 1.0--1.5" alloy | Best for: springs, clips, ADI.
Highlight: `Same hardness as tempered martensite -- but tougher, more ductile, and less distortion`.

**Right -- Conventional Q&T (Martensite)** (accent `#E8A020`): Final structure: tempered martensite | Hardness: 28--63 HRC | Toughness: good but less | Distortion: baseline (100%) | Temper: YES mandatory | Section limit: none with proper hardenability | Best for: broad range, large sections, max hardness.

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting + Footer

**Troubleshooting** (Y: 28.5" to 32.5"). Four cards.

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| PEARLITE IN MICRO | Transfer too slow; section too thick | Higher-alloy steel; rapid transfer; increase agitation |
| RETAINED AUSTENITE | Hold time too short | Extend isothermal hold; verify by metallography |
| LOW HARDNESS | Salt bath temp too high; incomplete austenitization | Lower salt temp; increase austenitizing time |
| CRACKING (RARE) | Thermal shock on salt entry | Pre-heat parts; control immersion rate |
