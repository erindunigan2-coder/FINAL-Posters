# Poster #117 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Tin -- Post-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 8. The rinse after tin plating and before post-treatment. This rinse has a specific urgency that other post-plate rinses lack: tin surfaces oxidize rapidly in air, and residual acid on the surface accelerates the formation of stannic oxide (white haze). Speed matters here more than in most rinse stages. The poster also covers drag-out recovery -- tin chemistry is not cheap, and capturing drag-out from the plating bath saves money.

### ZONE 4 -- Drag-Out Recovery

**Section label:** `DRAG-OUT RECOVERY -- SAVE YOUR TIN` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Recovery Panel**

Y: 15.3" to 20.0". Full-width rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`.

**Three-column layout:**

| Column | Header Color | Header | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | `#E8A020` | `THE COST` | Tin chemistry is expensive. Every rack or barrel carries 0.1--0.5 mL/sq ft of plating solution out of the tank. At $20--50/gal for tin chemistry, drag-out adds up fast. |
| Center | `#27AE60` | `THE RECOVERY` | First rinse tank (still or low-flow) captures concentrated drag-out. Periodically return this rinse water to the plating bath. Replenishes tin and acid. |
| Right | `#2EC4B6` | `THE METHOD` | Stagnant recovery rinse -> test for Sn content -> add back to bath if clean. Monitor for contamination before return. Use DI water for recovery rinse. |

Bottom rule: `A drag-out recovery rinse pays for itself in weeks on a busy tin line.` Inter Medium 14 pt `#E8A020`

### ZONE 5 -- White Haze Prevention

**Section label:** `WHITE HAZE -- THE POST-PLATE RINSE DEFECT` -- Y: 20.7".

**BLOCK E -- Haze Prevention Panel**

Y: 21.3" to 26.0".

**Two-column layout:**

**Left -- What Is It:**
- Rounded rect, X: 0.5", W: 11.0", H: 4.5", fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`
- Title: `WHITE HAZE = STANNIC OXIDE` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`

Content:
- `Freshly plated tin + air + residual acid = Sn4+ oxide film`
- `Appears as white, milky haze on surface`
- `Destroys solderability`
- `Cannot be removed without stripping and replating`
- `Forms in as little as 30--60 seconds of air exposure with acid residue`

**Right -- How to Prevent It:**
- Rounded rect, X: 12.0", W: 11.5", H: 4.5", fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#27AE60`
- Title: `PREVENTION` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#27AE60`

Content:
- `Transfer from plating to rinse in < 30 seconds`
- `Immerse completely -- no partial dipping`
- `Use flowing rinse to displace acid quickly`
- `Move to anti-tarnish dip immediately after rinse`
- `Never leave tinned parts sitting in air between stages`
- `DI water rinse reduces mineral spotting`

Bottom note: `White haze is a speed problem, not a chemistry problem. Move faster.` Inter Medium 14 pt `#E8A020`

## Design Notes

This is the most time-critical rinse poster in the series. The timer graphic and "IMMEDIATE TRANSFER" arrow are the visual anchors. White haze is a uniquely tin-specific defect -- no other plating process has this exact problem at this stage. The drag-out recovery section adds economic value: tin chemistry is expensive enough that recovery pays for itself.
