## Phase 4 -- Four Fixturing Methods (HERO)

Y: 4.2" to 14.5". Section label: `FOUR FIXTURING APPROACHES -- SIMPLE TO SOPHISTICATED`. 2x2 grid of method panels.

| Method | Accent | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| ROTARY TABLE | `#2EC4B6` | Part on rotary table; 30--120 RPM | Cylindrical parts: shafts, rolls, bushings |
| LINEAR SLIDE | `#E8A020` | Flame head or part on carriage; CNC or manual | Flat surfaces: lathe ways, rails, gear teeth |
| BETWEEN CENTERS | `#27AE60` | Headstock/tailstock; rotates while flame traverses | Long shafts, axles, rolls |
| MANUAL FREEHAND | `#E8A020` | Operator holds torch; controls all variables manually | Repair, one-off, field work, irregular geometries |

Freehand callout: `FLAME HARDENING CAN BE DONE FREEHAND -- the only surface hardening process that can be performed with standard welding equipment and no special fixtures.`

## Phase 6 -- Flame Distance + Manual vs. Automated

**Left -- Flame-to-Part Distance** (horizontal bar gauge):
< 0.125 in = TOO CLOSE (Coral) | 0.25--0.50 in = OPTIMAL small (Emerald) | 0.50--0.75 in = OPTIMAL large (Emerald) | 0.75--1.5 in = ACCEPTABLE (Amber) | > 1.5 in = TOO FAR (Coral).

**Right -- Manual vs. Automated:**
Manual: no equipment investment, max flexibility, field-portable; tolerance +/- 0.030--0.060 in; best for repair, prototypes.
Automated: repeatable, consistent traverse, documented; tolerance +/- 0.020--0.030 in; best for production, spec-controlled work.
