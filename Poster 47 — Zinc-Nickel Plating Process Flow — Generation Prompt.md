## Phase 6 -- Why Zinc-Nickel? Comparison

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section: `WHY ZINC-NICKEL? -- Zn-Ni VS. PLAIN ZINC`.

**Left -- Zinc-Nickel Alloy (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
Accent `#27AE60`. Title: `ZINC-NICKEL ALLOY (12--16% Ni)` / Subtitle: `The Corrosion Protection Champion`.

Properties:
- Salt spray (red rust): 720--1,000+ hours
- Salt spray (white rust): 200--500 hours
- Corrosion mechanism: Sacrificial + barrier (dual protection)
- Hardness: 350--450 HV (much harder than Zn)
- Temperature resistance: Stable to 250 F (120 C)
- Passivation: Trivalent only (RoHS compliant)
- Typical applications: Automotive fasteners, brake parts, underbody
- Replaced: Cadmium plating in most automotive specs
- Bath complexity: Higher -- alloy ratio monitoring required
- Cost: 2--3x plain zinc (justified by performance)

Highlight: `5--10x the salt spray of plain zinc. The spec-driven choice when failure is not an option.`

**Right -- Plain Zinc (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
Accent `#E8A020`. Title: `PLAIN ZINC (ACID OR ALKALINE)` / Subtitle: `The Baseline Standard`.

Properties:
- Salt spray (red rust): 72--200 hours (with tri + sealer)
- Salt spray (white rust): 24--96 hours
- Corrosion mechanism: Sacrificial only
- Hardness: 60--80 HV (soft)
- Temperature resistance: Passivate degrades above 140 F
- Passivation: Trivalent or hexavalent
- Typical applications: General fasteners, brackets, stampings
- Standard for: Cost-effective corrosion protection
- Bath complexity: Lower -- simpler chemistry
- Cost: Baseline (lowest zinc plating cost)

Highlight: `The workhorse for general corrosion protection. When 100--200 hours salt spray meets the spec, plain zinc wins on cost.`

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting Quick Hits

| # | X | Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5" | Ni% OUT OF SPEC | Metal ratio imbalance, temp shift, CD mismatch | Check Zn:Ni ratio; XRF at LCD and HCD |

| 4 | 18.0" | POOR SALT SPRAY | Alloy out of spec, passivate exhausted, no sealer | XRF alloy; replenish passivate; add sealer |
