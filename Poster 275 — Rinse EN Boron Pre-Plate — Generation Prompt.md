## Phase 4 -- Contamination Pathway Hero

Y: 4.2" to 14.5". Section: `WHAT DRAG-IN DOES TO YOUR EN-B BATH`.

Three-panel horizontal layout (Y: 5.0" to 14.0"):

**Panel 1 -- Source (accent `#E8A020`):** `DRAG-OUT SOURCES`
- From acid activation: HCl/H2SO4 residues, Chloride (Cl-) `#E05C5C`, dissolved iron
- From zincate: NaOH/ZnO residues, excess zinc
- From Sn/Pd colloidal: Tin, palladium, HCl, chromate from etch `#E05C5C`

**Panel 2 -- Rinse (accent `#2EC4B6`):** `THE RINSE`
- DI counterflow 2-stage min, ambient, 30--60 sec, target <20 uS/cm

**Panel 3 -- Consequence (accent `#E05C5C`):** `IF DRAG-IN REACHES THE EN-B BATH`
- Chloride: pitting, accelerates aging
- Fe/Cu: EN-B MORE sensitive than EN-P; decomposition nucleation
- Chromate: poisons stabilizer at ppm levels; bath goes inert
- `DMAB cost: 5--10x hypophosphite` `#E8A020`

Arrows between panels: 3pt `#3A4055`.

## Phase 5 -- EN-B-Specific Contamination Concerns

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `WHY EN-B IS LESS TOLERANT THAN EN-P`.

**Left -- Metal Contamination Sensitivity (accent `#E05C5C`):**
- DMAB = stronger reducing agent than hypophosphite
- Lower energy barrier for spontaneous metal reduction
- Fe2+, Cu2+ reduced to particles more easily
- Particles nucleate uncontrolled decomposition
- EN-P tolerates low-level Fe/Cu; EN-B may crash at same levels
- Continuous filtration (1--5 um) is essential

**Right -- Cost Impact (accent `#E8A020`):**
- DMAB: 5--10x more per unit reducing power
- EN-B bath life: 3--5 MTO (DMAB) or 2--4 MTO (NaBH4) vs. EN-P 6--8 MTO
- Every premature dump wastes hundreds in DMAB
- Bottom highlight: `The cheapest step on the line protects the most expensive bath. Rinse well.`

## Phase 6 -- Substrate-Specific Rinse Notes

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `RINSE NOTES BY SUBSTRATE`.

2x2 grid:

| Substrate | Accent | Key Note |
|---|---|---|
| Steel -- After Acid Activation | `#2EC4B6` | Chloride is primary threat; DI essential; transfer quickly |
| Aluminum -- After Double Zincate | `#E8A020` | Minimize rinse time; zinc layer oxidizes; transfer to EN-B within 30 sec |
| Stainless -- After Wood's Strike | `#27AE60` | NiCl2 + HCl residues; significant chloride drag-in; double counterflow |
| Plastics -- After Sn/Pd Colloidal | `#E05C5C` | Chromate from CrO3 etch is greatest risk; extended rinse (3+ stages); consider permanganate |

## Phase 7 -- Defect Grid

Y: 26.5" to 32.5". Section: `RINSE-RELATED EN-B DEFECTS`.

2x2 grid:

| Defect | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| PITTING | Chloride drag-in from HCl or Wood's strike | Improve rinse; DI; <20 uS/cm |
| BATH DECOMPOSITION | Fe/Cu metallic contamination | Extended rinse; continuous filtration |
| SKIP PLATING | Oxidized activation surface from slow transfer | Minimize transfer; keep parts wet |
| STABILIZER POISONING | Chromate (Cr6+) drag-in from plastic etch | Extended rinse after chromic etch; switch to permanganate |
