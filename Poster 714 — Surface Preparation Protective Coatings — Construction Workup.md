# Poster #714 -- Construction Workup
## Surface Preparation -- Protective Coatings

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of 8. This poster covers surface preparation for three substrate types: steel (blast), concrete (mechanical profile + moisture testing), and previously coated surfaces (maintenance). The hero is a three-pathway layout showing the different prep requirements for each substrate. Concrete prep is the major differentiator from Cluster 7 -- moisture testing, pH verification, and ICRI concrete surface profiles are new concepts for this cluster.

### ZONE 4 -- Concrete Moisture Testing

**Section label:** `CONCRETE MOISTURE -- THE HIDDEN ENEMY` -- Y: 15.2".

**Two-column layout (Y: 15.8" to 20.8"):**

**Left -- ASTM F1869 Calcium Chloride Test (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `CALCIUM CHLORIDE TEST (ASTM F1869)` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#E8A020`
- Steps (JetBrains Mono 13 pt `#F0EDE8`):
  1. `Seal pre-weighed anhydrous CaCl2 dish under plastic dome on concrete`
  2. `Wait 60--72 hours`
  3. `Re-weigh dish -- weight gain = moisture vapor emission rate (MVER)`
  4. `PASS: < 3 lb / 1,000 ft2 / 24 hours`
  5. `FAIL: coating will blister or delaminate from trapped moisture`
- Note: `Test measures moisture EMISSION, not total moisture content. New concrete may take 28+ days to dry sufficiently.` Inter Regular 12 pt `#F0EDE8` at 60%.

**Right -- ASTM F2170 RH Probe Test (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `RH PROBE TEST (ASTM F2170)` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#E8A020`
- Steps:
  1. `Drill hole to 40% of slab depth`
  2. `Insert RH probe and seal`
  3. `Wait 24--72 hours for equilibration`
  4. `Read relative humidity`
  5. `PASS: < 75% RH`
- Note: `RH probe method is preferred by most coating manufacturers -- it measures moisture at the depth where the coating bond exists.` Inter Regular 12 pt `#F0EDE8` at 60%.

**Two-column layout (Y: 21.2" to 26.8"):**

| Contaminant | Max Limit | Test Method |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride (Cl-) | < 3 ug/cm2 | Bresle patch (ISO 8502-6) |
| Sulfate (SO4 2-) | < 10 ug/cm2 | Bresle patch (ISO 8502-6) |
| Total conductivity | < 7 ug/cm2 NaCl equiv | Conductometric (ISO 8502-9) |

### ZONE 6 -- Maintenance Prep + Defect Grid

**Two-column layout (Y: 27.2" to 32.3"):**

**Left -- Maintenance Decision Tree (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Section label: `MAINTENANCE PREP DECISION` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#E05C5C`.

Decision steps (Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`):
- `1. Test existing coating adhesion (ASTM D3359 crosshatch)`
- `   Result >= 3B: PROCEED to overcoat prep`
- `   Result < 3B: FULL REMOVAL required`
- `2. Check compatibility (same generic type preferred)`
- `   Epoxy over epoxy: yes (within recoat window or scuff sand)`
- `   Urethane over epoxy: yes`
- `   Epoxy over alkyd: requires full removal or barrier coat`
- `3. Feather existing coating edges with 80-grit sander`
- `4. Spot-blast bare areas to SSPC-SP11`
- `5. Apply primer to bare areas, then full topcoat system`

**Right -- Common Prep Failures (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**

Section label: `PREP FAILURES` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#E05C5C`.

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Coating over curing compound (concrete) | Curing compound acts as bond breaker | Grind or blast to remove entirely |
| Insufficient profile (steel) | Worn nozzle or wrong media | Check nozzle orifice; use angular media |
| Coating over moisture (concrete) | Applied before slab dried | Wait 28+ days; re-test with F2170 |
| Overcoat over failed coating | Did not adhesion-test existing | Test first; remove if < 3B |

Each: small card, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`.

## Design Notes

This poster differentiates Cluster 8 from Cluster 7 by introducing concrete as a major substrate. Steel prep is similar to priming (blast to spec) but concrete brings entirely new concepts: ICRI surface profiles, calcium chloride moisture testing, RH probe testing, pH verification, and the curing compound trap. The three-pathway hero makes the substrate distinction clear at a glance. The maintenance prep decision tree serves the real-world scenario where most protective coating work is recoating over existing systems, not new construction.
