# Poster #82 -- Construction Workup
## Activation -- Chrome (Decorative)

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 3 of 8. Activation for decorative chrome is about removing the nickel passive film so the chrome will bond. Two approaches exist: a mild external acid dip (separate tank), or in-tank activation where the initial immersion in the chrome bath serves both activation and plating. Many modern lines, especially trivalent, use the in-tank method.

Hero visual: a split-view showing both activation methods side by side -- external acid tank vs. in-tank activation in the chrome bath.

### ZONE 4 -- Operating Parameters

**Section label:** `PARAMETERS AT A GLANCE` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Comparison Table (Y: 15.3" to 20.3")**

| Parameter | External Acid Dip | In-Tank Activation |
|---|---|---|
| Chemistry | 1--5% H2SO4 or 1--3% HCl | Chrome bath solution |
| Temperature | Ambient | Bath temp (80--120 F tri; 95--120 F hex) |
| Time | 10--30 sec | 5--15 sec (before plating current applied) |
| Current | None (chemical activation) | None (immersion) or brief anodic (hex) |
| Rinse after | Single rinse before chrome | Not applicable (already in chrome bath) |
| Advantage | Guaranteed activation even after delay | Eliminates a tank; fastest path |
| Disadvantage | Adds transfer time; another tank to maintain | Requires fast Ni-to-Cr transfer |

Header row: `#3A4055`. Alt rows: `#1E2435` / `#252B3D`.

## Design Notes

The split-view hero is the defining visual for this poster. Most activation posters in the series focus on a single method. Decorative chrome has two legitimate approaches, and the operator needs to understand both. The adhesion science panel in Zone 5 makes the invisible visible -- the passive film is the villain of this poster, and showing its formation and removal in three stages tells the whole story. Keep the parameter table lean; this is a simple chemistry step with few variables.
