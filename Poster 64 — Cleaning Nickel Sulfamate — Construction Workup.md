# Poster #64 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- Nickel (Sulfamate)

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of 8. Cleaning for sulfamate nickel has identical requirements to Watts nickel (Poster #56). The same two-step sequence applies: alkaline soak clean followed by electrocleaner. The water-break test remains the go/no-go criterion. The unique sulfamate angle: organic contamination is even more damaging to sulfamate baths than Watts baths because organics spike internal stress -- and stress control is the entire reason you chose sulfamate in the first place.

Hero visual: same dual-tank layout as Poster #56 but with sulfamate-specific context callouts emphasizing the organic contamination / stress connection.

### ZONE 4 -- Why Cleaning Matters More for Sulfamate

**Section label:** `WHY SULFAMATE DEMANDS BETTER CLEANING` -- Y: 14.7". Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 24 pt, `#F0EDE8`.

**BLOCK D -- Organic Contamination Stress Connection**

Y: 15.3" to 18.5". Full-width prominent callout.
- Rounded rect, fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 2 pt `#E05C5C`, radius 8

Title: `THE ORGANIC-STRESS CONNECTION` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#E05C5C`

Body (Inter Medium 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 160%):

> In a Watts bath, organic contamination causes pitting and haze -- cosmetic problems. In a sulfamate bath, organic contamination spikes tensile stress immediately and unpredictably. This destroys the primary advantage of sulfamate nickel.
>
> Sources of organic contamination from cleaning:
> - Oil not fully removed by soak clean
> - Surfactant carry-over from cleaner
> - Decomposed electrocleaner additives
> - Masking tape adhesive residue
> - Fingerprints (never handle parts bare-handed)
>
> Every organic molecule that reaches the sulfamate bath shifts the deposit toward higher tensile stress. For electroforming applications where stress must be near-zero, cleaning is not "good enough" until it is perfect.

### ZONE 5 -- Anodic vs. Cathodic + Electroforming Substrates

**Two-column layout (Y: 20.7" to 26.3"):**

**Left -- Anodic vs. Cathodic (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Same content as Poster #56. Anodic (reverse) preferred for nickel pre-treatment. Cathodic embeds metals and risks HE on high-strength steel.

**Right -- Electroforming Mandrel Cleaning (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**

- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `CLEANING ELECTROFORMING MANDRELS` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`
- Body (Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 155%):

> Mandrels require special attention because the sulfamate deposit must release cleanly after plating.
>
> **Aluminum mandrels:** Alkaline etch (to remove oxide), then anodize or chromate for release layer. Clean to water-break-free before chromate.
>
> **Steel mandrels:** Standard clean + light chrome flash or proprietary release agent. Surface finish of the mandrel directly transfers to the electroform.
>
> **Wax/epoxy mandrels:** Solvent clean only -- no alkaline or acid immersion. Surface must be smooth and defect-free.
>
> Any contamination on the mandrel transfers to the electroform surface. Cleaning mandrels is cleaning the product.

### ZONE 6 -- Common Failures + Safety

**Two-column layout (Y: 26.7" to 32.3"):**

**Left -- 4 Cleaning Failure Modes (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**

| Failure | Root Cause | Result in Sulfamate Bath |
|---|---|---|
| Organic carry-over | Cleaner too dilute or time too short | Stress spike -- defeats purpose of sulfamate |
| Surfactant drag-in | Cleaner not fully rinsed | Organic contamination of sulfamate bath |
| Metal embedding (cathodic) | Excessive cathodic cleaning | Dark spots, inclusions in deposit |
| Mandrel contamination | Release agent contaminated or mandrel not cleaned | Electroform adhesion failure or surface defects |

Cards: fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`.

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**

Same as Poster #56:
> - Alkaline cleaners: caustic burn hazard (pH 12--14). Gloves, goggles, apron.
> - Electrocleaner gas generation: ventilation required.
> - Hot solutions (140--190 F): splash burn risk.
> - Nickel salts: IARC Group 1 carcinogen (inhalable). Mist extraction near heated solutions.

## Design Notes

This poster shares 70% of its DNA with Poster #56 (Watts Cleaning) -- the cleaning process is identical. The 30% difference is what makes it worth a separate poster: the organic-stress connection and the electroforming mandrel section. Sulfamate users are a different audience (aerospace engineers, electroformers) and they need to see their specific concerns addressed. The organic-stress callout in Zone 4 is the single most important unique element -- make it visually dominant.
