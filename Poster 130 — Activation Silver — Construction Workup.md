# Poster #130 -- Construction Workup
## Activation -- Silver

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 3 of 8. Acid activation removes surface oxides and exposes clean, reactive metal for the silver strike. For silver plating, activation is substrate-dependent -- copper/brass uses dilute sulfuric acid; nickel-plated substrates use a lighter sulfuric dip. The critical rule: parts must go DIRECTLY to the silver strike after activation. Any delay allows re-oxidation and compromises adhesion.

### ZONE 4 -- Substrate-Specific Activation

**Section label:** `ACTIVATION BY SUBSTRATE` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Table (Y: 15.3" to 20.3")**

| Substrate | Acid | Concentration | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper / Brass | H2SO4 | 5--10% v/v | 15--30 sec | Most common silver substrate |
| Nickel-plated | H2SO4 | 5% v/v | 10--15 sec | Lighter dip -- nickel is more active |
| Steel (after Cu strike) | HCl | 10--20% v/v | 15--30 sec | Steel path uses HCl not H2SO4 |
| Kovar / Alloy 42 | H2SO4 | 5--10% v/v | 15--30 sec | Electronics connector alloys |

Standard table style. "Concentration" and "Time" columns in JetBrains Mono.

Note below table: `Sulfuric acid (H2SO4) is preferred for copper-family substrates. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is preferred for steel. Never mix acid types in the same tank without a thorough rinse between substrate types.` Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8` at 70%.

### ZONE 6 -- Time-Critical Path + Common Errors

**Section label:** `TIMING AND COMMON ERRORS` -- Y: 26.7".

**BLOCK F -- Four Cards (Y: 27.3" to 32.3")**

| Card | Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OVER-ACTIVATION | Time too long or acid too strong | Pitting on copper; hydrogen embrittlement on steel. Follow time limits. |
| 2 | UNDER-ACTIVATION | Time too short or spent acid | Oxide remains; silver peels. Replace acid when slow to activate. |
| 3 | DELAY TO STRIKE | Parts sit in air after activation | Re-oxidation. Process within 2 min. |
| 4 | WRONG ACID FOR SUBSTRATE | H2SO4 on steel or HCl on copper | Smut or surface damage. Match acid to substrate per table. |

Standard card style, left accent `#E05C5C`.

## Design Notes

The key message on this poster is SPEED. Activation opens a time window that closes fast. The substrate-specific table is the reference operators will check at the line. The before/after oxide concept is educational -- most operators know they need to "dip in acid" but do not understand why.
