**Left -- Parameter Table (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|

| Water temperature | Ambient (65--85 F) |

| Immersion time per tank | 30--60 sec |
| Agitation | Parts dip or mild air sparging |
| Key risk | NaOH drag-in to passivation bath |

**Right -- Optional Bright Dip (X: 15.0", W: 8.5"):**
Accent `#E8A020`. Title: `OPTIONAL: NITRIC ACID BRIGHT DIP / DE-SMUT`.
- `0.5--1% v/v HNO3 | Ambient | 10--30 sec`
- Purpose: Remove surface smut, brighten Zn-Ni before passivation
- Not required on all lines -- depends on deposit and passivation type
- Rinse immediately after -- do NOT carry HNO3 into passivation

## Phase 6 -- Common Failures + HE Bake Timing

Y: 21.0" to 27.0". Section: `WHAT GOES WRONG`.

### Step 7 -- Common failures (5 rows)

| Failure | Root Cause | Result |
|---|---|---|
| NaOH drag-in to passivate | Insufficient rinsing; single rinse | Passivate flakes; fails salt spray |
| No drag-out recovery | Skipped recovery tank | Chemistry wasted; cost increase 15--25% |
| Water staining | DI not used on appearance parts | Staining visible under passivation |
| Parts dry between rinse and passivate | Slow transfer | Surface oxidizes; adhesion drops |
| Excess NaOH in rinse | Water not flowing | Rinse becomes alkaline sump |

## Phase 7 -- Drag-Out Economics + Safety

Y: 27.0" to 32.5".

**Left -- Drag-Out Recovery Economics (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**
Title: `DRAG-OUT RECOVERY -- THE NUMBERS` `#27AE60`.
- Zn-Ni baths cost significantly more than plain zinc per gallon
- Barrel line drag-out: 0.5--2.0 gal per 1,000 lbs of parts
- Recovery tank captures 50--80% of chemistry
- Return to plating bath weekly or as conductivity indicates
- Payback: 3--6 months on production line
- Bonus: reduces waste treatment load and nickel discharge

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**
Fill `#E05C5C` at 10%, border 1pt `#E05C5C`. Title: `SAFETY`.
- Rinse water contains NaOH and nickel compounds
- Nickel: dermal sensitizer -- avoid prolonged skin contact
- Drain to waste treatment -- never sanitary sewer
- Bright dip (HNO3): strong oxidizer, toxic fumes
- PPE: gloves, goggles, apron for all rinse/bright dip work
