# Poster #605 -- Construction Workup
## Safety & PPE -- Ferritic Nitrocarburizing (FNC / QPQ)

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Molten salt is unforgiving. At 1050-1125 F, the nitrocarburizing bath will cause catastrophic burns on contact, and water is the trigger for a violent steam explosion. This poster's number-one message: MOISTURE IS THE ENEMY. Every safety procedure, every PPE requirement, every emergency response protocol circles back to keeping water away from molten salt. The cyanate chemistry adds a secondary chemical hazard. This is the most safety-critical poster in the FNC cluster.

### ZONE 2 -- Hazard Grid (HERO)

**Section label:** `FOUR HAZARDS IN FNC / QPQ OPERATIONS` -- Y: 3.1". Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 28 pt `#E05C5C`.

**BLOCK B -- Four Hazard Cards (2x2 layout)**

Row 1 (Y: 3.8"):

*Card 1 -- Molten Salt Burns (X: 0.5", W: 11.0", H: 5.2"):*
- Title: `MOLTEN SALT BURNS` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`
- Stat: `1050--1125 F NITROCARBURIZING BATH` JetBrains Mono 20 pt `#E05C5C`
- Details:
```
- Molten salt CLINGS to skin and clothing
  (unlike water or oil, salt adheres)
- Burns are deep and catastrophic
- Even the oxidizing bath at 700--800 F
  causes severe burns on contact
- Splash during loading/unloading is the
  primary risk scenario
- Salt splash can travel 6+ feet from
  the bath edge
```
- Mitigation (Inter Medium 12 pt `#27AE60`):
  `Full PPE for ALL salt bath operations. No exposed skin within splash zone. Elbow-length heat-resistant gauntlets mandatory.`

*Card 2 -- Steam Explosion (X: 12.0", W: 11.5", H: 5.2"):*
- Title: `MOISTURE / STEAM EXPLOSION` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`
- Stat: `WATER + MOLTEN SALT = VIOLENT ERUPTION` JetBrains Mono 18 pt `#E05C5C`
- Details:
```
- ANY moisture on parts, fixtures, or
  tools causes INSTANT steam generation
- Water expands 1,600x when converting
  to steam at 1050 F
- Result: explosive ejection of molten
  salt from the bath
- Even condensation from humid air can
  trigger micro-eruptions
- Preheat step (600--700 F) is MANDATORY
  safety procedure, not optional
```
- Mitigation:
  `Preheat ALL parts to 600--700 F before immersion. Verify dryness. NEVER skip preheat. NEVER immerse cold or damp parts.`

Row 2 (Y: 9.3"):

*Card 3 -- Cyanate Chemistry (X: 0.5", W: 11.0", H: 5.2"):*
- Title: `CYANATE SALT CHEMISTRY` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`
- Stat: `NaCNO / KCNO -- REGULATED WASTE` JetBrains Mono 18 pt `#E8A020`
- Details:
```
- Cyanate (CNO-) is less toxic than
  cyanide (CN-) but still regulated
- Bath regeneration produces CN-
  (cyanide) as a byproduct
- Cyanide monitoring required
- Waste disposal: regulated hazardous
  waste in most jurisdictions
- Skin and eye contact causes irritation
- Ingestion is dangerous
```
- Mitigation:
  `Proper waste manifesting and disposal. Cyanide monitoring per regulatory schedule. Wash hands after any salt contact. Eyewash station within 10 seconds.`

*Card 4 -- Fumes (X: 12.0", W: 11.5", H: 5.2"):*
- Title: `SALT BATH FUMES` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`
- Stat: `NITROGEN OXIDES + SALT AEROSOL` JetBrains Mono 18 pt `#E8A020`
- Details:
```
- Nitrocarburizing bath generates NOx
  fumes during processing
- Oxidizing bath (NaNO3/NaNO2) produces
  additional NOx when parts are immersed
- Salt aerosol is respiratory irritant
- Long-term exposure: respiratory damage
- Fume extraction MANDATORY over all
  salt bath positions
```
- Mitigation:
  `Local exhaust ventilation over every bath. P100 + organic vapor respiratory protection. Monitor NOx levels per OSHA.`

*Molten Salt Burn:*
```
1. REMOVE victim from salt bath area
2. DO NOT attempt to remove adhered salt
   from skin (tears tissue)
3. Flood the burn with clean running
   water for 20+ minutes
4. Call 911 immediately for any salt burn
   -- ALL molten salt burns require
   emergency medical treatment
5. Remove contaminated clothing ONLY if
   not stuck to skin
6. Cover with sterile dressing
7. Treat for shock (lay flat, elevate legs)
```

*Steam Explosion:*
```
1. EVACUATE the area immediately
2. Do NOT approach the bath
3. Call 911 if anyone is injured
4. Wait for salt to stop erupting
   (usually subsides in seconds)
5. Secure the area -- molten salt
   may be on floor, equipment, walls
6. Account for all personnel
7. Investigate: what was the moisture
   source? Wet part? Condensation?
   Leaking cooling line above bath?
8. NEVER resume operations until
   root cause is identified and fixed
```

*Fire in Salt Bath:*
```
CRITICAL: NEVER USE WATER

Water on a salt bath fire causes
steam explosion and spreads the fire

CORRECT RESPONSE:
1. DRY CHEMICAL extinguisher (Class D)
2. DRY SAND to smother
3. Purpose-built salt bath lid/cover
   to starve fire of oxygen
4. Evacuate if fire cannot be controlled
5. Call fire department -- inform them
   of molten salt / cyanate salt hazards

The fire is from organic contamination
in the salt (oil, cutting fluid residue).
The salt itself is not flammable --
the contaminant is.
```

## Design Notes

The moisture rule callout (Zone 3) is the single most important element on this poster. It's full-width, Coral-bordered, and unmissable. Every molten salt bath accident investigation starts with "how did moisture get in?" -- this poster plants that awareness at the center of the visual hierarchy. The fire suppression panel explicitly states "NEVER USE WATER" three times because the instinct to grab a water extinguisher is deeply ingrained and absolutely wrong in this context.
