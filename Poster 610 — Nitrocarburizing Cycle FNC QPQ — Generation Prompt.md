## Phase 4 -- Surface Cross-Section (HERO)

Y: 2.9" to 15.5". Section label: `THE FNC-TREATED SURFACE -- WHAT YOU'RE BUILDING`.

Large vertical cross-section diagram showing three zones from surface to core:

**Layer 1 -- Compound Zone (top):** Fill `#F0EDE8` at 25%, dashed bottom border `#E8A020`. Labels: `COMPOUND ZONE` ("White Layer"), 10--25 um (0.0004--0.001"). Primarily epsilon (Fe2-3N) iron nitride. 600--1000+ HV. Wear and corrosion resistant. Porous -- sealed by Q-P-Q oxidizing quench.

**Layer 2 -- Diffusion Zone (middle):** Fill gradient `#27AE60` at 20% fading to transparent. Labels: `DIFFUSION ZONE`, 0.005--0.025" (0.13--0.64 mm). Nitrogen in solid solution + fine nitride precipitates (Fe4N, CrN). Provides fatigue resistance and load support. Hardness gradient from surface to core.

**Layer 3 -- Core (bottom):** Fill `#1A1F2E`. Labels: `CORE (UNAFFECTED)`. Original microstructure preserved. No Q&T required before FNC. As-machined, normalized, or annealed all work.

## Phase 5 -- Mechanism of Action

Y: 15.5" to 22.0". Section label: `HOW IT WORKS -- CYANATE DECOMPOSITION AND DIFFUSION`.

**Left -- The Chemistry (Emerald):** Cyanate decomposition: 4NaCNO -> Na2CO3 + 2NaCN + CO + 2N. Released N and C atoms absorb into ferrite surface. CO provides carbon. NaCN (cyanide) is byproduct -- requires monitoring. Replenish salt as CNO depletes to maintain 35--40%.

**Right -- What Happens in the Steel (Teal):** 1. N diffuses into ferrite (interstitial). 2. N concentration exceeds solubility at surface. 3. Epsilon Fe2-3N compound zone precipitates. 4. Below: N in solid solution + alloy nitrides (CrN, MoN). 5. C co-diffuses. 6. ALL below Ac1 -- stays ferritic. No austenite, no martensite, no quench. 7. Hard surface, tough core, minimal distortion. 8. Compound zone is porous -- Q-P-Q fills pores with magnetite.

## Phase 6 -- Steel Response + Dimensional Change

Y: 22.0" to 32.5".

**Left -- Steel Response Table (W: 14.0"):**

Section label: `STEEL RESPONSE TO FNC -- NOT ALL STEELS ARE EQUAL`.

| Steel | Surface HV | Application | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1018/1020 | 350--550 | General engineering | Moderate -- dramatic improvement over untreated |
| 1045 | 400--600 | Shafts, pins | Good |
| 4140/4340 | 500--700 | Hydraulic cylinders, gun barrels | Good -- Cr/Mo form hard nitrides |
| H13 | 900--1100 | Die casting cores | Excellent -- 5% Cr produces very hard case |
| 410/420 SS | 800--1000 | Corrosion + wear | Good but slower (passive film) |
| Ductile iron | 400--600 | Crankshafts, camshafts | Good -- widely used automotive |
| Cast iron | 350--500 | Cylinder liners, brake rotors | Moderate |

Key principle: Steels with nitride-forming elements (Cr, Mo, Al, V) produce hardest cases. In `#27AE60`.

**Right -- Dimensional Change (W: 8.5"):**

Big number: `<0.0002"` 48pt `#27AE60`. (< 5 um) typical growth. No phase transformation, no quench distortion, thin compound zone. Parts can be finish-machined BEFORE FNC -- no post-treatment grinding. Major cost advantage over carburizing.

Pill callout below: `FNC is to surface hardening what a clear coat is to automotive paint -- it changes the surface without changing the part.` Teal.
