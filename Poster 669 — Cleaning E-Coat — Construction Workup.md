# Poster #669 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- E-Coat

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of 9. Cleaning for e-coat is a two-stage alkaline system -- spray pre-clean followed by immersion main clean. The challenge is unique to automotive: car bodies are complex enclosed structures that must flood and drain through designed openings. Oil loading, silicate residues, and foam control are the critical variables. A dirty body ruins every downstream stage.

### ZONE 4 -- Cleaner Chemistry + Controls

**Section label:** `CLEANER CHEMISTRY -- WHAT IS IN THE TANK` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Three-Component Breakdown (Y: 15.3" to 18.5")**

Three side-by-side callout boxes:

| Component | X | W | Accent | Title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkaline Builder | 0.5" | 7.33" | `#2EC4B6` | ALKALINE BUILDER |
| Surfactant System | 8.0" | 7.33" | `#E8A020` | SURFACTANT SYSTEM |
| Chelating Agents | 15.5" | 8.0" | `#27AE60` | CHELATING AGENTS |

Each box: Rounded rect H: 3.0", fill `#1E2435`, left accent 0.06".

*Alkaline builder:*
- `NaOH, sodium metasilicate`
- `pH 10--12 operating range`
- `Provides saponification of oils`
- `CAUTION: Silicate residues cause phosphate adhesion failure -- use low-silicate formulations`

*Surfactant system:*
- `Non-ionic / anionic blend`
- `Low-foam required for spray stages`
- `Emulsifies mineral oils and greases`
- `Defoamer additions may be needed at elevated temperatures`

*Chelating agents:*
- `EDTA, GLDA, or citrate-based`
- `Sequester hard water minerals (Ca, Mg)`
- `Prevent mineral scale deposits on parts and tank walls`
- `Extend bath life by keeping metals in solution`

## Design Notes

This poster establishes the foundation for the entire e-coat cluster. The two-stage cleaning concept (spray + immersion) is specific to automotive e-coat and differentiates it from single-stage cleaning in most plating shops. The body drainage concept is critical -- car bodies are not flat parts, and the dip-drain-dip sequencing is a major engineering consideration that most plating shop operators have never encountered.
