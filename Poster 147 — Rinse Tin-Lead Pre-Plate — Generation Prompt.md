## Phase 5 -- Drag-In to Solder Bath

Y: 14.5" to 20.5". Section: `WHAT ACID DRAG-IN DOES TO YOUR SOLDER BATH`.

Full-width panel, fill `#1E2435`. Three columns:

| Column | Header Color | Header | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | `#E8A020` | BATH DILUTION | Excess acid lowers metal concentrations and alters the Sn:Pb ratio over time. Changes alloy composition -- the most critical control parameter. |
| Center | `#E05C5C` | COPPER CONTAMINATION | Dissolved copper from activation drags into solder bath. Cu >5 ppm causes immersion deposits, dark spots, alloy shift. The #1 metallic contaminant. |
| Right | `#E05C5C` | CHEMISTRY WASTE | Every mL of drag-in is plating chemistry you must replace. Tin and lead salts are expensive. Drag-in is money and lead waste down the drain. |

Bottom: `Good rinsing extends bath life and stabilizes alloy composition. Poor rinsing shifts the ratio and contaminates the bath.` `#27AE60`

## Phase 6 -- MSA Compatibility Advantage

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `THE MSA ADVANTAGE -- ACID FAMILY MATCHING`.

**Left -- MSA Line (Compatible) (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
- Fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#27AE60`
- Title: `MSA ACTIVATION --> MSA BATH` `#27AE60`
- Drag-in is the SAME ACID as the bath. No foreign anion. Dilution only concern. Single overflow often adequate.
- Bottom: `This is why MSA lines are cleaner -- one acid family from activation to plate.` `#27AE60`

**Right -- Mixed-Acid Line (Risk) (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
- Fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`
- Title: `H2SO4 ACTIVATION --> MSA BATH` `#E05C5C`
- Sulfate anions into MSA bath. Sulfate co-precipitates with lead as PbSO4 (insoluble, white sludge). Lowers lead, shifts alloy ratio. More aggressive rinsing needed.
- Bottom: `Avoid sulfuric activation on MSA solder lines if possible. If required, add an extra rinse stage.` `#E05C5C`
