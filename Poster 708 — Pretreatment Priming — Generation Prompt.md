## Phase 4 -- Three-Pathway Decision (HERO)

Y: 4.2" to 15.0". Section label: `PRETREATMENT DEPENDS ON PRIMER TYPE AND SUBSTRATE`.

Decision node at top: `WHAT PRIMER AND SUBSTRATE?` `#E8A020`. Three branching paths down:

**Path 1 -- Zinc-Rich on Steel (accent `#27AE60`):** Big text `NO PRETREATMENT`. Blast profile IS pretreatment. Zinc must contact steel. Phosphate = insulator = defeats primer. SP10/SP5 provides anchor + clean metal + surface energy. Badge: `BLAST PROFILE = PRETREATMENT`.

**Path 2 -- Epoxy on Steel (accent `#E8A020`):** Big text `OPTIONAL`. Blast is sufficient for most. Optional iron phosphate or wash primer for spot repair. SP6 new steel, SP3 maintenance. Badge: `BLAST ALONE USUALLY SUFFICIENT`.

**Path 3 -- Aerospace on Aluminum (accent `#2EC4B6`):** Big text `REQUIRED`. Chromate MIL-DTL-5541 (Type I/II). Anodize MIL-PRF-8625 Type I or IIB. Ti/Zr sol-gel non-chrome. Prime within 24 hr. Badge: `CONVERSION COATING = MANDATORY`.

## Phase 5 -- Galvanic Circuit + Aerospace Table

### Why No Pretreatment for IOZ (Y: 15.0"-21.0")

Section label: `THE GALVANIC CIRCUIT -- WHY PHOSPHATE DEFEATS IOZ`. Two comparison panels.

Left -- CORRECT (`#27AE60`): Zinc on bare steel. Direct contact. Galvanic circuit complete. Sacrificial protection.

Right -- WRONG (`#E05C5C`): Zinc on phosphate on steel. Insulator layer. Circuit broken. Expensive primer with zero galvanic benefit.

Verdict: `Phosphate under IOZ = paying for galvanic protection you are not getting.`

### Aerospace Table (Y: 21.0"-27.0")

Section label: `AEROSPACE PRETREATMENT OPTIONS`. 6-row table: Hex chromate (legacy), TCP (gaining), Chromic anodize (declining), Thin sulfuric anodize (replacing), Ti/Zr sol-gel (approved select), Zn-Ni plate (replacing Cd).
