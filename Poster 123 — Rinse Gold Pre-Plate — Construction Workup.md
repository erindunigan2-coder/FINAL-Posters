# Poster #123 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Gold -- Pre-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 4 of 8. The final rinse before parts enter the gold plating bath. This is the most critical rinse in the entire gold process -- and arguably the most critical rinse in any plating line. DI (deionized) water is mandatory, not optional. Tap water introduces calcium, magnesium, iron, and chloride that can permanently damage a gold bath worth hundreds to thousands of dollars per gallon. This poster hammers the DI-only rule and explains why.

### ZONE 5 -- Mineral Contamination

**Section label:** `WHAT TAP WATER MINERALS DO TO A GOLD BATH` -- Y: 20.7".

**BLOCK E -- Contamination Table**

Y: 21.3" to 26.0".

| Mineral | Typical in Tap Water | Gold Bath Threshold | Effect if Exceeded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calcium (Ca) | 10--200 ppm | Problem at any level | Forms insoluble salts, roughens deposit |
| Iron (Fe) | 0.1--5 ppm | > 10 ppm | Dark deposit, roughness |
| Copper (Cu) | 0.01--1 ppm | > 5 ppm | Dark, discolored deposit |
| Zinc (Zn) | 0.01--5 ppm | > 1 ppm | Pitting, discoloration |
| Chloride (Cl-) | 10--250 ppm | > 1 ppm | Attacks gold complex -- catastrophic |
| Silica | 5--50 ppm | Problem at any level | Hazy deposit, poor adhesion |

Header: `#3A4055`. Alternating rows: `#1E2435` / `#252B3D`.
Threshold values: `#E05C5C`.

Bottom note: `A single batch rinsed in tap water can introduce enough chloride to damage a gold bath permanently.` Inter Medium 14 pt `#E05C5C`

## Design Notes

This is the most important rinse poster in the entire series. The DI-only rule is absolute in gold plating and yet is violated regularly by shops that do not understand the consequences. The tap water vs. DI comparison with actual mineral content numbers makes the argument concrete. The contamination table connects tap water minerals directly to gold bath damage. The conductivity target (< 5 microS/cm) gives operators a single number to check.
