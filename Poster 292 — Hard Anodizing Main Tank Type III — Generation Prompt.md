## Phase 5 -- Current Ramp Protocol

Y: 14.0" to 18.5". Section: `CURRENT RAMP PROTOCOL -- PREVENTS BURNING`.

**Left -- Ramp Sequence:**

| Time | Current Density | Voltage | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0--2 min | 6--12 ASF | 15--25 V | Initial ramp -- gentle start |
| 2--5 min | 12--18 ASF | 25--35 V | Intermediate ramp |
| 5--15 min | 18--36 ASF | 35--50 V | Ramp to full CD |
| 15 min--end | 24--36 ASF (hold) | 50--75+ V (rising) | Hold; voltage rises as oxide thickens |

Note: `Voltage rises continuously as oxide grows (increasing resistance). 75--100+ V is normal for thick coatings.`

**Right -- Why Ramp?:**
Full current on bare aluminum = instant heat at surface. Heat cannot dissipate through thin initial oxide. Result: BURNING. The ramp allows thin oxide to form first, distributing current evenly.
Alternative: `Some shops use VOLTAGE CONTROL instead of current control.` `#E8A020`

## Phase 6 -- Film Thickness + Mixed Acid Variant

Y: 18.5" to 22.5".

**Left -- Film Thickness vs Time:**

| Target | um | Time at 30 ASF | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 mil | 25 um | 30--45 min | Minimum per MIL-A-8625F |
| 2.0 mil | 50 um | 60--90 min | Default per spec |
| 3.0 mil | 75 um | 90--135 min | Near alloy limits for 2024, 7075 |
| 4.0 mil | 100 um | 120--180 min | Maximum practical; 6061 only |

**Right -- Mixed Acid Variant:**
`ALUMILITE 225/226 (OXALIC ACID ADDITION)`: H2SO4 12% + Oxalic acid 1%. Benefits: improved throwing power, reduced burning tendency, harder/more crack-resistant coating.
