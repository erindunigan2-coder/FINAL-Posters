# Poster #703 -- Construction Workup
## Industrial Priming -- Process Flow

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

> **NUMBERING FLAG:** Poster 704 already exists with the same title ("Industrial Priming -- Process Flow"). This file was written as Poster 703 per the gap-fill assignment. Drew: please confirm which number is canonical and retire the duplicate.

The cluster overview for Industrial Priming Systems. These are not decorative coatings -- they are the foundation of corrosion protection for steel bridges, offshore platforms, power plants, and aircraft. Three primer families dominate: zinc-rich (inorganic and organic) for galvanic protection, epoxy for barrier protection, and aerospace chromated/non-chrome for military and commercial flight hardware. The zinc-rich primer is the star: 75-85% zinc by weight in the dry film, providing the same sacrificial cathodic protection principle as hot-dip galvanizing -- but applied as paint.

Hero visual: horizontal flow diagram (left-to-right, 8 stages) showing the complete priming process from surface preparation through inspection, with a primer family comparison panel below.

### ZONE 4 -- Galvanic Protection Principle

**Section label:** `HOW ZINC-RICH PRIMERS PROTECT STEEL` -- Y: 22.2". Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, 28 pt, `#F0EDE8`.

**BLOCK E -- Galvanic Protection Diagram (Y: 22.9" to 28.3")**

Full-width rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020` 0.06".

**Left -- Principle Diagram (X: 1.0", W: 11.0"):**
- Simplified cross-section showing:
  - Bottom layer: `STEEL SUBSTRATE` fill `#3A4055`, label `#F0EDE8`
  - Middle layer: `ZINC-RICH PRIMER (75-85% Zn)` fill `#E8A020` at 20%, with zinc particle dots
  - Scratch/damage through both layers exposing steel
  - Arrow from zinc particles toward exposed steel: `Zn -> Zn2+ + 2e-` (oxidation)
  - Arrow from electrons to steel: `e- flow to steel` (cathodic protection)
  - Label at exposed steel: `STEEL PROTECTED (cathode)`
  - Label at zinc particles: `ZINC CORRODES (anode)`
- Caption: `Zinc corrodes preferentially, donating electrons to the steel. The steel remains cathodically protected even at the scratch.` Inter Regular 12 pt `#F0EDE8`

**Right -- Key Facts (X: 12.5", W: 10.5"):**
- Title: `WHY IT WORKS` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`

Bullet list (Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 155%):
- `Zinc is anodic to steel in the galvanic series`
- `Zinc particles must be in electrical contact with each other AND the steel`
- `This is why zinc-rich primers need 75-85% zinc by weight`
- `This is why conversion coating UNDER zinc-rich primer defeats the purpose -- it insulates zinc from steel`
- `Same principle as hot-dip galvanizing, applied as a paint film`
- `IOZ standalone: 1,500-3,000+ hours salt spray at 3 mil DFT`
- `Full system (IOZ + epoxy + urethane): 5,000-10,000+ hours`

Big stat: `75-85%` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 48 pt `#E8A020`

## Design Notes

The galvanic protection diagram in Zone 4 is the conceptual anchor of this poster and arguably the most important concept in the entire priming cluster. The zinc particles corroding to protect steel -- shown as a simple cross-section with electron flow arrows -- makes the principle tangible. The "no conversion coating under zinc-rich" rule is counterintuitive for anyone coming from other painting processes, so it gets called out explicitly. The primer family comparison gives the viewer a one-glance map of the three worlds of industrial priming.
