# Poster #147 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Tin-Lead -- Pre-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 4 of 8. The last rinse before parts enter the tin-lead plating bath. This rinse removes residual activation acid. Unlike pure tin plating where chloride contamination is the primary rinse threat, tin-lead pre-plate rinse has a unique advantage: if MSA is used for both activation and plating, the drag-in is chemically compatible with the bath. The rinse is still critical -- it removes dissolved copper from the activation step and prevents dilution -- but the compatibility factor means a single overflow is often adequate on well-managed MSA lines.

### ZONE 4 -- Drag-In to Solder Bath

**Section label:** `WHAT ACID DRAG-IN DOES TO YOUR SOLDER BATH` -- Y: 14.7".

**BLOCK D -- Consequences Panel**

Y: 15.3" to 20.0". Full-width rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`.

Three-column layout:

| Column | Header Color | Header | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | `#E8A020` | `BATH DILUTION` | Excess acid lowers metal concentrations and alters the Sn:Pb ratio over time. Dilution changes alloy composition -- the most critical control parameter in solder plating. |
| Center | `#E05C5C` | `COPPER CONTAMINATION` | Dissolved copper from activation drags into the solder bath. Copper >5 ppm causes immersion deposits, dark spots, and alloy composition shift. The #1 metallic contaminant in solder baths. |
| Right | `#E05C5C` | `CHEMISTRY WASTE` | Every mL of drag-in is plating chemistry you must replace. Tin and lead metal salts are expensive. Drag-in is money and lead waste down the drain. |

Bottom rule: `Good rinsing extends bath life and stabilizes alloy composition. Poor rinsing shifts the ratio and contaminates the bath.` Inter Medium 14 pt `#27AE60`

**Right -- Mixed-Acid Line (Risk):**
- Rounded rect, X: 12.0", W: 11.5", H: 4.5", fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`

- `Activation acid: Sulfuric acid (H2SO4)`
- `Plating bath acid: MSA`

- `PbSO4 is insoluble -- forms white sludge`
- `Lowers lead concentration, shifts alloy ratio`
- `Requires more aggressive rinsing to prevent`

Bottom note: `Avoid sulfuric activation on MSA solder lines if possible. If required, add an extra rinse stage.` Inter Medium 13 pt `#E05C5C`

## Design Notes

The MSA compatibility callout (Zone 5) is the unique value-add for this poster. Most plating references do not emphasize acid family matching, but it is a real operational advantage of MSA systems -- and a strong argument for converting legacy fluoborate lines. The PbSO4 precipitation risk from sulfuric activation drag-in is a specific, actionable warning that operators on mixed-chemistry lines need to see. Copper contamination (<5 ppm threshold) is the primary metallic contaminant concern -- it directly affects alloy composition and creates visible defects.
