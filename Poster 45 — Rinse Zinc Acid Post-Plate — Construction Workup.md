# Poster #45 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Zinc (Acid) -- Post-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 8. The most critical rinse in the acid zinc line -- same as every plating line. This rinse removes chloride-bearing drag-out before the parts enter the passivate tank. Chloride drag-in to chromate passivation tanks degrades passivate quality and shortens bath life. Watson's research brief identifies this as the #1 passivate failure mode in job shops.

Hero visual: two-path consequences diagram (good rinse vs. bad rinse) -- same concept as Poster #37 but adapted for acid zinc chemistry. The key difference: acid zinc drag-out is acidic (not alkaline), so the consequence is chloride contamination of the passivate rather than pH neutralization.

### ZONE 5 -- Problems

**Section label:** `WHAT GOES WRONG AT THE POST-PLATE RINSE` -- Y: 21.7".

| Problem | Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passivate degradation | Short bath life, frequent replenishment | Chloride drag-in overwhelming passivate | Add rinse stages; drag-out recovery tank |
| Uneven passivate color | Patchy or mottled chromate appearance | Localized chloride residue (recesses, blind holes) | Extend rinse time; air agitation; spray nozzles |
| White haze on zinc | Cloudy surface before passivation | Zinc hydroxide or chloride salt residue | Ensure thorough rinsing; check water quality |
| Passivate pH dropping | Must add alkali constantly to passivate | Acidic drag-out from zinc bath | Improve rinse; monitor final rinse pH |
| Water spots | Marks after drying | Hard water in rinse | Use DI for final rinse stage |

## Design Notes

The key differentiator from Poster #37 (Post-Plate Rinse -- Alkaline) is the nature of the drag-out. Alkaline zinc drag-out is caustic (NaOH) and neutralizes the passivate acid. Acid zinc drag-out is acidic (HCl/KCl) and contaminates the passivate with chloride -- a different failure mechanism. The consequences diagram right path should emphasize chloride attack on chromate chemistry rather than pH neutralization.

The optional bright dip (HNO3 or HCl) between the final rinse and passivation is unique to acid zinc and some decorative processes. It's included here because Watson's research notes it specifically.
