### Step 7 -- Chloride stress impact callout (Y: 12.0" to 14.8")

Full-width callout, fill `#E05C5C` at 12%, border 2pt `#E05C5C`. THE HERO ELEMENT.
- Title: `CHLORIDE DRAG-IN INCREASES STRESS` 24pt `#E05C5C`
- HCl activation leaves chloride ions on the part. In Watts (37--55 g/L NiCl2): negligible. In sulfamate (especially zero-chloride): even small additions shift deposit toward higher tensile stress.
- Increasing NiCl2 from 0 to 30 g/L can shift stress from near-zero to moderate tensile. Every gram of chloride matters for electroforming and fatigue-critical applications.
- `The pre-plate rinse is your last line of defense against chloride contamination of the sulfamate bath.`

## Phase 5 -- Parameters + Chloride-Free Context

Y: 15.0" to 21.0". Two-column layout.

**Left -- Parameter Summary (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

| Parameter | Target | Why (Sulfamate-Specific) |
|---|---|---|
| Rinse stages | 2 minimum (counterflow) | Tighter rinse for chloride removal |
| Conductivity | < 100 microS/cm | Stricter than Watts (< 200) |
| Water type | DI preferred | Reduces all dissolved ions |
| Overflow rate | High -- continuous | Must dilute chloride effectively |
| Immersion time | 30--60 sec with agitation | Balance speed with thoroughness |

**Right -- Zero-Chloride Sulfamate Context (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**
Fill `#1E2435`, accent `#E8A020`. Title: `ZERO-CHLORIDE SULFAMATE`.
- High-purity electroforming baths operate with ZERO NiCl2. Use insoluble Ti mesh anodes + sulfamate concentrate additions.
- ANY chloride contamination degrades deposit quality.
- For zero-chloride baths: DI water exclusively, dedicated pre-plate rinse not shared with other processes.

## Phase 6 -- Watts vs. Sulfamate Rinse Rules + Failures

Y: 21.0" to 27.0". Two-column layout.

**Left -- Rinse Rule Comparison (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
Title: `DIFFERENT BATHS, DIFFERENT RINSE RULES`.

| Factor | Watts | Sulfamate |
|---|---|---|
| Acid drag-in | Bad (lowers pH) | Bad (lowers pH + hydrolysis risk) |
| Chloride drag-in | Harmless | HARMFUL (increases stress) |
| Conductivity target | < 200 microS/cm | < 100 microS/cm |
| Recommended stages | 1 minimum | 2 minimum (counterflow) |
| Water type | City OK | DI preferred |
| Overall urgency | Moderate | HIGH |

"HARMFUL" in `#E05C5C`.

**Right -- 4 Failure Modes (X: 12.5", W: 11.0"):**

| Problem | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride contamination | HCl drag-in not removed | Stress increase in sulfamate deposit |
| Acid drag-in (pH drop) | Inadequate rinse | pH below 3.0 causes sulfamate hydrolysis |
| Re-oxidation | Parts air-dried | Adhesion failure |
| Cross-process contamination | Shared rinse with Watts or other bath | Mixed chemistry drag-in |

## Phase 7 -- Practical Tips + Safety

Y: 27.0" to 32.5". Two-column layout.

**Left -- Tips (X: 0.5", W: 14.0"):**
Accent `#27AE60`.
- For chloride-free sulfamate baths: use DI water exclusively.
- Two-stage counterflow is the minimum.
- If activated with HCl: rinse must remove ALL chloride. Consider extended immersion or extra stage.
- Keep parts wet between rinse and plating.
- Monitor sulfamate bath chloride weekly. Rising chloride = failing rinse.

**Right -- Safety (X: 15.5", W: 8.0"):**
Coral-tinted glass. Acidic rinse water | Route to waste treatment | Nickel-containing: hazardous waste.
