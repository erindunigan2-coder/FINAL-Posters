## Phase 5 -- Common Defects + Salt Spray Testing

### Defects Table (Y: 15.5"--22.0")

Section label: `COMMON DEFECTS -- Q-P-Q FAILURE MODES` in `#E05C5C`.

| Defect | Cause | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Poor corrosion (salt spray fails early) | Insufficient quench time; poor polish (Ra too high) | Extend Q1/Q2 to full 30 min; polish to Ra 8--16 spec |
| Thin/absent compound zone | Low bath temp (below 1050 F); depleted CNO (below 35%) | Verify temp; replenish salt to 35--40% CNO |
| Staining/discoloration | Salt residue not rinsed; contaminated oxidizing bath | Improve rinse; maintain bath purity |
| Distortion | Prior hardened part; FNC temp exceeded prior temper | Verify thermal history before processing |
| Pitting (micro-pits) | Moisture on parts before immersion (micro-steam explosions) | Improve preheat drying; extend preheat time |

### Salt Spray Testing (Y: 22.0"--28.5")

Section label: `SALT SPRAY TESTING -- ASTM B117 EXPLAINED`.

**Left -- The Test (Teal):** ASTM B117: place in salt fog cabinet, 5% NaCl at 95 F continuous, inspect at intervals, record time to first red rust. Test is accelerated (hours =/= real-world exposure). Comparative ranking, not calendar prediction. Most Q-P-Q specs require min 200 hr.

**Right -- Interpreting Results (Emerald):** 200--500 hr on 1018. Factors that improve: better polish, longer quench, fresh baths, good rinse. Factors that reduce: poor polish, short quench, depleted/contaminated baths, salt residue. Context: hard chrome 24--96 hr (Q-P-Q is 2--10x better), black oxide 1--4 hr (Q-P-Q is 50--500x better).
