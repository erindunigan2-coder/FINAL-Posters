## Phase 4 -- Dual-Mechanism Diagram (HERO)

Y: 4.2" to 15.5". Section label: `HOW YOUR CLEANER ACTUALLY WORKS -- TWO MECHANISMS`.

Two-column hero layout.

**Left -- Saponification** (emerald-accented glass card, H ~9.5"):
- Title: `SAPONIFICATION` (Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 24 pt `#27AE60`)
- Subtitle: `For Animal & Vegetable Fats (Polar Oils)`

Reaction diagram -- three boxes connected by arrows:
- `Fat / Oil (RCOOH)` (`#E8A020`) + `NaOH (Alkali)` (`#2EC4B6`) --> `RCOONa (Soap) + Glycerol` (`#27AE60`)

Key points:
- Requires elevated temperature (> 60 C / 140 F)
- Only works on fats containing fatty acid esters
- Products are fully water-soluble
- Higher NaOH = faster saponification
- This is why temperature matters so much

**Right -- Emulsification** (teal-accented glass card, H ~9.5"):
- Title: `EMULSIFICATION` (Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 24 pt `#2EC4B6`)
- Subtitle: `For Mineral Oils & Synthetic Lubricants (Non-Polar)`

Micelle diagram -- central circle (oil droplet, amber-tinted) with radiating surfactant molecules:
- Inside: `OIL DROPLET`
- Outward: `Hydrophilic head (water-loving)` (`#2EC4B6`)
- Inward: `Hydrophobic tail (oil-loving)` (`#E8A020`)
- Label: `MICELLE`

Key points:
- Surfactant molecules trap oil in micelles
- Micelles suspend oil in water phase
- CMC (critical micelle concentration): 0.1-1 g/L
- Above CMC, more surfactant does NOT help
- Temperature must stay BELOW cloud point

**Bottom callout** (emerald glass pill):
`Saponifiable soils are the easy ones -- just add heat and caustic. Non-saponifiable soils need surfactant. If you have both, you need both mechanisms working together.`

## Phase 6 -- Surfactant Science

Y: 22.0" to 27.5". Section label: `SURFACTANT SCIENCE -- THE 60-SECOND VERSION`.

Two glass cards side by side.

**Left -- CMC** (teal-accented):
- Title: `CRITICAL MICELLE CONCENTRATION (CMC)`
- The minimum surfactant concentration needed to form micelles and clean effectively
- Typical CMC: 0.1-1 g/L for nonionic surfactants
- Below CMC: poor cleaning -- surfactant not forming micelles
- At CMC: cleaning efficiency activates
- Above CMC: cleaning efficiency plateaus -- more does not help
- Highlight: `Adding more surfactant above CMC wastes money and can cause foam problems` (`#E8A020`)

**Right -- Cloud Point** (amber-accented):
- Title: `CLOUD POINT`
- The temperature at which nonionic surfactant becomes insoluble and solution turns cloudy
- Typical cloud point: 140-175 F (60-80 C)
- ABOVE cloud point: emulsion breaks down, oil re-deposits, cleaning efficiency collapses
- `Match your surfactant cloud point to your operating temperature -- or you will clean parts and then un-clean them.`

## Phase 7 -- Common Failures

Y: 27.5" to 32.5". Section label: `WHAT GOES WRONG -- 5 COMMON FAILURES`.

Glass table rows (alternating fills):

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Water break (oil film after rinse) | Insufficient time/temp; bath exhausted; surfactant depleted | Increase time/temp; rebuild bath; add surfactant |
| White silicate residue on aluminum | Silicate too high, temp too high, or rinse inadequate | Reduce silicate; lower temp; improve rinse; add acid desmut |
| Etching of aluminum | NaOH too high for substrate; temperature too high | Reduce NaOH; increase silicate inhibitor; lower temperature |
| Etching of zinc die cast | Excessive immersion time in strong caustic | Shorten time to < 3 min; reduce NaOH |
| Foam overflow | Surfactant overdose; drag-in of incompatible chemistry | Check surfactant concentration; use low-foam grade for spray |
