## Phase 6 -- Flame vs. Induction Comparison

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section label: `FLAME VS. INDUCTION -- WHICH SURFACE HARDENING?`.

**Left -- Flame Hardening** (accent `#E8A020`):
Heat source: oxy-fuel flame | Precision: moderate (operator dependent) | Case depth: 0.050--0.250 in | Tolerance: +/- 0.030 in | Heating rate: 50--200 F/sec | Cycle time: minutes (progressive) | Best for: large parts, low volume, field repairs, irregular shapes | Capital: low | Operator dependency: HIGH.
Highlight: `No custom coil needed -- adapts to any geometry with standard torch equipment`.

**Right -- Induction Hardening** (accent `#2EC4B6`):
Heat source: electromagnetic induction | Precision: excellent (coil defines pattern) | Case depth: 0.020--0.300 in | Tolerance: +/- 0.005--0.010 in | Heating rate: 100--1000 F/sec | Cycle time: seconds | Best for: high-volume, precision, small-to-medium parts | Capital: high | Operator dependency: LOW.
Highlight: `Fastest surface hardening method -- seconds per part in production`.

## Phase 7 -- Troubleshooting Quick Hits + Footer

**Troubleshooting** (Y: 28.5" to 32.5"). Section label: `QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING -- 4 COMMON PROBLEMS`. Four cards, left accent `#E05C5C`:

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| OVERHEATING / MELTING | Flame too close; dwell too long; wrong tip | Increase distance; increase traverse speed; select proper tip |
| SOFT SPOTS | Insufficient temp; uneven flame coverage | Verify temperature (pyrometer); improve flame head design |
| CRACKING | Quench too severe; section too thin | Reduce quench; preheat; stress relieve before hardening |
| NON-UNIFORM CASE | Manual technique variation | Automate traverse; use CNC control; verify with test coupons |
