# Poster #503 -- Construction Workup
## Masking & Fixturing -- Flame Spray

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Masking and fixturing for flame spray. The good news: flame spray has lower particle velocity than plasma or HVOF, so overspray penetration is less aggressive and masking can be simpler. Manual operation is common -- many applications are hand-held gun work without robotic fixturing.

**BLOCK B -- Materials Matrix**

Y: 5.0" to 12.0". Full-width table.

| Material | Max Temp | Reusable | Best For | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hi-temp masking tape (silicone adhesive) | 260 degC (500 degF) | No | Edges, transitions, irregular shapes | Adhesive residue if overheated |

### ZONE 5 -- Do's and Don'ts

**Section label:** `MASKING BEST PRACTICES` -- Y: 22.2".

**Left -- DO (X: 0.5", W: 11.0", accent `#27AE60`):**
- `Mask AFTER grit blasting -- tape on blasted profile adheres better`
- `Use metal masks for production -- reusable and precise`
- `Protect all threads, bearing surfaces, and mating faces`
- `Verify mask integrity between spray passes`
- `Keep 3--5 mm gap between mask edge and desired coating edge for blending`

**Right -- DON'T (X: 12.0", W: 11.5", accent `#E05C5C`):**
- `Never mask BEFORE cleaning -- traps contamination underneath`
- `Never use plastic tape not rated for temperature -- melts and contaminates`
- `Never leave gaps in masking -- overspray reaches further than you think`
- `Never reuse single-use tape -- adhesive degrades with heat cycles`
- `Never block cooling air paths with masking material`

Each item: Inter Regular 14 pt, bullet indicator in accent color.
