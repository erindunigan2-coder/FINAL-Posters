# Poster #149 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Tin-Lead -- Post-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 8. The rinse after tin-lead plating and before post-treatment. This rinse shares the speed urgency of the pure tin post-plate rinse -- the solder surface oxidizes rapidly in air with residual acid. But the tin-lead post-plate rinse has an additional, non-negotiable requirement that pure tin does not: **lead-bearing rinse water must be segregated.** Lead wastewater discharge is regulated under 40 CFR 433 (0.43 mg/L daily max), and lead-bearing sludge may be classified as F006 hazardous waste. This poster covers speed, drag-out recovery (tin-lead chemistry is expensive), and the mandatory lead waste segregation.

Y: 15.3" to 20.0". Full-width rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`.

**Three-column layout:**

| Column | Header Color | Header | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | `#E8A020` | `THE COST` | Tin-lead chemistry is expensive -- metal salts of both Sn and Pb, plus MSA. Every rack carries 0.1--0.5 mL/sq ft of plating solution. Lead compounds are especially costly to dispose of as hazardous waste. |
| Center | `#27AE60` | `THE RECOVERY` | First rinse stage (still or low-flow) captures concentrated drag-out. This is lead-bearing solution -- it can be returned to the plating bath if clean, reducing both chemistry cost and hazardous waste volume. |

## Design Notes

This is the most regulation-heavy rinse poster in the entire series. The lead waste segregation panel (Zone 5) has no equivalent in any other cluster -- it exists because tin-lead plating generates regulated hazardous waste at every rinse stage. The drag-out recovery section serves double duty: cost savings AND hazardous waste volume reduction. The effluent card in Zone 6 (Card 4) breaks the teal accent pattern with coral -- because that metric is regulatory, not operational. That visual break is deliberate: it signals "this one has consequences."
