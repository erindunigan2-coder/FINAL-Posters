# Poster #160 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- Zinc Phosphate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 1 of 8. Zinc phosphate cleaning is more aggressive than iron phosphate cleaning -- higher pH, higher temperature, longer time -- because the process includes a conditioning step that "resets" the surface. But the demands are also higher: zinc phosphate is LESS forgiving of residual soils than iron phosphate. Heavy stamping oils, waxes, and rust preventatives MUST be completely removed. Two-stage cleaning (pre-clean + clean) is common on automotive lines.

The silicate warning from iron phosphate applies here with even greater force -- silicate residues poison the zinc phosphate bath.

| Defect | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skip areas on phosphate | Residual oil, wax | Increase conc/temp/time; add pre-clean |
| Phosphate powdery | Over-etched surface | Reduce alkalinity; add etch inhibitor |
| Uneven phosphate crystals | Mixed soils, variable cleaning | Improve loading; pre-clean heavily soiled parts |
| Bath contamination downstream | Silicate carry-over | Switch to silicate-free cleaner immediately |

## Design Notes

The key difference from Poster #152 (Cleaning -- Iron Phosphate) is the higher stakes. Zinc phosphate is less forgiving. The poster must communicate urgency -- the bottom callout in the hero zone ("less forgiving than iron phosphate") sets the tone. The two-stage cleaning concept is unique to zinc phosphate (at this level of complexity) and gives automotive operators a concrete process improvement path.
