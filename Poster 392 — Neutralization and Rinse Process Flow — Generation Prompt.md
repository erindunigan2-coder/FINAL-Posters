## Phase 4 -- Rinse Architecture Comparison

Y: 16.3" to 21.8".

| Type | Description | Water Efficiency | Rinse Quality | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single running | One tank, continuous overflow | POOR -- highest consumption | Adequate | Non-critical |
| Double running | Two tanks in series | Better -- ~50% reduction | Good | General plating |
| Counterflow | 2--3 tanks; fresh water enters last | EXCELLENT -- 90--95% reduction | Excellent | Standard of care |
| Spray rinse | Low-volume spray above tanks | Best efficiency | Good for drag-out | Combined w/ immersion |
| Drag-out (still) | No running water; recovers chemistry | Saves chemicals, not water | Recovery, not cleanliness | Chemical recovery |

## Phase 5 -- Rinse Ratio Math + Key Principles

Y: 22.0" to 28.5".

| Principle | Accent |
|---|---|
| Counterflow is ALWAYS better than parallel single rinses | `#27AE60` |
| Reduce drag-out FIRST -- slow withdrawal, air knife, drain time 10--15 sec | `#E8A020` |
| Drag-out volume: 50--200 mL per m2 of part surface | `#2EC4B6` |
| Conductivity monitoring is the cheapest quality tool in the shop | `#E8A020` |
| NEVER share drains between cyanide and acid rinse streams | `#E05C5C` |
