## Phase 4 -- Masking Materials + Field vs. Shop (HERO)

Y: 2.9" to 15.5". Section label: `MASKING MATERIALS INVENTORY`.

**Materials Table (Y: 3.8"-11.0"):**

| Material | Temp Rating | Reusable | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-temp masking tape | 150-260 degC | No | Straight edges, flat surfaces | Silicone adhesive; apply firmly |
| Aluminum foil tape | 300 degC+ | No | Irregular shapes, wrapping | Conforms well; tear-off removal |
| Sheet metal shields | 500 degC+ | Yes | Production runs; repeated patterns | Custom-cut per job |
| Silicone plugs/caps | 260 degC+ | Yes | Bolt holes, threads, bores | Snap-in; quick application |
| Cardboard + tape | ~150 degC | No | Field work; non-critical masking | Monitor for charring near flame |
| Ceramic fiber tape | 1000 degC+ | Limited | Heat-intensive zones | Rarely needed for wire spray |

Header fill `#3A4055`. Data: Inter Regular 12pt.

**Field vs. Shop Masking (Y: 11.5"-15.3"):** Two panels.

*Left -- Field Masking* (accent `#E8A020`):
- Tape and foil are primary materials
- Cardboard for large non-spray areas
- Metal shields when pattern repeats
- Secure masking against wind
- Remove after spray, before sealer

*Right -- Shop Masking* (accent `#27AE60`):
- Metal shields for production repeatability
- Silicone plugs/caps for fastener holes
- Tape for one-off and fine edge control
- Fixturing integrates masking shields
- Cleaner environment -- less contamination risk

## Phase 5 -- Fixturing

Y: 15.5" to 22.0". Section label: `FIXTURING -- KEEPING IT SIMPLE`.

**Left -- Shop Fixturing (W: 11.0"):** accent `#27AE60`.

| Fixture | Use Case | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Turntable | Cylindrical parts (shafts, rollers, pipes) | 10-60 RPM; operator sprays while part rotates |
| V-block cradle | Short cylinders, discs | Simple; gravity-held |
| Hook/chain suspension | Complex shapes | Part hangs and rotates freely |
| Bench clamp | Flat plates, small parts | Quick setup; manual repositioning |

Note: `Wire combustion spray is a MANUAL process. The operator holds the gun. Fixturing just holds the part -- not the gun.`

**Right -- Field Fixturing (W: 11.5"):** accent `#E8A020`.
- The structure IS the fixture -- bridges, tanks, platforms
- Scaffolding provides access and consistent standoff
- Man-lifts / cherry pickers for large vertical surfaces
- Operator moves around the part
- Wind shields may be needed
- Lighting critical in enclosed spaces

Note: `In field work, "fixturing" means "how does the operator safely reach the work area with consistent standoff distance?"`

## Phase 6 -- Best Practices + Common Mistakes

### Masking Rules (Y: 22.0"-28.5", left W: 11.0")

Section label: `MASKING RULES & EDGE QUALITY`.

Title: `MASKING APPLICATION RULES` accent `#2EC4B6`.
1. Apply masking AFTER grit blasting, not before
2. Press tape edges firmly -- lifted edges cause feathered overspray
3. Overlap tape strips by minimum 6 mm (1/4")
4. Metal shields overhang spray edge by minimum 25 mm (1")
5. Masking must withstand atomizing air blast (40-80 PSI)
6. Check masking integrity after each spray pass
7. Remove masking while coating is still warm

### Overspray & Edge Control (right W: 11.5")

Title: `OVERSPRAY & EDGE CONTROL` accent `#E8A020`.
- Wire spray produces more overspray than plasma or HVOF
- Overspray dust settles on masked areas -- shake off between passes
- Tape edge produces sharper line than metal shield
- For critical edges: tape the edge line, then shield the bulk area
- Overspray beyond masking is normal -- plan clean-up

### Common Mistakes (Y: 28.5"-32.5")

Section label: `MASKING MISTAKES -- 4 COMMON PROBLEMS`. Four cards, left accent `#E05C5C`.

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| MASKING BLOWS OFF | Tape not pressed firmly; wind | Press edges hard; use metal shields in wind |
| OVERSPRAY UNDER MASK | Gap between mask and surface | Press edges; tape-then-shield combo |
| CHARRED CARDBOARD | Flame too close; not heat-rated | Use metal shields near flame; replace with foil tape |
| COATING PULLS WITH MASK | Removed too late; bonded to edge | Remove while warm; score edge with knife |
