# Poster #715 -- Construction Workup
## Cleaning -- Protective Coatings

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Poster Designer*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 2 of 8. Cleaning for protective coatings covers two substrate types: steel (SSPC-SP1 + salt removal + post-blast blow-down) and concrete (alkaline degrease + power wash + optional acid etch). The hero is a two-track cleaning sequence showing steel and concrete paths side by side. The salt removal step is critical for marine and coastal environments -- invisible chloride contamination is the #1 cause of premature failure on offshore steel.

### ZONE 4 -- Salt Removal Deep Dive

**Section label:** `SALT REMOVAL -- CRITICAL FOR MARINE AND COASTAL STEEL` -- Y: 14.7".

**Full-width panel (Y: 15.3" to 20.8"):**

**Left -- The Process (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C`
- Title: `SALT REMOVAL PROCEDURE` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E05C5C`

Steps (JetBrains Mono 13 pt `#F0EDE8`, numbered):
1. `Before blasting: pressurized fresh water rinse (3,000--5,000 psi)`
2. `Allow surface to dry completely`
3. `Abrasive blast to SSPC-SP10 or SP5`
4. `After blast: test with Bresle patch (ISO 8502-6)`
5. `Verify: Cl- < 3 ug/cm2, SO4 2- < 10 ug/cm2`
6. `If limits exceeded: re-wash, re-blast, re-test`

**Right -- Why It Matters (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
- Rounded rect, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`
- Title: `THE OSMOTIC BLISTERING MECHANISM` Barlow SemiBold 18 pt `#E8A020`

Content (Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 160%):
- `Soluble salts (chloride, sulfate) trapped under the coating are hygroscopic`
- `They attract moisture through the coating film via osmosis`
- `Water accumulates at the salt deposit, creating a blister`
- `The blister grows until the coating lifts and corrosion begins underneath`
- `This process is invisible until the blister is large enough to see or feel`

Warning: `Coastal and offshore environments: ALWAYS test for soluble salts. "It looks clean" is not a test result.` Inter Medium 14 pt `#E05C5C`.

### ZONE 5 -- Concrete Acid Etch + Oil-Free Air

**Two-column layout (Y: 21.2" to 26.8"):**

**Left -- Acid Etch Procedure (Concrete) (X: 0.5", W: 11.0"):**

Section label: `ACID ETCH -- LAST RESORT FOR CONCRETE` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#E8A020`.

Callout, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E8A020`:
- `When to use: mechanical prep (shot blast, grinder) is not available`
- `Solution: 10--15% muriatic (hydrochloric) acid in water`
- `Apply to dry concrete surface`
- `Watch for vigorous fizzing (CO2 release) -- indicates the acid is etching`
- `If no fizzing: surface has been sealed or contaminated -- acid etch will not work`
- `Rinse THOROUGHLY with clean water`
- `Neutralize with baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) solution`
- `Allow 24+ hours drying before coating`
- Inter Regular 13 pt `#F0EDE8`

Warning: `Acid etch is a FALLBACK method. Shot blasting produces a more consistent profile and better adhesion. Use acid etch only when mechanical prep is truly unavailable.` Inter Medium 13 pt `#E05C5C`.

**Right -- Oil-Free Air Verification (Steel) (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**

Section label: `OIL-FREE AIR -- ASTM D4285` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#2EC4B6`.

Callout, fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#2EC4B6`:
- Title: `BLOTTER TEST` Barlow SemiBold 16 pt `#2EC4B6`
- Steps:

  2. `Direct air stream onto paper for 60 seconds`

Section label: `CLEANING MISTAKES` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 22 pt `#E05C5C`.

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Skip salt wash on coastal steel | Osmotic blistering within months |
| Blast without solvent cleaning first | Oil driven into profile |

| Power wash concrete without drying time | Trapped moisture blisters coating |

## Design Notes

This poster mirrors the cleaning poster from Cluster 7 (#706) but adds the concrete cleaning path. The salt removal deep-dive earns its own zone because osmotic blistering is the single most expensive failure mode in marine and offshore protective coatings -- and it starts at the cleaning stage. The acid etch panel is deliberately labeled "last resort" because shot blasting is always superior for concrete; acid etch is a field expedient.
