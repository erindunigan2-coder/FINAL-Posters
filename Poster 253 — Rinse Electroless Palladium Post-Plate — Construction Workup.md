# Poster #253 -- Construction Workup
## Rinse -- Electroless Palladium -- Post-Plate

*Alaina -- Plating Posters Inc Creative Lead*
*v1.0 -- 2026-04-26*

Stage 6 of 8. This rinse stops the electroless reaction, removes Pd bath drag-out, and prepares the surface for post-treatment. In ENEPIG, parts proceed immediately to the immersion gold bath after this rinse -- speed matters. The Pd surface is more oxidation-resistant than EN but still benefits from quick transfer.

Hero visual: rinse system with ENEPIG process flow continuation indicator.

### ZONE 4 -- Post-Plate Handling Rules

**Section label:** `POST-PLATE HANDLING -- DO AND DON'T` -- Y: 14.7".

**Two-column layout (Y: 15.3" to 20.3"):**

- Title: `DO` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 24 pt `#27AE60`
- Content (Inter Regular 14 pt `#F0EDE8`, line height 160%):
  - `Rinse immediately after removal from Pd bath`
  - `Use DI water -- municipal water can stain`
  - `Keep parts wet during transfer to next step`
  - `Use cold rinse to stop reaction quickly`
  - `Proceed to immersion gold promptly (ENEPIG)`

**Right -- DON'T (X: 12.0", W: 11.5"):**
- Rounded rect H: 4.8", fill `#1E2435`, left accent `#E05C5C` 0.06"
- Title: `DON'T` Barlow Condensed ExtraBold 24 pt `#E05C5C`
- Content:
  - `Allow parts to air-dry with Pd bath residue`
  - `Touch freshly plated Pd surfaces (fingerprints)`
  - `Stack parts wet -- galvanic attack at contact points`
  - `Delay transfer to Au bath in ENEPIG (surface oxidizes)`
  - `Skip the rinse -- Pd drag-out contaminates Au bath`

- `IPC-4556: no heat treatment between Pd and Au in ENEPIG stack`

## Design Notes

The ENEPIG transition callout (Zone 5) is the unique element on this poster. The three-step horizontal flow inside the callout visually connects the Pd bath exit to the Au bath entry, reinforcing that this rinse is a brief waypoint, not a resting point. The Pd recovery note acknowledges the economics -- palladium is expensive and drag-out recovery matters. The DO/DON'T format in Zone 4 is directly actionable for the operator.
