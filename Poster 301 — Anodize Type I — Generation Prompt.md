## Phase 5 -- Contamination Thresholds + Self-Healing

Y: 14.5" to 20.5".

**Left -- Bath Chemistry Limits:**

| Parameter | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CrO3 | 30--50 g/L | Analyze weekly |
| Dissolved Al | < 10 g/L | Precipitates at higher levels |
| Sulfate (SO4) | < 0.5 g/L | Causes blistering |
| Chloride (Cl-) | < 25 ppm | Causes pitting |
| Trivalent Cr (Cr3+) | < 3 g/L | Reduces bath efficiency |

**Right -- Self-Healing Property:**
`Type I's unique advantage: residual Cr(VI) trapped in the sealed oxide acts as a built-in corrosion inhibitor. When the oxide is scratched, Cr(VI) leaches out and passivates the exposed aluminum. No other anodize type has this property. This is the single hardest feature to replicate in Cr(VI)-free alternatives.` `#E8A020`

## Phase 6 -- Cr(VI) Safety Panel

Y: 20.5" to 26.5". Section: `HEXAVALENT CHROMIUM SAFETY -- NON-NEGOTIABLE`.

Full-width coral-tinted section:
- `CrO3 is an IARC Group 1 carcinogen (lung, nasal cavity)`
- `OSHA PEL: 5 ug/m3 (8-hr TWA) -- the lowest PEL of any common plating chemical`
- `Engineering controls: enclosed tank with local exhaust ventilation; fume suppressant`
- `PPE: Chemical splash goggles, face shield, rubber apron, rubber gloves, respiratory protection`
- `Medical surveillance: baseline + annual pulmonary function, nasal exam`
- `Waste: Cr(VI) is a RCRA hazardous waste -- reduce to Cr(III) before disposal`
- `Spill: Treat as hazmat; Na2S2O5 reduction`
