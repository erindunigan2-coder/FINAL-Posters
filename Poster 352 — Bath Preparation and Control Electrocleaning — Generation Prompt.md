## Phase 4 -- Bath Composition Hero

Y: 4.2" to 15.0". Section label: `WHAT IS IN YOUR ELECTROCLEANER -- 5 COMPONENTS`.

Five glass cards (3 top row, 2 bottom centered):

| Component | Accent | Concentration | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) | `#2EC4B6` | 22-60 g/L (3-8 oz/gal) | Primary alkalinity; solution conductivity; saponification |
| Sodium Carbonate (Na2CO3) | `#E8A020` | 15-45 g/L (2-6 oz/gal) | Alkalinity buffer; maintains consistent pH |
| Trisodium Phosphate (TSP) | `#27AE60` | 7.5-30 g/L (1-4 oz/gal) | Emulsifier; soil dispersant; water softener |

| Chelating Agent (Gluconate/EDTA) | `#E8A020` | 0-7.5 g/L (0-1 oz/gal) | Complexes dissolved metals to prevent cathodic smut |

Bottom callout (teal glass pill): `Electrocleaner formulations are deliberately simpler than soak cleaners. The gas evolution and electrical action do the mechanical work -- the chemistry provides the environment.`

## Phase 5 -- Makeup Procedure

Y: 15.0" to 21.0". Section label: `TANK MAKEUP -- STEP BY STEP`.

Six numbered glass step rows:

| Step | Instruction | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill tank to 2/3 volume with warm water (40-50 C) | Use warm water to aid dissolution |
| 2 | Add NaOH slowly with agitation | EXOTHERMIC (`#E05C5C`) |
| 3 | Add carbonate and phosphate builders; dissolve completely | Stir thoroughly between additions |
| 4 | Add surfactant LAST -- low-foam grade required | Gas evolution creates aggressive agitation; high-foam = overflow |
| 5 | Bring to operating volume and temperature (150-160 F target) | Connect heater; verify thermostat |
| 6 | Set up rectifier: verify polarity, set voltage (6-12 V), confirm bus bars | TEST with dummy load before first production (`#E05C5C`) |

## Phase 6 -- Analytical Control

Y: 21.0" to 27.0". Section label: `KEEPING IT RIGHT -- ANALYTICAL CONTROL`.

Two glass cards.

**Left -- Titration** (teal-accented):
| Test | Method | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Free Alkalinity | 10 mL sample with N/10 HCl to phenolphthalein | 5-10 points |
| Total Alkalinity | Titrate to methyl orange endpoint | 8-15 points |

Frequency: `Daily on production lines; 2-3x per week on intermittent use`

**Right -- Chloride Contamination** (coral-tinted glass, coral accent):
- Title: `CHLORIDE CONTAMINATION -- CRITICAL`
- `Chlorides cause substrate corrosion during electrocleaning that leads to salt spray failure after plating`
- Method: `Mohr titration (AgNO3 / K2CrO4)`
- Action level: `> 10 g/L = PROBLEM`
- Source: `HCl drag-in from acid pickle, contaminated water, chlorinated solvents`
- Fix: `Partial dump and rebuild; investigate drag-in source`
- `> 10 g/L Cl- = DUMP TRIGGER` (JetBrains Mono 14 pt `#E05C5C`)

## Phase 7 -- Bath Life & Dump Criteria

Y: 27.0" to 32.5". Section label: `WHEN TO DUMP -- BATH LIFE INDICATORS`.

Glass table with coral-tinted header:

| Parameter | Action Level | Symptom | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chloride Content | > 10 g/L | Etching, pitting, salt spray failure | Partial dump + rebuild; identify source |
| Dissolved Metals | > 5 g/L total | Cathodic smut; discoloration | Partial dump; increase chelator; switch anodic |
| Alkalinity Drop | Cannot maintain free alk > 5 pts | Rapid consumption; poor conductivity | Rebuild; check acid drag-in |
| Surfactant Depletion | Parts still oily after normal cycle | Water break failure | Add surfactant; if persistent, dump |
| Age / Throughput | 6-12 months (production) | General degradation | Scheduled rebuild |
