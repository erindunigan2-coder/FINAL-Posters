## Phase 5 -- Double Temper Guidance

Y: 14.5" to 22.0". Section label: `WHEN TO DOUBLE (OR TRIPLE) TEMPER`.

**Left -- Double Temper**: When: tool steels, HSS, > 5% retained austenite, critical aerospace/bearing, AMS 2759. Why: first temper conditions RA; second temper tempers fresh martensite from RA conversion.

**Right -- Retained Austenite**: What: untransformed austenite trapped in microstructure. Why it matters: softer, dimensionally unstable, transforms under load. How to reduce: double/triple temper, sub-zero treatment (-100 to -320 F). Typical: 52100 as-quenched 6--12%, M2 20--30%; after double temper < 5%.

## Phase 6 -- Temper Comparison + Footer

Y: 22.0" to 28.5". Section label: `WHY MARTEMPERING NEEDS TEMPER AND AUSTEMPERING DOESN'T`.

**Martempering** (Emerald): martensite (BCT, supersaturated, brittle, stressed) --> temper converts to tempered martensite (carbides precipitate, stress relieved, tough, stable).

**Austempering** (Amber): bainite (ferrite + carbide, already stable, tough as-formed, no BCT strain) --> no temper needed (tempering bainite may even reduce toughness).

**Footer**. Title: `Temper -- Martempering`. Version `v1.0 -- 2026`.
Disclaimer: `Source: General industry knowledge; ASM Handbook Vol. 4; AMS 2759.`
